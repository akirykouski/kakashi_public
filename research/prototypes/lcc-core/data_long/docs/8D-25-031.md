# 8D-25-031 Cracks at fastening holes, Meccanica Chisone

## 1. D0 Symptom and emergency response

### 0.1 Product and Scope

The BR-310 is a roof rail bracket machined at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A., Plant 2 (Chivasso), from extruded profile in EN AW-6082 in temper T6. The profile is delivered as 6 m bars by Meccanica Chisone S.r.l. Each bar is cut to 118 mm lengths and machined on cell MC-3 (two Doosan-class horizontal machining centers with a shared pallet pool). The finished part has two clearance holes of 9.0 mm diameter and one slotted hole of 9.0 x 14.0 mm, all used for the fastening screws to the roof rail. The nominal wall thickness around the holes is 3.2 mm at the thinnest point, and the screw seat takes a tightening torque of 22 Nm.

The defect is a crack that starts at the edge of a fastening hole and runs outward into the wall. Typical crack length on the first parts was 1.5 to 6 mm. Cracks were found on the hole edge facing the extrusion direction, in most cases on the hole nearest to the bracket end.

### 0.2 First Notice

The first notice came on Monday 6 October 2025 at 06:40, during the first-piece check of the early shift on cell MC-3. Operator Giacomo Fenoglio was checking the hole edges of the first bracket after the pallet change and saw a fine line at the edge of hole 2 under the 10x loupe. He stopped the cell, tagged the part and called the shift supervisor, Ilaria Penna, at 06:48.

By 07:05 the supervisor had checked the previous 40 parts of the night shift that were still in the cell washer basket. Three of those 40 parts showed the same line at a hole edge. This changed the event from a single suspect part to a repeat finding, and she called the quality department.

### 0.4 Emergency Actions in the First Hours

The emergency actions had one purpose: to stop more cracked parts from moving forward while the extent was still unknown. They were taken in this order.

1. **Stop of the source (0 to 1 h).** Cell MC-3 was stopped at 06:48. The two other cells that also run BR-310 (MC-1 and MC-5) were stopped at 08:35 after the quality manager's decision. Total production stop time for BR-310 on 6 October was 7 h 10 min until the sorted release of the first safe parts.
2. **Freeze of material (1 to 3 h).** All BR-310 bars, work in progress and finished parts were frozen in the inventory system at 09:05. Warehouse staff placed red hold tags on every pallet in the raw material rack, the cell buffer area and the finished goods bay.
3. **Check of the machine side (1 to 2 h).** The process engineer, Marta Occhipinti, compared the last tool change records, the spindle load trend and the clamping pressure log. Drill 8.6 mm had been changed 11 hours earlier, at 19:50 the day before. The tool was measured at 08:10 and was within tolerance. Clamping pressure had stayed at 55 to 58 bar for all pallets. No machine cause was visible in the first look, but it was not excluded.
4. **Isolation of suspect parts (2 to 4 h).** Parts from the last 24 hours of production were collected from the washer, the buffer and the packing station and put into a marked quarantine cage (cage Q1 in the finished goods hall).
5. **First evidence (3 to 6 h).** Four cracked parts and two good parts from the same pallet were delivered to the laboratory at 10:30. The laboratory cut the holes in cross-section and photographed them at 50x and 200x by 15:30.
6. **Traceability check (2 to 5 h).** Using the bar lot label on the machining router, the team found that the cracked parts came from the extrusion lot CH-2509-14, with a few from lot CH-2509-17. A third lot, CH-2510-02, was in the rack but had not yet been machined.

The following statement was written at the end of the first day and was used to start the team work.

- **Where found:** first-piece check on MC-3, then buffers and finished stock.
- **When:** first seen on 6 October 2025 at 06:40 on parts machined during the night shift of 5 to 6 October.

## 3. D3 Containment details

### 3.1 Containment Objective

The objective of the containment is that no BR-310 bracket with a crack at a fastening hole leaves any Valsangone stock location, and that every part that is released can be recognized as checked. The containment works on the symptom only. It does not depend on the cause, which the team has not yet established.

The containment covers all BR-310 brackets and all bars from the three Chisone lots concerned (CH-2509-14, CH-2509-17 and CH-2510-02), in every location where they are held. The start of containment was 6 October 2025 at 09:05, with the inventory freeze.

### 3.4 Sorting Method

**3.4.1 Visual check.** Each hole edge, on both the inlet and the exit side, is examined at 10x magnification under a lamp with 1,000 lux at the part. The inspector checks three holes per part, six edges in total. Any line, however short, that starts at the hole edge is treated as a crack indication.

**3.4.2 Eddy current check.** Each part that passes the visual check is passed over an eddy current probe set up on a reference part with an artificial notch of 0.5 mm length. The probe is verified at the start of each shift and after every 250 parts, using the reference part. Any reading above the alarm threshold sends the part to the second check.

**3.4.3 Second check.** Parts with an indication from either method are examined a second time by a different inspector. If the second inspector confirms the indication, the part is scrapped. If not confirmed, the part goes to the laboratory for a decision. In the first seven days, 11 parts were sent to the laboratory this way, and 4 of them were found to have no crack.

**3.4.4 Inspector qualification.** Each inspector took a 45-minute briefing and a test with 20 parts, of which 6 had known cracks. An inspector had to find all 6 to be allowed to work. Of 22 persons tested, 19 passed at the first attempt and 3 passed after a second briefing.

**3.4.5 Rate and shift pattern.** The measured rate was 95 parts per hour per inspector for visual plus eddy current checks. Sorting ran on two shifts, 06:00 to 14:00 and 14:00 to 22:00, from Tuesday 7 October.

### 3.7 Clean-Point Marking

The clean point is the first place in the chain from which a part is known to be free of the symptom. Parts that pass the sorting are marked so that they cannot be confused with unchecked parts.

**3.7.1 Part marking.** Each checked and good part receives a green paint dot of 4 mm diameter on the rear face, away from any functional surface. The dot is applied by a marking pen at the end of the check station. Parts checked on a later date use a different mark: a second dot in blue is applied beside the green one for parts checked from the second week.

**3.7.2 Container marking.** Every container of good parts carries a green label with the text "BR-310 SORTED", the date, the inspector's initials, the location code and the lot number. Cracked parts go into a red container labeled "SCRAP – CRACK". Parts waiting for the laboratory decision go into a yellow container.

**3.7.3 Production marking.** Parts machined after the restart carry a clean-point mark of a different type, a laser-etched two-letter code and a date code on the rear face, applied after the final check on every part. The code lets the team separate the parts made after the restart from sorted parts.

## 4. Measurement results

## 1. Measurement Results

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

This section records the measurements taken at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A. between 14 October and 14 November 2025 on BR-310 roof rail brackets and on the EN AW-6082 T6 extrusions from which they are machined. The extrusions are supplied by Meccanica Chisone S.r.l. The defect under investigation is a short crack that starts at the edge of one of the four fastening holes (nominal diameter 8.50 mm, tolerance +0.09/0 mm) and runs outward in the plane of the flange, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mm long. The aim of the measurements was to find out which measurable characteristics differ between lots with a high crack rate and lots with a low or zero crack rate. Six extrusion lots were included, covering all lots machined since the first reported crack.

Each bar is 6.0 m long and is sawn into billets that yield 40 brackets per bar. Machining is done on two horizontal machining centres, MC-1 and MC-2, with the same tool set and program. The hole sequence is centre drill, twist drill to 8.30 mm, then a reamer to 8.50 mm. Brackets from every lot were run on both machines.

### 1.2 Gauges Used and Measurement Capability

All gauges were within their calibration interval when used. A gauge repeatability and reproducibility study was run on the hole-diameter and roughness measurements before the lot comparison, using 10 parts, 3 operators and 3 repeats.

| Characteristic | Gauge | Resolution | Gauge R&R (% of tolerance) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hole diameter | Air gauge with 8.50 mm setting ring pair | 0.001 mm | 9.4 |
| Hole position | Coordinate measuring machine (CMM-2) | 0.002 mm | 6.1 |
| Surface roughness Ra in hole | Stylus profilometer, 0.8 mm cut-off | 0.01 µm | 12.8 |
| Burr height at hole exit | Optical measuring microscope, 30x | 0.005 mm | 14.6 |
| Hardness | Brinell tester, 2.5 mm ball, 62.5 kgf | 1 HBW | 7.9 |
| Electrical conductivity | Eddy-current conductivity meter, 60 kHz | 0.1 MS/m | 4.2 |
| Flange thickness | Digital micrometer | 0.001 mm | 5.5 |
| Crack detection | Fluorescent penetrant line plus 10x visual check | not applicable | see 1.7 |

### 1.7 Detection Capability and Observations

A blind test with 40 brackets (14 with known cracks, 26 without) was run to check the penetrant line. Three inspectors each classified all 40 parts. Detection of cracks longer than 1.5 mm was 100 %, of cracks between 0.8 and 1.5 mm was 79 %, and there were 2 false calls out of 78 classifications on good parts. The 10x visual check in the machining cell detected only 57 % of the cracks longer than 1.5 mm, so the crack rates in 1.3 describe penetrant results and not cell inspection results.

The main observations from the measurements are these:

1. Crack rate varies by a factor of more than 20 between lots, while hole diameter, position and surface roughness do not.
2. Hardness, conductivity, Mn content and elongation separate the lots in the same order as the crack rate.
3. Machine MC-1 shows about twice the crack count of MC-2 across all lots, but the difference narrows to a ratio of 1.3 in the worst lots.
4. Cracks occur preferentially at the holes nearest the extrusion surface.

## 5. D4 Ishikawa analysis

## 2. D4 Ishikawa Analysis

### 2.1 Approach

The cross-functional team met on 3 November 2025 and again on 10 November 2025. The team consisted of the quality engineer (chair), a machining process engineer, a toolroom supervisor, a metrology technician, a production shift leader, a purchasing representative and, by video link, a materials engineer from Meccanica Chisone S.r.l. The effect analysed was: "Cracks of 1.5 to 4.5 mm at the edge of fastening holes on BR-310 brackets, detected after machining, at a sampled rate of 3.9 % overall and up to 11.0 % in single lots."

Each of the six classic branches was brainstormed first without reference to data. Every candidate cause was then screened against the measurements in the previous section and given one of three ratings: unlikely (data contradicts it), possible (data neither supports nor contradicts it), or likely (data supports it). The ratings below are working judgements at the time of writing and are tied to the evidence cited.

### 2.2 Man

The team considered operator influence on loading, clamping and tool change. Three points were examined.

First, clamping force. Brackets are held by two hydraulic clamps. Operators set the hydraulic pressure at the start of each shift, with a nominal value of 55 bar. Spot readings across six shifts showed 52 to 61 bar. Over-clamping could add bending stress near the holes, but the crack position (holes 3 and 4) does not match the clamp positions, which are close to holes 1 and 2.

Second, tool change practice. Operators change the reamer on a count of 600 parts, with a tolerance that in practice allows changes between 560 and 720 parts. Records for L-4519 and L-4520 show the same range as for other lots.

Third, shift distribution. Of the 47 cracked parts, 18 came from the first shift, 16 from the second and 13 from the third, close to the share of production per shift (38 %, 34 % and 28 %). No shift effect was found.

Rating: unlikely as the primary cause. Clamping stays on the list as a possible minor contributor.

### 2.3 Machine

The two machining centres have different spindle ages: MC-1 has 21,400 spindle hours and MC-2 has 9,700. Machine-related candidates were spindle runout, coolant delivery through the tool, and axis behaviour during peck drilling.

Spindle runout measured at the tool tip was 6 µm on MC-1 and 3 µm on MC-2 with a test bar. A runout of 6 µm is within the machine limit of 8 µm but could give uneven reamer loading. This fits the observation that MC-1 shows more cracks than MC-2 in good lots. The effect does not explain why both machines produce high crack rates on L-4519 and L-4520.

Through-tool coolant pressure was 24 bar on MC-1 and 31 bar on MC-2, against a set point of 30 bar. The lower pressure on MC-1 was traced to a partly blocked filter cartridge. Lower coolant pressure may raise cutting temperature at the hole wall, and this is a second reason that MC-1 might be less favourable than MC-2.

Rating: possible, as a contributor that raises crack incidence on MC-1. Not likely as the cause of the large lot-to-lot differences.

### 2.4 Method

The machining method was reviewed step by step. The twist drill step removes 8.30 mm diameter in a single peck cycle at 4,200 rpm and a feed of 0.22 mm per revolution. The reamer takes 0.20 mm on diameter at 1,800 rpm and a feed of 0.35 mm per revolution. The program has not been changed since the start of series production 14 months ago.

Two method weaknesses were identified. The hole is not deburred as a separate operation: the exit burr is removed by a nylon brush in a later station, which leaves a small edge radius of 0.05 to 0.10 mm, a possible local stress raiser. Also, the drilling cycle uses a full-depth peck with no chip break on the last 3 mm, which gave long chips and occasional chip wrapping on the drill (reported by operators on about 1 part in 300).

The sawing and billet preparation method was also noted. Billets are cut with a coolant-flooded saw and stacked on a pallet; no marking of the extrusion surface orientation is applied, so the position of the hole relative to the original outer surface is not controlled. This is consistent with the preferential cracking at holes 3 and 4 but needs confirmation.

Rating: possible. Burr condition and surface orientation are likely contributors only if the material is sensitive to them, since the method was identical for all lots.

### 2.5 Material

This branch received the most attention because of the lot-to-lot pattern. Candidate causes were composition, heat treatment condition, grain structure and extrusion surface condition.

Composition: Mn in L-4519 and L-4520 is 0.46 to 0.47 wt % against 0.62 to 0.63 wt % in the best lots. A lower Mn level reduces the dispersoids that restrain recrystallisation during extrusion and solution treatment, which can lead to a coarse-grained layer near the extrusion surface.

Heat treatment condition: the hardness of 104 to 105 HBW and conductivity of 27.0 to 27.2 MS/m in the worst lots are consistent with a higher degree of precipitation strengthening, for example from a faster quench or a different ageing time. Elongation of 9.2 to 9.6 % is lower than in other lots. A harder, less ductile condition is less tolerant of local cutting stress.

Surface and grain structure: supplier data show a different press line and a different die for the 6 October deliveries compared with earlier lots, according to the supplier representative. This has not yet been confirmed by documents.

Rating: likely. The material evidence has the strongest correlation with crack rate.

### 2.6 Measurement

The measurement branch asked whether the apparent differences between lots could come from the way cracks are found. The penetrant line detects 100 % of cracks over 1.5 mm and 79 % between 0.8 and 1.5 mm. The cell visual check finds 57 % of cracks over 1.5 mm, so most cracks are found only at the later station. Detection capability is the same for all lots, so it cannot explain the lot difference, but it explains why cracks escaped to the later stage. Gauge R&R for hole diameter (9.4 %) and roughness (12.8 %) are acceptable, so diameter and roughness data can be trusted. The supplier certificate Mn values were about 0.08 wt % higher than the laboratory results for two lots, which means incoming checks based only on certificates would not have flagged the difference.

Rating: unlikely as a cause of cracking; possible as a cause of late detection.

### 2.7 Environment

Candidate environmental factors were shop temperature, coolant concentration and storage of extrusions. Shop temperature during the period was 19 to 27 °C, with the highest readings in October afternoons. Machine thermal growth was compensated in all cases. Coolant concentration was checked weekly by refractometer and stayed between 6.5 % and 8.0 % against a target of 7.0 % ± 1.0 %. Extrusion storage is indoors on racks, with a mean time from delivery to machining of 9 days; L-4519 and L-4520 were machined after 7 and 8 days. No link with crack rate was seen for any of these.

Rating: unlikely.

## 6. D4 Five-why analysis and verification tests

## 3. D4 Five-Why Analysis and Verification Tests

### 3.1 Approach

The Ishikawa ratings in the previous section produced three chains worth following: a material chain, a machining chain, and a detection chain. Each chain was written as a five-why sequence, and each answer was tied to a test or measurement. Where no test has yet been done, the answer is marked as an assumption. The laboratory work was done by the Valsangone metallurgical laboratory, with additional tests at Laboratorio Prove Materiali Orba.

### 3.6 Metallography

Cross sections were cut through hole 3 of six brackets per lot, mounted, polished and etched to show grain structure. The depth of the coarse recrystallised layer, measured from the extrusion outer surface, was recorded at four points per section.

| Lot | Coarse layer depth mean (mm) | Maximum (mm) | Grain size in core (µm) | Grain size in layer (µm) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L-4471 | 0.28 | 0.41 | 55 | 190 |
| L-4472 | 0.22 | 0.35 | 52 | 175 |
| L-4498 | 0.35 | 0.52 | 58 | 210 |
| L-4519 | 1.12 | 1.46 | 60 | 520 |
| L-4520 | 1.36 | 1.71 | 62 | 560 |
| L-4533 | 0.64 | 0.88 | 59 | 340 |

In the worst lots the hole wall of holes 3 and 4 lies within the coarse layer for most of its length, since those holes are 0.9 to 1.5 mm from the outer surface. In L-4471 and L-4472, the layer is thinner than that distance and the hole wall is mostly in fine core grain. Grain size in the core is similar in all lots, so the difference is confined to the surface layer.

### 3.7 Fractography

Eight cracked brackets (five from L-4520, two from L-4519, one from L-4533) were opened by bending after cooling in liquid nitrogen vapour and examined in the scanning electron microscope. In seven of the eight, the crack started at the hole wall within 0.2 mm of the reamed surface and grew along grain boundaries and through large grains, with flat facets of 300 to 600 µm. One part from L-4533 showed a start at a burr fold at the hole exit, with finer grain at the fracture. Fatigue striations were not found, and the crack face had features of overload and some intergranular separation. No inclusions or porosity larger than 20 µm were found at the initiation sites. The dominant mode is therefore cracking in coarse grain under residual or assembly-related stress, not fatigue from service.

### 3.8 Residual Stress and Hole-Making Trial

Residual stress at the hole wall was measured on brackets from L-4472 and L-4520 at three positions per hole.

| Lot | Hole | Mean tangential stress (MPa) | Range (MPa) |
|---|---|---|---|
| L-4472 | Hole 1 | +42 | +30 to +58 |
| L-4472 | Hole 3 | +51 | +38 to +66 |
| L-4520 | Hole 1 | +64 | +45 to +80 |
| L-4520 | Hole 3 | +118 | +96 to +141 |

The higher stress in L-4520 at hole 3 is consistent with material that is harder and has a coarse surface layer. A trial on 120 brackets from L-4520 bars varied coolant pressure (24 or 30 bar) and edge treatment (brush only, or brush plus a 0.15 mm chamfer cut), 30 brackets per condition. Cracks found by penetrant were 7 of 30 (24 bar, brush), 5 of 30 (30 bar, brush), 3 of 30 (24 bar, chamfer) and 2 of 30 (30 bar, chamfer). Both factors help, but the best condition still shows 6.7 % cracking, so machining changes alone do not remove the problem on this material.

### 3.9 Interpretation and Open Questions

The tests support a link between the coarse-grain surface layer, higher hardness and lower elongation in lots L-4519 and L-4520 and the cracking. They do not yet explain the following points:

1. L-4533, with a layer of 0.64 mm and a crack rate of 2.5 %, shows that the layer depth alone does not give a simple threshold.
2. The reason for the lower Mn and the higher hardness in the 6 October lots is not established, since supplier process records are still awaited.
3. The heat treatment re-test for L-4519 and L-4520 has results for one ageing condition only, with 1 crack in 6 brackets, which is too few to conclude.
4. The contribution of assembly torque to the stress at the hole is not measured, since all cracks were found before assembly.

## 7. D5 and D6 Candidate corrective actions

## 1. Scope and basis for selecting actions

### 1.1 Problem recap
Cracks have been found at the edge of the fastening holes on the BR-310 roof rail bracket. The bracket is machined at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A. from EN AW-6082 extruded profile supplied by Meccanica Chisone S.r.l. Each bracket has four fastening holes, two of diameter 8.5 mm and two of diameter 6.6 mm. The cracks start at the hole edge and run 0.6 to 2.4 mm into the web. They are visible at 10x magnification and were first found in the final visual check.

### 1.2 Why this section lists candidates and not a single fix
The root cause has not been confirmed. Metallography on nine samples and fractography on six samples point to three contributing factors that may act alone or together. The first is a coarse recrystallised grain layer at the extrusion surface, seen in 5 of 9 samples. The second is a burr and a work-hardened rim at the hole edge, linked to drill wear above 70% of tool life. The third is residual stress from the clamping of the profile during drilling. D5 therefore lists corrective actions against each hypothesis. The trials in D6 are designed so that each one can show whether its hypothesis holds. No action is treated as the confirmed fix until its trial results are in.

### 1.3 Selection criteria
Each candidate is rated on four criteria:

- Expected effect on crack incidence, based on the lab evidence.
- Cycle time impact. The BR-310 cell has a takt of 41 s and can tolerate at most +4 s.
- One-off cost and lead time.
- Risk of moving the problem elsewhere, for example into thread quality or hole position.

## 2. Options considered

### 2.1 Option table

| ID | Hypothesis addressed | Candidate action | Expected effect | Cycle time | One-off cost (EUR) | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Hole edge condition | Reduce drill tool life limit from 1,800 holes to 1,000 holes | Medium | +0.0 s | 900 (extra tool cost per month) | 1 week |
| A2 | Hole edge condition | Change drill geometry to 135 degree split point with polished flute and 0.15 mm edge radius | Medium to high | +0.0 s | 2,400 | 3 weeks |
| A3 | Hole edge condition | Add a chamfer and deburr step with a spring-loaded combined tool in the same station | High | +3.2 s | 5,800 | 4 weeks |
| A4 | Clamping stress | Lower clamp force from 9 kN to 5 kN and add a third support pad under the web | Medium | +0.0 s | 3,100 | 3 weeks |
| A5 | Clamping stress | Re-sequence operations so the 6.6 mm holes are drilled before the profile is side-clamped | Low to medium | +1.1 s | 1,200 | 2 weeks |

### 2.2 Options set aside
A7 (spot face) exceeds the cycle time limit of +4 s and doubles the tool cost, so it is kept only as a fallback. A5 is kept in the plan but only as a combined trial with A4, since alone it is unlikely to change the stress state enough. A9 does not address any confirmed hypothesis. It is included in the plan as a low-cost hygiene measure and will not be counted as evidence of effectiveness.

## 3. D6 trial plan

### 3.1 Trial design principles
All trials use bars from two supplier lots with different measured grain layer depths. Lot L-A has a layer of 0.10 mm to 0.15 mm. Lot L-B has a layer of 0.30 mm to 0.45 mm. Each trial runs both lots, so that a result can be separated from the material effect. Pieces are checked by 10x visual inspection and by eddy current at the hole edge. A sample of 30 pieces per trial cell is cut for metallography.

### 3.3 Acceptance criteria for a trial
A trial is considered to have shown an effect when all of the following hold:

1. Crack incidence is 0 in the trial quantity, and the upper 95% confidence limit is at or below 0.25%. With 1,200 pieces and zero findings the upper limit is about 0.25%, so this is the reason for the quantity in T1 and T2.
2. Hole diameter capability Cpk is at least 1.67 on the 8.5 mm holes and 1.50 on the 6.6 mm holes.
3. Thread or fastener torque test on 50 pieces shows no change in failure torque beyond 5% of baseline.
4. Cycle time remains at or below 45 s.

### 3.4 What happens to the data
Every crack found in a trial is sectioned and its starting point is recorded against the grain layer depth, the drill tool life consumed, and the clamp station used. This allows the team to test the three hypotheses directly. If cracks appear only on lot L-B, the grain layer hypothesis gains weight. If cracks follow tool life regardless of lot, the hole edge hypothesis does.

## 8. D7 Preventive actions and lessons learned

## 1. Purpose of D7

### 1.1 What D7 covers
D5 and D6 deal with the BR-310 bracket. D7 deals with the question of how to stop the same type of failure appearing on this product again and on other products that share the same material, the same hole-making method, or the same supplier. Because the cause is not yet confirmed, the preventive actions below are written for the three working hypotheses: surface grain layer, hole edge condition and clamping stress. They apply whichever hypothesis the trials confirm.

### 1.2 Principle
Every preventive action is tied to a gap in the existing process controls, not to a general wish for improvement. Each gap is named below with the evidence that it existed.

## 2. FMEA updates planned

The process FMEA for the BR-310 drilling and finishing steps did not list cracking at the hole edge as a failure mode. It listed only "hole out of tolerance" and "burr present". Severity for the new mode is set at 8, because a crack at a fastening hole can reduce joint strength. Occurrence was set to 2 on the old sheet only because no cracks had been reported. Detection relied on a 10x visual check at 1 piece in 20, rated 7.

## 5. Lessons learned

### 5.1 What we learned so far

1. A failure mode that has never occurred is easy to leave out of the FMEA. Cracking at a hole edge was missing, and this left both prevention and detection weak.
2. A sample check of 1 in 20 at 10x finds a 1% defect rate with only about 64% probability across one shift. It is too weak for a safety-relevant hole edge.
3. Certificates from the supplier did not show grain layer depth, because it was not asked for. We ask for what we measure, and we did not measure it.
4. Tool life was set by dimensional wear, not by hole edge quality. These two limits are not the same.
5. Sorting took much longer than planned because the first sorting method, visual only, had a poor detection rate and was repeated with eddy current.

### 5.2 Actions arising from the lessons

| Lesson | Action | Owner | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Missing failure modes | Add a prompt to the FMEA template: "edge cracking, burr, residual stress" for all drilled aluminium parts | Chiara Moretti | 2026-02-27 |
| Weak sample detection | Review all visual sampling plans for hole-edge features using the gauge study method | Paola Zanetti | 2026-03-31 |
| Supplier data gap | Add grain layer depth to the technical agreement with Meccanica Chisone as a reported value per lot | Ernesto Rinaldi | 2026-02-13 |

### 3.5 Meaning of each signature

A signature in this table does not say that a cause has been found, and it does not say that any action is sufficient. The signature has the following limited meanings.

1. For the 8D leader, it means the text and the numbers in the report were checked against the attachments.
2. For the sponsor, it means the investigation was reviewed on the date shown.
3. For team members, it means the person contributed data or took part, and that the data in their area were quoted correctly.
4. For the supplier, it means the supplier's own data were quoted correctly. It does not mean the supplier accepts any explanation.
5. For the external laboratory, it means the figures and descriptions from its reports were quoted correctly.

### 3.6 How the signatures are to be collected

The 8D leader sends the report as a PDF by email to each signatory. Each person signs by hand or with a qualified electronic signature, and returns the page to the 8D leader. The date is written by the signer, not by the 8D leader.
