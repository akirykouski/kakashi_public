# Aurelia supplier quality manual, rev 3 (excerpt)

## 1. Scope, applicability and order of precedence

### 1.1 Purpose of this manual

This Supplier Quality Manual sets out the quality expectations that Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") places on the companies that supply it with parts, materials, assemblies, tooling and services. It describes how Aurelia and its suppliers plan, prove, produce and improve product quality, and it gives suppliers a single reference for the methods and measures Aurelia uses to judge performance.

The manual is written for the people who carry out the work: supplier quality engineers, plant quality managers, launch managers, production supervisors and laboratory leads. It is not a substitute for a supplier's own quality management system. It describes what Aurelia expects that system to deliver.

### 1.2 Organisations to which the manual applies

The manual applies to every legal entity that holds, or is being considered for, a purchase relationship with Aurelia for production or service parts. This includes:

1. Direct suppliers (Tier 1) that deliver to an Aurelia plant, a regional distribution centre or a contract assembly site.
2. Sub-tier suppliers (Tier 2 and below) that supply any Tier 1 supplier with content used in an Aurelia product.
3. Suppliers of tooling, gauges, fixtures and test equipment built specifically for Aurelia programmes.
4. Contract manufacturers, finishing houses and outside processors that perform a step on Aurelia-bound product, even where the supplier of record subcontracts that step.
5. Providers of engineering, sorting, containment and logistics services that affect the quality of delivered product.

A Tier 1 supplier is responsible for making sure its own suppliers understand and meet the parts of this manual that apply to them. Aurelia will not accept the use of a sub-tier supplier as a reason for a gap in performance.

### 1.3 Supplier categories

Not every requirement in this manual applies in the same depth to every supplier. Aurelia assigns each supplier to one of four categories when the first purchase relationship is created. The category is reviewed once a year by the Supplier Quality department.

| Category | Typical content | Annual spend threshold (EUR) | Depth of manual application |
|---|---|---|---|
| A – Safety and regulatory critical | Braking, steering, restraint and high-voltage components | Any value | Full manual, on-site audits every 12 months |
| B – Function critical | Powertrain, chassis, electronics, thermal systems | Above 2.0 million | Full manual, on-site audits every 24 months |
| C – Standard | Trim, fasteners, brackets, general mouldings | 0.25 to 2.0 million | Core requirements, remote review every 12 months |
| D – Indirect and low risk | Packaging, consumables, non-product services | Below 0.25 million | Basic requirements, questionnaire only |

A supplier may hold different categories for different part families. In that case the stricter category governs the shared elements of the quality system, such as calibration and traceability.

### 1.4 Geographic and legal coverage

The manual applies at all supplier sites worldwide, including sites in countries where Aurelia has no manufacturing presence. Where a supplier operates several plants, each plant that makes, finishes, tests or stores Aurelia-bound product is treated as a separate site for the purposes of audits, performance ratings and capability studies. A parent company's corporate certificate does not replace a site-level review.

Suppliers must also comply with the laws of the countries in which they operate and in which the finished vehicle is sold. Nothing in this manual asks a supplier to break a local law. If a supplier believes a requirement conflicts with a local law, it must tell the Aurelia Supplier Quality engineer in writing so that the matter can be resolved before production begins.

### 1.5 Relationship between the manual and purchase orders

The manual does not create an obligation to buy. Purchase obligations arise only from a purchase order, a long-term supply agreement or a nomination letter issued by Aurelia Purchasing. The manual works alongside those commercial documents as follows:

1. Each purchase order and supply agreement carries a standard clause that incorporates this manual by reference. By accepting the order, the supplier accepts the manual as part of the contract.
2. The manual defines how quality is demonstrated. The purchase order defines what is bought, in what quantity, at what price and by what date.
3. Where a purchase order contains a quality requirement that is more specific than the manual, the purchase order requirement is followed for that part only.
4. Where a purchase order is silent on a quality topic that the manual covers, the manual applies in full.
5. The manual version in force on the date a purchase order is issued is the version that governs that order, unless the order states a later version.

Commercial terms such as price, payment, liability caps and delivery incoterms are outside this manual and are governed only by the commercial documents.

### 1.6 Order of precedence

Occasionally two documents give different answers to the same question. To avoid argument at the moment of a problem, Aurelia uses a fixed order of precedence. A higher-ranked item overrides a lower-ranked item where they conflict.

| Rank | Document type | Example of what it governs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Applicable law and type-approval regulation | Safety, homologation, labour and environmental law |
| 2 | Negotiated supply agreement or purchase order special terms | Part-specific quality targets, agreed volumes |
| 3 | Aurelia engineering drawing, 3D model and material specification | Dimensions, tolerances, material properties |
| 4 | Aurelia customer-specific requirements for the supplier's category | Reporting formats, audit cadence |
| 5 | This Supplier Quality Manual | General quality planning and measurement methods |
| 6 | Supplier's own procedures and work instructions | Day-to-day execution at the supplier site |

If a supplier finds a conflict between two items of equal rank, or cannot tell which item governs, it must ask the Aurelia Supplier Quality engineer for a written ruling and must not decide the question alone. Production may continue on the basis of the written ruling.

### 1.7 Language, units and formats

The controlling language of this manual is English. Translations into Italian, German, Spanish, Portuguese, Polish, Czech, Turkish and Simplified Chinese are provided for convenience; if a translation and the English text differ, the English text is followed. All dimensions use SI units unless the drawing states otherwise. Dates are written in day-month-year order. Capability indices are reported to two decimal places and defect rates in parts per million (ppm) rounded to the nearest whole number.

Electronic data exchange takes place through the Aurelia supplier portal. Suppliers must nominate at least two named portal users per site, one of whom is a quality representative, so that messages are never left unread during absences.

### 1.8 Confidentiality and use of Aurelia information

Drawings, specifications, test results, volume forecasts and audit findings shared under this manual are Aurelia confidential information. Suppliers may share them only with employees and sub-tier suppliers who need them to perform the work and who are bound by equivalent confidentiality duties. Copies must be stored on access-controlled systems. Retention periods for quality records are set out in the glossary and onboarding sections of this manual and in the purchase order; where none is given, quality records are kept for at least 15 years after the end of series production of the vehicle programme.

### 1.9 Responsibilities for interpretation

The Supplier Quality department, based at the Modena headquarters, owns this manual and answers questions about its meaning. Regional supplier quality teams in Europe, the Americas and Asia-Pacific act as the first point of contact. A supplier that disagrees with an interpretation may escalate to the Head of Supplier Quality within 10 working days; a reply is given within a further 15 working days. Escalation does not interrupt delivery obligations while the question is considered.

## 2. Definitions and abbreviations

### 2.1 How to use this glossary

The terms below are used throughout the manual with the meanings given here. Where an industry term has several common meanings, the meaning in this glossary is the one Aurelia applies. Terms are grouped by theme so that suppliers can find related ideas together. Words in the text that are not defined here carry their ordinary dictionary meaning.

### 2.2 Quality planning terms

| Term | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Advanced Product Quality Planning | APQP | A structured method for planning a product launch in five phases: planning, product design, process design, product and process validation, and launch with feedback. It gives suppliers and Aurelia a common timeline and shared deliverables. |
| Production Part Approval Process | PPAP | The package of evidence a supplier submits to show that its process can make parts that meet every drawing and specification requirement at the quoted production rate. The package typically contains dimensional results, material and performance test results, capability studies and process documents. |
| Part Submission Warrant | PSW | The cover document of a PPAP submission. It names the part, the drawing level, the reason for submission, the quantity produced and the production run it represents, and carries the signature of an authorised supplier representative who declares that the evidence is true. |
| Control plan | CP | A written description of the checks and controls used at each process step to keep product within requirements. It lists characteristics, methods, sample sizes, frequencies, measurement tools and the reaction to be taken when a check fails. |
| Failure Mode and Effects Analysis | FMEA | A team-based method that lists the ways a design or process could fail, rates each by severity, occurrence and detection, and records actions to lower the risk. |
| Design FMEA | DFMEA | An FMEA performed on a product design, usually by the design owner. |
| Process FMEA | PFMEA | An FMEA performed on a manufacturing or assembly process, usually by the supplier. |
| Risk Priority Number | RPN | The product of severity, occurrence and detection ratings, each on a scale of 1 to 10, giving a number from 1 to 1,000. Aurelia also reviews high severity ratings on their own, whatever the RPN. |
| Process flow diagram | PFD | A drawing of every step from receiving to shipping, including storage, inspection, rework and transport. |
| Special characteristic | SC | A product feature or process parameter identified by Aurelia as having a strong effect on safety, regulatory conformity, fit or function. Aurelia marks these on the drawing with defined symbols. |
| Key Performance Indicator | KPI | A measure used to track supplier performance, such as delivered ppm or on-time delivery. |

### 2.3 Measurement and statistics terms

| Term | Abbreviation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Measurement System Analysis | MSA | A set of studies that determine how much of the variation seen in measured data comes from the measuring system rather than the product. |
| Gauge repeatability and reproducibility | GR&R | An MSA study that separates the variation caused by one appraiser repeating a measurement (repeatability) from the variation between different appraisers (reproducibility). Aurelia expects at least 3 appraisers, 10 parts and 3 trials for a standard study. |
| Bias | – | The difference between the average of repeated measurements and an accepted reference value. |
| Linearity | – | The change in bias across the working range of a gauge. |
| Stability | – | The change in a gauge's results over time when measuring the same reference. |
| Number of distinct categories | ndc | The number of separate groups of parts the measuring system can reliably tell apart. Aurelia expects a value of 5 or more. |
| Statistical Process Control | SPC | The use of statistical methods, mainly control charts, to monitor a process in real time and to act on signs of instability before defective parts are made. |
| Control chart | – | A time-ordered plot of a measured characteristic with a centre line and upper and lower control limits calculated from process data. |
| Rational subgroup | – | A small set of consecutive parts taken so that variation inside the subgroup is mainly short-term variation. |
| Process capability index | Cp | The width of the tolerance divided by six standard deviations of short-term variation. It ignores how well the process is centred. |
| Process capability index, centred | Cpk | The smaller of the distances from the process average to each tolerance limit, divided by three standard deviations of short-term variation. |
| Process performance index | Ppk | The same calculation as Cpk but using long-term variation. It is the usual index for an initial study. |
| Parts per million | ppm | The number of defective parts per million parts delivered or inspected. |

Aurelia's usual capability targets are shown below. They may be tightened for characteristics of special importance and are stated in the purchase order or on the drawing.

| Use of the index | Characteristic type | Minimum value |
|---|---|---|
| Initial study (Ppk) | Special characteristic | 1.67 |
| Initial study (Ppk) | Other characteristic | 1.33 |
| Ongoing production (Cpk) | Special characteristic | 1.33 |
| Ongoing production (Cpk) | Other characteristic | 1.00 |

### 2.6 Interpretation conventions

The words "must" and "shall" express a requirement. "Should" expresses a recommendation that a supplier may depart from if it can explain why. "May" expresses permission. A "working day" is Monday to Friday excluding public holidays at the Aurelia site concerned. A "launch" is the period from the start of pre-series builds to 90 days after the start of series production.

## 3. Supplier selection and onboarding

### 3.1 Overview of the selection path

Aurelia selects suppliers through a staged path so that quality, delivery and financial risks are understood before business is awarded. The stages are: initial screening, self-assessment, capacity and financial checks, site visit, scoring and nomination, and onboarding. A candidate may be stopped at any stage if the results show a risk that cannot be managed. Typical elapsed time from invitation to nomination is 10 to 14 weeks.

| Stage | Owner | Typical duration | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Initial screening | Purchasing | 1 week | Short list of candidates |
| 2. Self-assessment | Supplier Quality | 2 weeks | Scored questionnaire |
| 3. Capacity and financial checks | Purchasing and Finance | 2 weeks | Capacity and risk ratings |
| 4. Site visit | Supplier Quality | 1 to 2 weeks | Audit report with findings |
| 5. Scoring and nomination | Sourcing committee | 1 week | Nomination decision |
| 6. Onboarding | Supplier Quality | 4 to 6 weeks | Launch-ready supplier |

### 3.2 Initial screening

Purchasing builds a short list from known suppliers, market research and referrals. A candidate enters the short list if it meets all of the following:

1. It has at least 3 years of continuous operation in the relevant product technology.
2. It can show at least two reference customers in the automotive or a comparable regulated industry.
3. It has no unresolved legal or ethics matter that Aurelia's compliance office considers material.
4. It is willing to accept the manual and to allow site access for audits.

Aurelia normally invites between three and five candidates to quote for each major part family.

### 3.3 Supplier self-assessment questionnaire

Each short-listed candidate completes the Supplier Self-Assessment through the portal. The questionnaire has 120 questions in eight groups and is scored out of 100 points. Evidence, such as procedure titles, organisation charts and sample records, must be uploaded for any answer worth 3 or more points.

| Group | Topic | Maximum points |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Management responsibility and organisation | 10 |
| 2 | Product and launch planning | 15 |
| 3 | Process control and capability | 20 |
| 4 | Measurement and calibration | 10 |
| 5 | Purchasing and sub-tier management | 10 |
| 6 | Problem solving and continual improvement | 15 |
| 7 | Logistics, packaging and traceability | 10 |
| 8 | Training and workforce competence | 10 |

A total of 75 points or more allows the candidate to move to the next stage. A score from 60 to 74 allows a single resubmission within 30 days. A score below 60 ends the candidacy for that sourcing round. Scores are checked by an Aurelia SQE, who may reduce points where evidence does not support an answer.

### 3.4 Capacity checks

Aurelia must be confident that a candidate can build the required volume with room for peaks. The capacity check compares the candidate's demonstrated output with Aurelia's peak forecast.

The candidate submits, for each relevant line or cell: the number of machines, the cycle time, planned shifts per week, planned downtime, and the OEE achieved over the previous 12 months. Aurelia calculates the available annual output and compares it with the peak demand, which is taken as 120% of the planned annual volume.

| Rating | Utilisation at peak demand | Meaning for nomination |
|---|---|---|
| Green | Up to 85% | Capacity is sufficient |
| Yellow | Above 85% to 95% | A written capacity plan with dated actions is required |
| Red | Above 95% | Nomination is not possible until capacity is added |

For example, a candidate producing a bracket at 40 seconds per part on one cell, running 2 shifts of 7.5 productive hours for 230 days per year at 80% OEE, can make about 1.24 million parts a year. Against a planned volume of 900,000 parts, peak demand of 1.08 million gives utilisation of 87%, so the rating is yellow and a capacity plan is requested.

The candidate must also identify single points of failure, such as one press or one test rig, and describe how production would continue if that equipment were lost for 10 working days.

### 3.5 Financial checks

Aurelia's Finance team reviews the candidate's financial health because a supplier that fails commercially can stop vehicle production. The review uses the last three annual accounts, a credit report from an external agency and, for Category A and B suppliers, a statement of ownership.

The main indicators are shown below.

| Indicator | Low risk | Medium risk | High risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Equity ratio | Above 30% | 15% to 30% | Below 15% |
| Current ratio | Above 1.4 | 1.0 to 1.4 | Below 1.0 |
| Net debt to operating earnings | Below 2.5 | 2.5 to 4.0 | Above 4.0 |
| Share of revenue from Aurelia, if awarded | Below 20% | 20% to 35% | Above 35% |
| Profit in each of last 3 years | All three | Two of three | One or none |

A candidate with two or more high-risk indicators is referred to the Finance director. Aurelia may ask for a parent guarantee, a staged payment plan or a tooling ownership arrangement to lower the risk. Financial information is kept confidential and used only for supplier selection and monitoring.

### 3.6 Site visit

A site visit is held for every Category A, B and C candidate that passes the earlier stages. For Category D candidates, a visit is optional.

1. **Planning.** The SQE agrees dates with the candidate at least 15 working days ahead and sends an agenda covering the process walk, the quality system review and the laboratory.
2. **Team.** The audit team has two or three members: an SQE, a process or manufacturing engineer and, for Category A, a quality manager from the receiving Aurelia plant.
3. **Duration.** A typical visit takes two days for a single-site candidate. Large or multi-process sites may need up to four days.
4. **Content.** The team follows the product from receiving to dispatch, reviews sample control plans and records, checks calibration status of the gauges in use, inspects storage and handling, and interviews operators and supervisors.
5. **Closing meeting.** The team states its findings at the end of the visit and leaves a written list of findings.

Findings are graded as major, minor or observation. A major finding is a gap that could allow nonconforming product to reach Aurelia. The candidate must send a written plan with owners and dates within 10 working days of the closing meeting. A candidate with more than two major findings does not proceed to nomination in the same sourcing round.

### 3.7 Scoring and nomination

The sourcing committee combines the results into one weighted score. The weights are fixed before the RFQ is released so that all candidates are judged the same way.

| Element | Weight |
|---|---|
| Commercial offer | 35% |
| Self-assessment score | 20% |
| Site visit result | 20% |
| Capacity rating | 15% |
| Financial risk rating | 10% |

The committee may depart from the highest score only when it records its reasons. Nomination is communicated by a nomination letter issued by Purchasing and copied to the SQE.

### 3.8 Onboarding after nomination

Once nominated, the supplier completes onboarding before the first launch activity. The SQE holds a kick-off meeting within 15 working days of the nomination letter. The supplier then:

1. Names a launch quality lead and a deputy, and shares their contact details.
2. Receives portal accounts and training on reporting, delivery scheduling and problem-solving tools; at least 4 supplier staff must attend.
3. Agrees a launch timeline following the APQP phases, with dated milestones.
4. Confirms the packaging concept, labelling format and returnable container flows.
5. Agrees the measurement plan for special characteristics, including which gauges need an MSA study before the first submission.
6. Provides emergency contacts available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

The supplier is reviewed 30, 60 and 90 days after the start of series production by the SQE, using delivered ppm, on-time delivery and the number of open corrective actions as the main measures. A supplier that meets its targets in the three reviews moves to the normal monitoring cycle for its category.

## 4. Advanced product quality planning and launch

4.1 Purpose and scope

This section sets out how suppliers to Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. plan, prove and ramp up a new product or process. It applies to every supplier that delivers series parts, assemblies or materials to the Modena powertrain plant, the Reggio Emilia body plant and the Cervia final assembly line. The aim is simple: by the start of production (SOP) the supplier must be able to make parts that meet every requirement, at the quoted rate, using the process that was planned and proven. The supplier owns the plan. The Aurelia Supplier Quality Engineer (SQE) assigned to the program reviews it at defined gateways.

4.2 Phases and gateways

Aurelia uses six phases separated by five gateways. Timing is counted in weeks before SOP, written as SOP-n. The supplier must align its internal schedule to these dates from the nomination letter onward.

| Phase | Name | Gateway at end | Target timing | Main outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Concept and quotation | G1 | SOP-78 weeks | Feasibility study, cost and capacity assumptions, risk list |
| 1 | Planning | G2 | SOP-66 weeks | Project timing, team charter, preliminary process flow, preliminary special characteristics list |
| 2 | Product design and development | G3 | SOP-48 weeks | Design failure analysis, prototype build results, gauge concepts |
| 3 | Process design and development | G4 | SOP-26 weeks | Process flow, process failure analysis, control plan draft, measurement system plan |
| 4 | Product and process validation | G5 | SOP-8 weeks | Run-at-rate result, capability studies, initial sample inspection results, packaging trial |
| 5 | Launch and ramp-up | Containment exit | SOP+12 to SOP+26 weeks | Launch containment results, lessons learned |

Each gateway has a checklist of exit criteria. The supplier completes a self-assessment seven working days before the gateway meeting and shares it with the SQE. The meeting is chaired by the supplier program manager and attended by the SQE, the Aurelia purchasing lead and, from G3 onward, an Aurelia manufacturing engineer. A gateway is scored with three colours: green means all criteria are met, yellow means one or more criteria are not yet met but a dated recovery plan exists, and red means a criterion that threatens SOP has no credible recovery. Any red score at G3, G4 or G5 triggers a weekly review chaired by the supplier's plant manager until the score improves.

4.3 Core planning requirements

4.3.1 Cross-functional team. Every program must have a named team of at least five functions: quality, manufacturing engineering, production, logistics and purchasing. The team leader must hold a role with authority to commit capital up to EUR 50,000 without further escalation.

4.3.2 Failure analysis. Design and process failure analyses must be carried out by the team, not by one person. Any failure mode with severity 9 or 10, or with a risk priority that the supplier's own method ranks in the top 10 percent of the analysis, must have a planned prevention or detection action with an owner and a date before G4.

4.3.3 Capacity planning. Capacity is planned on a basis of 85 percent of available time at the quoted rate, using three shifts of 7.5 productive hours, five days a week, unless the program charter states otherwise. The supplier must show that peak demand of 120 percent of the nominal weekly volume can be met within four weeks using overtime or a sixth day, without new tooling.

4.3.4 Sub-supplier management. The supplier is responsible for the planning of its own sub-suppliers. Any sub-supplier that provides a part carrying a special characteristic (see section 6) must be included in the gateway self-assessment from G3.

4.4 Run-at-rate

The run-at-rate event demonstrates that the production process, using production tooling, production gauges, production operators and production-intent material, can make good parts at the quoted rate. It is scheduled in Phase 4, no later than SOP-12 weeks, and the SQE is invited at least ten working days ahead.

The rules for the event are:

1. Duration: a continuous run of at least 8 hours, or the time needed to make 300 consecutive parts, whichever is longer.
2. Rate: the process must run at 100 percent of the quoted hourly rate. A run at 95 to 99.9 percent requires a documented explanation and a second run before G5.
3. Staffing: the planned crew size and skill mix must be used. Extra helpers who would not be present in series production are not allowed.
4. Material: parts must be made from series material supplied by the series sub-suppliers, in series packaging.
5. First-pass yield: the first-time-through yield at the last operation must be at least 97.0 percent, with no rework loop that is absent from the process flow.
6. Measurement: every special characteristic is measured on at least 30 parts spread evenly across the run, and the results are used for the capability calculation described in section 5.
7. Downtime: unplanned stoppages are logged by cause. Total unplanned downtime must not exceed 5 percent of the run time.

The overall equipment effectiveness reached during the run is recorded as a reference for ramp-up. Aurelia expects at least 80 percent for automated lines and 70 percent for manual or mixed lines.

4.5 Production validation and initial samples

Initial samples are produced from the run-at-rate or a run of equal standing. The standard sample is 30 parts per tool, per cavity or per fixture. For a part with eight or more cavities the SQE may agree a reduced plan in writing. Each sample part is checked against the drawing, with a full dimensional layout, material and performance test results, and appearance review under the lighting conditions defined by the Aurelia design office. The part submission warrant is signed by a supplier officer who has the authority to bind the company.

4.6 Launch containment

Launch containment is a temporary, deliberate extra layer of inspection that starts with the first series shipment. It exists because the first weeks of production carry the highest risk of escapes.

4.6.1 Level 1. The supplier performs its own additional inspection at a dedicated station, separate from the production line and staffed by trained inspectors who do not report to production. Level 1 is mandatory for every new program. The station checks all special characteristics that cannot be fully controlled in process, plus the visual and functional checks defined in the launch containment plan.

4.6.2 Level 2. If, during Level 1, the supplier reports more than 500 defective parts per million at the station, or any single event that reaches the Aurelia line, the SQE may require Level 2. In Level 2 an additional inspection is carried out by an independent resource appointed by the supplier but selected from a list shared by Aurelia. The cost is carried by the supplier.

| Item | Level 1 | Level 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Inspection location | Supplier site, separate station | Supplier site or Aurelia receiving dock |
| Sample | 100 percent of defined characteristics | 100 percent of all listed characteristics |
| Minimum duration | 20 production days | 30 production days |
| Reporting | Daily summary by 09:00 next day | Daily summary plus weekly review call |
| Exit condition | 20 consecutive production days with zero nonconforming parts found at the station and capability targets met | 30 consecutive production days with zero escapes and 10,000 consecutive good parts |

If a defect is found during containment, the exit count restarts from zero. Parts held at the station are identified with a red tag, and the cause is investigated using a structured problem-solving method with a first containment action within 24 hours.

4.7 Ramp-up monitoring

During ramp-up the supplier reports weekly to the SQE: scrap rate, first-pass yield, delivery performance against the call-off schedule, and the capability of each special characteristic. The target for delivery performance is 98 percent of call-offs on time and in full. Targets for the first 12 weeks are 3,000 PPM or better for parts delivered to the Aurelia line, falling to 50 PPM or better by week 26.

## 5. Measurement systems and statistical process control

5.1 Purpose and scope

This section defines how suppliers prove that their measurement systems are fit for purpose, how they demonstrate process capability, and how they use statistical process control (SPC) to keep processes stable. It applies to all gauges, test equipment and software used to accept product or to control a process that makes a characteristic listed on the control plan. It also applies to equipment owned by a sub-supplier if the data are used to release parts to Aurelia.

5.2 Measurement system planning

5.2.1 Gauge selection. The resolution of a gauge must be no coarser than one tenth of the total tolerance of the characteristic. For a feature with a tolerance of 0.20 mm, the gauge must resolve at least 0.02 mm. Where a gauge reads to a finer resolution than needed, the supplier must still record data at the displayed resolution and not round it.

5.2.2 Measurement system plan. Each control plan line that calls for a measurement must refer to a measurement system plan entry that names the gauge, its identification number, the method, the operator group, the calibration interval and the study type. The plan is drawn up by Phase 3 and kept current for the life of the product.

5.2.3 Calibration. Every gauge is traceable to national standards through an accredited laboratory. The default calibration interval is 12 months for hand gauges and 6 months for coordinate measuring machines under daily use. Master parts and setting rings are calibrated every 24 months. If a gauge is found out of tolerance at calibration, the supplier must review all measurements made since the last conforming check and record the outcome.

5.3 Measurement system studies

5.3.1 Variable gauge studies. The standard study uses 10 parts that span the expected process spread, 3 appraisers and 3 trials. The result is expressed as a percentage of the characteristic tolerance (%GRR to tolerance). For a characteristic that is used to control the process, the supplier must also calculate %GRR to total process variation. The number of distinct categories (ndc) must be at least 5.

5.3.2 Attribute studies. For go/no-go gauges and visual checks, the study uses 50 parts, with at least 20 percent of parts near the limits, 3 appraisers and 3 trials. The agreement between appraisers and the reference value, measured by the kappa statistic, must be at least 0.85 for special characteristics and 0.75 for other characteristics.

5.3.3 Bias, linearity and stability. Bias and linearity are studied once when the gauge is introduced and again after any major repair. Stability is checked with a master part, measured 5 times every shift on special characteristics, and plotted on a control chart.

The limits for variable gauge studies are:

| Result (%GRR to tolerance) | Special characteristic | Other characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| 10 percent or less | Meets the requirement | Meets the requirement |
| Above 10 and up to 20 percent | Not accepted for series use; improvement plan required before G5 | Usable; improvement plan recommended |
| Above 20 and up to 30 percent | Not accepted | Usable only with a written justification from the quality manager and a plan to reach 20 percent within 6 months |
| Above 30 percent | Not accepted | Not accepted |

The ndc must be at least 5 in all cases where the table shows the study as meeting the requirement or being usable.

5.4 Process capability targets

Capability is calculated from data collected in a way that reflects real production. Aurelia distinguishes between preliminary performance (Ppk), which is based on a short run with all sources of variation present, and ongoing capability (Cpk), which is based on subgroup data from a stable process.

| Stage | Index | Special characteristic (safety or regulatory) | Special characteristic (function) | Other characteristic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Preliminary, at run-at-rate | Ppk | 2.00 or higher | 1.67 or higher | 1.33 or higher |
| Ongoing, series production | Cpk | 1.67 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 1.33 or higher |
| Long-term review, every 12 months | Ppk | 1.67 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 1.33 or higher |

The rules for calculating capability are:

1. Use at least 100 individual readings, or at least 25 subgroups of size 5, from a minimum of three production hours.
2. Test the data for normality. If the distribution is not normal, use a method suitable for the actual distribution, such as a transformation or a percentile approach, and state the method.
3. For one-sided tolerances such as flatness or position, calculate only the relevant side and note that the process boundary is zero.
4. Do not remove outliers unless a special cause has been identified and written down.
5. If the index falls below the target, 100 percent inspection with a gauge that meets the limits above is required until the target is met again.

5.5 Sampling and SPC in series production

5.5.1 Control charts. For continuous variable data on a special characteristic, the supplier uses an X-bar and R chart, or an individuals and moving range chart if parts cannot be grouped. Control limits are calculated from at least 25 subgroups of a stable process and are recalculated only when a documented process improvement has permanently reduced variation.

5.5.2 Default sampling plan. Unless the control plan states otherwise, sampling is as follows:

| Characteristic class | Subgroup size | Frequency | Chart or method |
|---|---|---|---|
| Special, safety or regulatory | 5 consecutive parts | Every hour, plus first and last part of every shift | X-bar and R chart, or 100 percent automated check |
| Special, function | 5 consecutive parts | Every 2 hours | X-bar and R chart |
| Other, with Cpk of 1.67 or higher | 3 consecutive parts | Every 4 hours | Run chart |
| Other, with Cpk between 1.33 and 1.67 | 5 consecutive parts | Every 2 hours | X-bar and R chart |

A first-off check is required after any tool change, setup, material lot change or restart of more than 30 minutes.

5.5.3 Out-of-control signals. The following signals call for action on any control chart: one point beyond a control limit; seven consecutive points on one side of the centre line; seven consecutive points rising or falling; two out of three points beyond two sigma on the same side. When a signal appears, the operator stops the process or adjusts it according to the reaction plan, and the parts made since the last conforming check are segregated and evaluated.

5.5.4 Over-adjustment. Operators must not adjust a process that is in control. A supplier whose charts show a sawtooth pattern consistent with over-adjustment must retrain the operators and show a corrected chart within four weeks.

5.6 Data retention and review

Measurement system studies, capability studies and control chart records are kept for the life of the product plus one year, and for at least 15 years for safety characteristics. The supplier's quality manager reviews capability results monthly. A summary of every special characteristic, showing the latest Cpk and the number of out-of-control signals in the period, is provided to the SQE on request within three working days.

## 6. Special characteristics and control plans

6.1 Purpose and scope

Special characteristics are the product features and process parameters that have the highest influence on safety, regulatory conformity, function, fit or customer perception. This section explains how Aurelia designates them, how the designation appears on drawings and specifications, how the supplier carries it into its own documents, and how the supplier must react when a special characteristic is out of control. The Aurelia design office assigns the designation for product features. The supplier may add process parameters that it finds critical to meeting a product feature.

6.2 Classes and symbols

Aurelia uses three classes of special characteristic. Each has a symbol on the drawing and a matching symbol in the supplier's documents.

| Class | Symbol on drawing | Short code | Meaning | Typical example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Safety and regulatory | Inverted triangle with the letter S | SC-S | Failure could cause injury or a breach of a legal requirement | Brake caliper bolt torque, airbag connector retention force |
| Critical function | Diamond with the letter F | SC-F | Failure causes loss of a primary vehicle function or major warranty cost | Piston ring gap, valve guide bore diameter |
| Appearance and fit | Square with the letter A | SC-A | Failure is visible to the customer or causes assembly difficulty | Door panel gap, paint film thickness on visible surfaces |

Symbols are drawn with a height of 5 mm on an A3 sheet, or at least 3.5 mm when the drawing is reduced, and are placed in line with the dimension or note they refer to. Aurelia does not use the supplier's own symbols on its drawings. The supplier may use internal symbols on its documents, but a conversion table to the Aurelia symbols must be included in the control plan.

6.3 Marking on drawings and models

6.3.1 Dimensions and tolerances. The symbol is placed directly adjacent to the dimension value or inside the feature control frame to which it applies. If a tolerance block carries more than one dimension, the symbol is repeated on each affected dimension.

6.3.2 Notes. A characteristic that cannot be expressed as a dimension, such as a surface finish, a material property or a process parameter, is listed in the special characteristics table in the drawing title area. Each line gives the item number, class symbol, requirement, test method and the minimum capability target from section 5.

6.3.3 Three-dimensional models. In a model-based definition, the symbol is attached to the annotation in the model and exported to the two-dimensional view. If the model and drawing disagree, the supplier must ask the Aurelia design office before manufacturing continues.

6.3.4 Count. A well-defined drawing carries no more than 12 designated characteristics for a simple part and no more than 40 for a complex assembly. A supplier who believes a drawing has too many to control effectively should raise it with the SQE during Phase 1.

6.4 Carrying special characteristics into supplier documents

Every designated characteristic appears in the following supplier documents with the same symbol and the same reference number:

1. Process flow diagram, at the step where the characteristic is created.
2. Process failure analysis, with the severity rating of 9 or 10 for SC-S, 7 or higher for SC-F, and 5 or higher for SC-A.
3. Control plan, as a dedicated line.
4. Work instruction, in a highlighted box with the nominal value, tolerance and check frequency.
5. Measurement system plan, with the study type and result.

Where a process parameter, such as a press force or an oven temperature, is found to determine a product special characteristic, it is added to the control plan as a process special characteristic. It is monitored with automatic recording where possible, and with a tolerance window tighter than the product tolerance by at least the capability needed to meet the target.

6.5 Control plan content

The control plan is a living document that covers the phases of prototype, pre-launch and production. Each plan must contain, as a minimum, the following columns.

| Column | Content required |
|---|---|
| Part and process step | Operation number and description, machine or tool identification |
| Characteristic | Product or process characteristic with class symbol |
| Specification and tolerance | Nominal value, limits, unit |
| Evaluation method | Gauge, identification number, study result |
| Sample size and frequency | As defined in section 5, or tighter if stated |
| Control method | Chart, poka-yoke, automatic rejection, first-off check |
| Reaction plan | Short description and reference to the detailed reaction instruction |
| Responsible role | Job title of the person who carries out the check and the person who reacts |

Prevention is preferred over detection. For SC-S characteristics the control method must include at least one error-proofing device that has been verified by deliberately presenting a nonconforming part. The verification is repeated at the start of each shift using a red-rabbit test piece, and the result is recorded.

6.6 Reaction plans

A reaction plan says what to do when a characteristic is nonconforming or the process is out of control. It must be specific enough that an operator can act without asking for help. The following structure is the minimum for each class.

| Step | SC-S | SC-F | SC-A |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate action | Stop the process at once and press the line stop | Stop the process or switch to 100 percent inspection | Adjust per work instruction and increase sampling |
| Containment scope | All parts since the last conforming check, including parts in transit and in the warehouse | All parts since the last conforming check | Parts since the last conforming check |
| Escalation | Shift leader immediately; quality manager within 1 hour | Shift leader immediately; quality manager within 4 hours | Shift leader within 1 shift |
| Inform Aurelia SQE | Within 4 hours if any suspect parts may have shipped | Within 24 hours if any suspect parts may have shipped | Within 3 working days if recurring |
| Restart condition | Three consecutive conforming subgroups, checked by someone other than the operator | Two consecutive conforming subgroups | One conforming subgroup |
| Root cause investigation | Structured method, first containment within 24 hours, cause within 10 working days | Within 15 working days | Within 20 working days |

Suspect parts are tagged, moved to a marked quarantine area and are not mixed with good parts. Rework is allowed only according to a written rework instruction that has its own control plan line. A rework operation that touches a special characteristic is followed by a full re-check of that characteristic on 100 percent of reworked parts.

6.7 Review of control plans

The supplier reviews each control plan at least once every 12 months and after any customer complaint, internal escape or process failure involving a special characteristic. The review confirms that the symbols, sample sizes, gauges and reaction plans still match the real process. The review is documented with the date, the participants and the outcome, and kept with the control plan. During a review, the team compares the number of reactions triggered in the last 12 months with the expected number. A characteristic with more than 6 reactions in a year is selected for a process improvement project.

## 7. Packaging, labelling and identification

**4.1 Purpose and scope**

This section sets out how suppliers of Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") pack, label and identify production parts, so that material arrives at the plants in Modena, Cassino and Kragujevac undamaged, clean, traceable and readable by scanner. It applies to all series-production and service parts, to pre-series parts shipped to a plant, and to sub-tier material that a supplier ships directly to Aurelia on behalf of a Tier 1 supplier. The requirements below are minimums. Where a packaging specification sheet for a part gives a tighter value, the sheet governs for that part.

The supplier is responsible for the condition of the part until the receiving operator at the plant has scanned the container. Damage, mix-ups and unreadable labels found at goods receipt are handled under the nonconforming product process.

**4.2 Returnable containers**

Aurelia operates a pool of returnable load carriers and prefers them for every part with a weekly volume above 400 pieces. Expendable packaging is used only where the packaging specification sheet allows it. Suppliers must not mix pool containers with their own or third-party containers on the same pallet.

| Container code | Type | External size (mm) | Max gross load | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AR-S1 | Small load carrier, foldable | 600 x 400 x 280 | 15 kg | Brackets, clips, small electronics |
| AR-S2 | Small load carrier, solid | 600 x 400 x 420 | 20 kg | Fasteners, small castings |
| AR-M1 | Medium pallet box | 1200 x 800 x 600 | 350 kg | Stampings, trim parts |
| AR-L1 | Large pallet box, drop-side | 1200 x 1000 x 970 | 800 kg | Brake discs, axle components |
| AR-R1 | Dedicated rack | Per drawing | Per drawing | Body panels, bumpers, glazing |

Containers are owned by Aurelia and remain its property. A supplier receives containers on loan and must keep a separate stock record for them. The target for container balance is a supplier stock that does not exceed 1.5 times the weekly requirement of that supplier, plus a buffer of 10 percent. A supplier that holds more than 2.5 times the weekly requirement for two consecutive weeks will be asked to return the surplus within 5 working days at its own cost.

**4.3 Handling, stacking and condition of containers**

1. Containers must be inspected on receipt from Aurelia and before every fill. Broken latches, cracked walls, bent rack frames and torn dividers are sorted out and returned to the plant with a damage tag.
2. Pallet boxes may be stacked at most three high in transit, and small load carriers at most six high on a pallet, unless the sheet states a different limit.
3. Each pallet must be strapped or wrapped so that it withstands a 0.5 g braking force without shifting. Wrap must not cover the container label.
4. Containers are filled to the quantity on the sheet, normally a fixed standard quantity. Part-filled containers are allowed only for the last container of a delivery, and they must carry a visible "partial" marker and the actual quantity on the label.
5. Parts must not protrude beyond the container edge and must not be in direct contact with each other where the sheet requires dividers, foam inserts or interleaving.
6. Aurelia charges EUR 180 for a lost small load carrier, EUR 640 for a lost pallet box and EUR 2,900 for a lost dedicated rack, applied to the supplier whose booking record shows the container last.

**4.4 Labels**

Every container and every pallet carries labels in a fixed position and in a fixed format. Labels are printed in black on white, on adhesive stock that stays attached for at least 6 months in a range from -20 to +70 degrees Celsius and is resistant to rubbing and light moisture. Handwritten corrections are not permitted.

| Label | Size | Applied to | Position | Mandatory content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part label | A6, 105 x 148 mm | Each container | Upper right of the short side, 20 mm from the edge | Aurelia part number, description, quantity, supplier code, batch, date, plant and unloading point |
| Pallet label | A5, 148 x 210 mm | Each pallet | Two adjacent sides, upper right corner | Despatch note number, number of containers, gross weight, ship-to |
| Mixed-load label | A5 | Pallets with more than one part number | Two adjacent sides | List of all part numbers and quantities, one barcode for each |
| Sample label | A6, orange border | Trial or sample parts | Next to the part label | Sample type, drawing index, inspection reference |

The part number printed on the label has 10 digits, followed by a two-letter index. The supplier code has six characters, as assigned by Aurelia purchasing. The date shown is the date of manufacture in the form DD.MM.YYYY. Text height is at least 3 mm for the part number and quantity, and at least 2 mm for other fields.

**4.5 Barcodes and print quality**

The part label carries a linear Code 128 barcode for each of the part number, quantity, supplier code and batch, and one two-dimensional Data Matrix symbol that contains all four fields in the order given in the field table of the packaging specification sheet. The pallet label carries the despatch note number as a Code 128 barcode.

Barcode requirements are as follows.

- Narrowest bar width (module): 0.25 mm minimum for linear codes, 0.5 mm for Data Matrix.
- Bar height of linear codes: at least 12 mm.
- Quiet zone: at least 5 mm on each side of the symbol.
- Print quality: grade B (3.0) or better on a 0 to 4 scale when measured with a verifier at the supplier's despatch area.
- Reading test: a handheld scanner must read the code on the first attempt from 20 to 60 cm, at 15 degrees tilt.

Suppliers must verify at least 1 label in every 200 printed, and at the start of each shift, and keep the verifier results for 24 months. A scan failure rate above 0.5 percent at a plant over a calendar month will result in a corrective action request to the supplier.

**4.6 Part identification and traceability marking**

Parts are marked directly where the drawing demands it. Each marked part must carry the supplier code, the date of manufacture in a coded form, and the production line or tool cavity number. Where there is no direct marking, the container label is the sole identification, and the supplier must keep records that allow the lot to be linked to the raw material lot, the process parameters and the final inspection results.

Traceability targets are:

- Identification of all lots delivered in the last 12 months within 4 working hours of a request.
- Identification of the affected quantity within one lot to a maximum of 500 pieces.
- Retention of lot records for 15 years for safety-relevant parts and 10 years for all others.

Batches must not be mixed. A new batch number starts at each material lot change, each tool change and each restart after an interruption longer than 60 minutes.

**4.7 Cleanliness**

Parts must arrive free of swarf, burrs, dust, residual cutting fluid and other loose contamination, and packaging must prevent contamination in transit. Where a part is cleanliness-controlled, the drawing states the class. Aurelia uses the following internal classes for extraction tests.

| Class | Typical parts | Max particle mass per part | Max particle size (longest dimension) |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Fuel and brake system components | 1.0 mg | 200 micrometres |
| C2 | Gearbox and engine internals | 3.0 mg | 400 micrometres |
| C3 | Chassis and body hardware | 10 mg | 800 micrometres |
| C4 | Visible-surface trim | Visual only | No visible particles at 0.5 m |

Cleanliness-controlled parts are packed in sealed bags or covered boxes with a tamper-evident closure and are never put into a container that has been used for uncleaned material. Pool containers used for class C1 and C2 parts must be cleaned by the supplier before each fill, and a cleaning record must be kept. Supplier extraction tests are run at least once per 5,000 parts and at least once a week, and the results are kept with the lot records.

**4.8 Storage, transit protection and audits**

Packed goods are stored in a dry, covered area. Pallets must be placed on the floor of a trailer in a way that avoids contact with the trailer wall, and trailers are inspected for cleanliness and dryness before loading. Aurelia plant staff check packaging at goods receipt on 5 pallets per delivery as a minimum and record the results. A supplier whose packaging causes more than 3 damage or labelling findings per 100 pallets in a quarter is visited by a supplier quality engineer, and the findings are reviewed in a joint packaging workshop within 30 days.

## 8. Logistics, delivery performance and scheduling

**5.1 Purpose and scope**

This section describes how Aurelia communicates demand to suppliers, how and when suppliers deliver, how delivery performance is measured and how extra transport costs are shared. It covers all series suppliers shipping to Aurelia plants, and to the central service parts warehouse in Bologna. The aim is a stable, predictable flow with no line stoppages and minimum stock.

**5.2 Electronic data interchange**

Suppliers with an annual value above EUR 250,000 must connect to Aurelia through the supplier portal gateway for electronic data interchange. Smaller suppliers may use the web portal. The messages exchanged are shown below.

| Message | Direction | Content | Frequency | Supplier response time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DELFOR | Aurelia to supplier | Forecast by week, 26-week horizon | Weekly, every Monday by 06:00 CET | Confirmation within 2 working days |
| DELJIT | Aurelia to supplier | Short-term call-off by day and delivery window | Daily, 14:00 CET | Acknowledgement within 2 hours |
| DESADV | Supplier to Aurelia | Advance shipping notice | At departure of the vehicle | Within 30 minutes of departure |
| INVOIC | Supplier to Aurelia | Invoice or credit note | After goods receipt | Within 5 working days of receipt |

The supplier must monitor its inbound gateway at least every 30 minutes on working days. A message that is not acknowledged within the set time is treated as received. A supplier must name two contacts for data interchange, one of whom must be reachable from 06:00 to 18:00 on working days. Gateway availability is expected to be 99.5 percent per month, and a supplier-side interruption longer than 4 hours must be reported by telephone to the logistics control desk.

**5.3 Forecasts and capacity**

The forecast shows planned demand and is not a purchase commitment unless a separate agreement states otherwise. Suppliers must compare each forecast to their capacity and respond if they cannot meet it. The following flexibility is expected at constant lead time.

| Horizon | Weeks | Supplier must supply up to |
|---|---|---|
| Frozen | 1 to 2 | 100 percent of the call-off, with no reduction |
| Short term | 3 to 6 | 120 percent of the last forecast |
| Medium term | 7 to 12 | 110 percent of the last forecast |
| Long term | 13 to 26 | For planning only; capacity review every quarter |

Suppliers must hold capacity for at least 115 percent of the peak weekly forecast of the next 12 weeks. Where a supplier sees a risk of falling below this level, it must tell the Aurelia buyer and the logistics planner in writing within 2 working days. A capacity review, including a shift model and bottleneck analysis, is carried out on site for suppliers with a delivery performance below the target for two consecutive months.

**5.4 Delivery windows and transport**

Each DELJIT call-off gives the plant, the unloading point, and the delivery window. Deliveries must arrive within the window. Earlier or later arrival may be refused or held at the supplier's cost.

| Plant | Standard window | Early tolerance | Late tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Modena assembly | 60 minutes | 30 minutes | 0 minutes |
| Cassino powertrain | 120 minutes | 60 minutes | 0 minutes |
| Kragujevac assembly | 120 minutes | 60 minutes | 0 minutes |
| Bologna service parts | 4 hours | 2 hours | 60 minutes |

The supplier must plan transit time with a safety margin of at least 10 percent. Trucks must have a tracking system that can share position data with Aurelia on request. Delivery quantity must match the call-off within a tolerance of zero for sequenced parts and plus or minus 2 percent for other parts, but never below the called quantity for the frozen horizon. Over-deliveries are returned or stored at the supplier's expense, and a fee of EUR 45 per pallet is charged for handling.

**5.5 Despatch notes and goods receipt**

Each vehicle must carry a printed despatch note listing every pallet, and the data must match the DESADV. A mismatch between the advance notice and the physical goods of more than 1 pallet or 2 percent of pieces is recorded as a documentation error. Goods are booked in at the plant by scanning the pallet label. Vehicles that arrive without a valid advance notice wait until it is received, and unloading is not guaranteed within the window.

**5.6 Delivery performance measurement**

Aurelia measures delivery performance each month for every supplier and plant. A delivery counts as on time only if it arrives in the window, with the called quantity, with a matching advance notice and with readable labels.

- Target for on-time-in-full delivery: 98.5 percent per month.
- Target for advance notice accuracy: 99.0 percent.
- Target for schedule adherence of the frozen horizon: 100 percent.

The score is published on the supplier portal on the fifth working day of the following month. A score below 95 percent in one month, or below 98.5 percent in three months out of six, leads to a recovery plan that the supplier submits within 10 working days.

**5.7 Contingency stock and shortages**

Suppliers must hold finished goods stock for at least 5 working days of average demand for parts that are produced on a single line or by a single tool, and 3 working days for all others. For imported parts, stock of 10 working days is held at the supplier or at a nearby warehouse. A supplier that expects a shortage must inform the logistics control desk at once, by telephone and e-mail, giving the part numbers, the expected missing quantity per day and the date of recovery. A shortage message sent less than 24 hours before the planned delivery is treated as a late message and recorded in the score.

**5.8 Premium freight**

Premium freight means any transport faster or more expensive than the standard route, for instance express road, courier, air or a special vehicle. It is used to protect production only when other options have been exhausted. All premium freight needs the authorisation of the Aurelia logistics control desk, which issues a reference number before the shipment leaves. The cost is assigned by cause.

| Cause | Cost borne by | Remarks |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier capacity, quality, tool or planning problem | Supplier, 100 percent | Includes recovery of a delayed delivery |
| Late supplier shipment after the cut-off | Supplier, 100 percent | Cut-off is 2 hours before departure |
| Aurelia call-off within the frozen horizon, supplier at stated capacity | Aurelia, 100 percent | Supplier must show capacity records |
| Call-off above the flexibility in section 5.3 | Aurelia, 100 percent | Only for the quantity above the limit |
| Carrier fault | Carrier, through the supplier | Supplier recovers the cost |
| Force majeure | Shared 50:50 | Requires evidence within 5 working days |

Premium freight without a reference number is not reimbursed. In addition to the freight cost, an administration fee of EUR 150 per shipment is charged to the responsible party. Suppliers whose premium freight cost exceeds 0.4 percent of their annual delivery value in any rolling 12-month period are asked to present a root cause review with measures.

**5.9 Escalation of delivery problems**

When the on-time-in-full result for a supplier falls under 95 percent, or when a stoppage is expected, the following escalation applies. At stage 1 the logistics planner and the supplier's customer service lead hold a daily call. At stage 2 the supplier's plant manager and an Aurelia purchasing director take part in a weekly review. At stage 3 the supplier's top management presents a recovery plan at Aurelia in Modena. A line stoppage caused by the supplier is charged under the rules of the nonconforming product section.

## 9. Nonconforming product and problem solving

**6.1 Purpose and scope**

This section defines how Aurelia reports nonconforming product to a supplier, how quickly the supplier must act, how the problem-solving report is structured, and which costs are charged back. It applies to nonconformities found at Aurelia plants, in the service network, in the field, and in Aurelia incoming inspection, whatever the part and whichever production stage.

**6.2 Complaint handling**

A complaint is raised by the Aurelia plant quality department or the supplier quality engineer and is sent to the supplier's named quality contact through the supplier portal and by e-mail. Each complaint receives a unique number and includes the part number, quantity found, batch, defect description, photographs, the place of detection and the number of vehicles affected, where known.

Complaints are classified by severity as follows.

| Class | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| A | Safety, legal or regulatory relevance, or a vehicle that cannot be driven or sold | Loose brake component, wrong part in a safety assembly |
| B | Line stoppage or rework at the plant, or functional fault found by a customer | Fitting problem stopping the line for more than 10 minutes |
| C | Visual, minor fit or documentation deviation without stoppage | Label mix-up, scratch on a hidden surface |

The supplier must name a responsible person for each complaint at the time of acknowledgement. Complaint contacts must be reachable on all production days from 06:00 to 22:00 for suppliers delivering to a three-shift plant.

**6.3 Response times**

The following time limits start at the moment Aurelia sends the complaint to the supplier, counted in clock hours for class A and in working time for the others.

| Step | Class A | Class B | Class C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acknowledgement and named contact | 2 hours | 4 hours | 1 working day |
| Immediate containment at supplier, in transit and at Aurelia | 4 hours | 24 hours | 3 working days |
| Confirmed sorting result and clean-lot date | 24 hours | 48 hours | 5 working days |
| Interim report (D1 to D4) | 3 working days | 5 working days | 10 working days |
| Final report (D1 to D8) | 20 working days | 30 working days | 45 working days |

If the supplier cannot reach the interim or final date, it must ask for an extension before the date, giving the reason and a new date. Aurelia decides whether to grant it. An extension granted does not suspend the chargeback rules.

**6.4 Containment and sorting**

Containment covers stock at the supplier, in transit, at the Aurelia plant and at any external warehouse. The supplier must give a written count for each of these locations. Parts that were made after the last known good lot are treated as suspect until inspected. Where Aurelia staff or a sorting service must be used at the plant to keep the line running, the supplier is told in advance and the cost is charged as set out below. Sorted-out nonconforming parts are tagged in red, kept in a locked area and returned to the supplier only on Aurelia instruction; if no instruction is given within 30 days, they are scrapped at the supplier's cost.

Certified clean lots are marked with a green dot label on every container for at least 10 consecutive deliveries or 4 weeks, whichever is longer. The label shows the date of the clean-lot cut-in.

**6.5 The 8D format**

The problem-solving report follows the eight disciplines. The supplier uses the Aurelia template on the portal or a template with the same fields.

| Discipline | Content required |
|---|---|
| D1 Team | Names, functions and phone numbers of at least 3 people, including a person from production and a person with decision authority |
| D2 Problem description | Is and is-not analysis, quantity, photographs, measurements against the drawing |
| D3 Containment | Actions, responsible person, date, stock counts, verification of effectiveness |
| D4 Root cause | Technical cause of occurrence and technical cause of escape, with evidence such as tests, repeat of the fault or process data |
| D5 Corrective actions | Actions for both causes, a ranking of options, expected effect |
| D6 Implementation and validation | Implementation date, validation data from at least 3 consecutive lots, updated control plan and process failure analysis |
| D7 Prevention | Transfer to similar parts, tools and lines, update of lessons learned |
| D8 Team recognition and summary | Short summary, cost, lessons for the next project |

A root cause that names "operator error" or "human mistake" without a stated process or system reason is not accepted as a final cause. Root cause analysis must use a structured method such as the 5-why chain or a fault tree, and the supplier must show the cause by repeating or measuring the fault where this is possible. The percentage of 8D reports that Aurelia returns with a request for rework is tracked as a quality measure for each supplier, and the target is below 10 percent.

Aurelia gives written feedback on each interim and final report within 5 working days of receiving it.

**6.6 Returns, analysis and field parts**

Parts returned from the field for analysis are sent to the supplier with a tag showing mileage, vehicle age and the failure description. The supplier must analyse a field part within 10 working days of receiving it and must report the result with photographs. Where the supplier finds no fault, it must show the test method and the test result and, if requested, join a joint analysis at the Aurelia laboratory in Cassino within 15 working days. Class A complaints always receive a joint review at the earliest possible time.

**6.7 Chargebacks**

Aurelia charges the supplier the cost caused by a nonconformity that it is responsible for. The fixed rates below apply from the day the complaint is raised. Actual cost is charged where it is higher and can be shown with documents.

| Cost item | Rate |
|---|---|
| Handling fee for each complaint | EUR 120 (class C), EUR 350 (class B), EUR 900 (class A) |
| Sorting or rework by Aurelia staff | EUR 46 per hour per person |
| External sorting service | Actual invoice plus 10 percent |
| Line stoppage | EUR 780 per minute, from the first minute |
| Scrapped Aurelia material | Part cost plus 15 percent handling |
| Premium freight | Under the rules for premium freight |
| Service or recall action | Actual cost to Aurelia, shared by cause |
| Supplier analysis not completed on time | EUR 200 per working day of delay, up to EUR 4,000 |

Chargebacks are sent as a debit note with a breakdown through the supplier portal, and are deducted from the next payment after 15 working days unless the supplier files an objection. An objection must be sent in writing with supporting evidence within those 15 working days, and Aurelia replies within 10 working days. Disputes that cannot be settled by the supplier quality manager and the Aurelia purchasing manager are passed to the commodity director for a decision.

**6.8 Performance and escalation**

Quality performance is measured in parts per million (ppm) delivered against parts found nonconforming at Aurelia. The target for series suppliers is 20 ppm or less per quarter, and zero for class A. Escalation is graded as follows.

- Level 1, controlled shipping: the supplier adds an extra inspection of the affected feature at its plant after the process, using a person independent of production, and sends the results with each delivery. This applies when ppm exceeds 50 in a quarter or after any class A complaint.
- Level 2, external controlled shipping: a third-party inspection company, chosen by Aurelia, inspects at the supplier's cost. This applies when level 1 does not give a clean result in 6 weeks, or when ppm exceeds 150.
- Level 3, business review: the supplier's top management and the Aurelia purchasing director meet to agree a recovery plan, and new business allocation is considered.

The supplier stays in controlled shipping until it delivers 8 consecutive weeks, or 3 consecutive lots, whichever is longer, with no nonconformity of the same type.

## 10. Warranty and field returns

4.1 Purpose and scope

This section sets out how Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") handles parts that are returned from vehicles in the field, how those parts are analysed, how no-trouble-found results are treated, and how warranty costs are recovered from suppliers. It applies to every supplier that delivers series parts, service parts or accessories to the Modena assembly plant, the Bologna powertrain plant or the Aurelia Parts Distribution Centre in Parma. The aim is simple: find the true cause of each field failure quickly, return the learning to the process that created the defect, and place cost with the party that caused it.

The following terms are used throughout.

- Field return: a part removed from a customer vehicle by an Aurelia dealer or authorised workshop because of a suspected fault.
- Warranty period: 36 months or 60,000 km for standard parts, 60 months or 100,000 km for the traction battery pack and its control electronics, and 24 months with unlimited distance for service parts, whichever limit is reached first unless stated otherwise.
- Cause-confirmed return: a field return in which the analysis reproduces and explains the reported fault.
- No-trouble-found (NTF): a field return in which neither the reported fault nor any other deviation from specification can be reproduced after the full analysis sequence in 4.4.
- Parts per million (PPM) in the field: the number of confirmed supplier-caused failures divided by the number of vehicles in service that contain the part, multiplied by one million.

4.2 Return parts handling

Dealers send removed parts to the Aurelia Returned Parts Centre in Parma within 5 working days of removal. Each part carries a tag showing the vehicle identification number, mileage, repair order date, fault code and the dealer's symptom description. Parts without a legible tag are held for 10 working days while the dealer is asked for the missing data. If the data cannot be recovered, the part is analysed on a technical basis only and is excluded from cost recovery.

The Returned Parts Centre applies the following rules.

1. Parts are stored in the condition received. No cleaning, reassembly, reflashing or functional testing is done before the supplier's identification marks are photographed.
2. Each part receives a return number and is stored in a locked, climate-controlled area held at 15 to 25 degrees Celsius and below 60 percent relative humidity.
3. Electronic control units are placed in anti-static bags and have their stored fault memory read out and saved before any other step.
4. Fluid-filled components are drained only under the written instruction of the analysis lead and the fluid is retained with the part.
5. A part that is released to a supplier for analysis is documented in the Returned Parts Centre log with the date, the recipient, and the quantity.

Suppliers may request parts through the Aurelia Supplier Portal. Parts are shipped at Aurelia's expense, in returnable boxes, within 3 working days of a request. Suppliers must retain all returned parts, and every fragment of any part that was dismantled, for at least 12 months after the analysis report is issued, so that Aurelia can inspect them on request.

4.3 Analysis timeline

The supplier's response times begin on the day the return is released for supplier analysis, which is shown in the portal. The times are fixed and apply to each return number.

| Step | Supplier obligation | Time limit from release |
|---|---|---|
| Receipt confirmation | Confirm part count and condition on the portal | 2 working days |
| Containment statement | State whether other vehicles or stock may be affected and name the actions taken | 24 hours after receipt confirmation |
| Initial analysis result | Visual inspection, non-destructive tests, comparison with the production record | 5 working days |
| Root-cause report | Full analysis with evidence, occurrence cause and escape cause | 15 working days |
| Corrective action plan | Dated actions with named owners, covering both causes | 20 working days |
| Effectiveness check | Data showing the corrective action works in production and in the field | 90 days after implementation |

Where the fault affects safety or could lead to a vehicle stopping on the road, the containment statement is due within 8 hours and the root-cause report within 10 working days. Aurelia states this classification when the part is released.

4.4 No-trouble-found analysis

NTF results are costly. In 2023 about 31 percent of electronic parts returned under warranty at Aurelia were first reported as NTF, and each one cost on average EUR 184 in handling, shipping and workshop labour before any supplier was involved. The target for the supplier base is an NTF share of no more than 15 percent of returns per part family.

Before a return may be classified as NTF, the supplier must complete every step of the following sequence and record the result of each step.

1. Read and compare the fault memory and the vehicle data supplied by Aurelia, including freeze-frame data at the time of the fault.
2. Perform an incoming inspection including X-ray or microscopy where the part type allows it.
3. Run the full functional test at room temperature and then across the temperature range of the specification, at least minus 40 and plus 105 degrees Celsius for under-bonnet electronics.
4. Subject the part to a vibration and thermal-cycling profile agreed with Aurelia. The minimum is 48 hours for electronics and 24 hours for mechanical assemblies.
5. Test the part on a reference vehicle or an equivalent bench simulation provided by Aurelia when requested.
6. Compare the part with 10 retained samples from the same production lot, using the same tests.

If the fault remains absent after all six steps, the supplier states NTF in the root-cause report. Aurelia then reviews the vehicle history. Where three or more NTF returns with the same fault code occur in the same part family within any rolling 6-month period, the supplier and Aurelia open a joint investigation and the NTF returns are treated as unexplained field failures. This investigation includes a review of connector design, software interaction, and the diagnostic logic of the vehicle.

Cost treatment of NTF returns is shown in 4.5. Suppliers may not classify a return as NTF simply because the part passes a standard end-of-line test, since the end-of-line test was passed at delivery.

4.5 Cost recovery

Aurelia recovers from the supplier the cost of warranty claims that are caused by the supplier's part, process or documentation. Recovery is based on the root-cause report and on the cost data in the warranty system. The cost elements are as follows.

| Cost element | Recoverable | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement part at purchase price | Yes | Price paid by Aurelia at the time of delivery |
| Dealer labour | Yes | Flat-rate repair time multiplied by the agreed rate of EUR 72 per hour |
| Diagnostic time | Yes, up to 1.5 hours | Same hourly rate |
| Towing and mobility service | Yes | Actual invoice, capped at EUR 350 per case |
| Handling and logistics in Aurelia | Yes | Fixed EUR 45 per returned part |
| Analysis at Aurelia laboratory | Yes | EUR 95 per analysis hour, maximum 12 hours per return |
| Goodwill payment to customer | Only if agreed in advance | Actual amount |
| NTF return | 50 percent of the cost elements above | Applies when the supplier completed 4.4 and the return remains NTF |

A supplier is liable for 100 percent of the elements above when the cause is confirmed to lie in the supplier's part or process. For NTF returns the supplier carries 50 percent of the costs when the same part number shows an NTF rate above 15 percent over a rolling 12 months, and 0 percent otherwise.

4.6 Invoicing and disputes

Aurelia invoices recoverable cost monthly, in arrears, with a line listing for each return number. Payment is due within 45 days of the invoice date. A supplier that disagrees with a line must say so in writing within 30 days, naming the return number and giving technical evidence. Aurelia replies within 20 working days. Where the parties still disagree, a joint review is held at the Parma Returned Parts Centre, with the part on the table, within 30 days. The part is examined by an engineer from each side and, if needed, by an independent laboratory chosen jointly. The laboratory fee is borne by the party whose position is not upheld.

4.7 Field performance targets

Field performance is evaluated per part family on a rolling 12-month basis. For standard components the target is 15 PPM in the field at 12 months in service. For safety-relevant components the target is 3 PPM. These figures feed the supplier scorecard described in the next section, and their calculation is the same on both documents.

## 11. Supplier performance scorecard and escalation

5.1 Purpose

Aurelia measures every supplier of series parts with a scorecard that is issued each month and summarised each quarter. The scorecard gives suppliers and Aurelia buyers the same objective view of quality, delivery, responsiveness and cost. It also determines when Aurelia intervenes and what steps follow. This section describes the key performance indicators, the rating bands, the four levels of escalation and the conditions under which Aurelia holds new business.

5.2 Key performance indicators

Five indicators make up the scorecard. Each is calculated by plant and part family and rolled up to supplier level using delivery volume as the weight.

| KPI | Definition | Target | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incoming quality PPM | Rejected parts divided by delivered parts, times one million, over the previous 3 months | 50 PPM or lower | 30 percent |
| Field PPM | Confirmed supplier-caused field failures per million vehicles in service, rolling 12 months | 15 PPM or lower (3 PPM for safety-relevant parts) | 25 percent |
| Delivery performance | Deliveries received on the confirmed date and in the confirmed quantity, divided by all deliveries | 98.5 percent or higher | 20 percent |
| Corrective action response | Share of requested corrective action reports delivered by the due date, with an acceptable root cause | 95 percent or higher | 15 percent |
| Line disruptions | Minutes of assembly line stoppage caused by the supplier per 1,000 vehicles | 4 minutes or lower | 10 percent |

Parts rejected because of Aurelia's own errors, such as wrong drawings or damage during unloading, are removed from the count after review by the plant quality manager. Premium freight caused by the supplier is recorded and charged but is not part of the score.

5.3 Scoring method

Each KPI is converted to a score from 0 to 100. A supplier that meets the target scores 100. The score falls in a straight line to 0 at the limit value, which is 4 times the target for PPM indicators, 90 percent for delivery performance, 60 percent for corrective action response and 20 minutes for line disruptions. The weighted total gives the supplier score.

For example, a supplier with 120 incoming PPM against a target of 50 has a score of 100 minus ((120 minus 50) divided by (200 minus 50) times 100), which is 53.3 for that indicator. Multiplied by the weight of 0.30 this contributes 16.0 points.

The score is calculated on the third working day of each month and is shown on the Aurelia Supplier Portal on the same day. Suppliers have 10 working days to question any data point. If they do not, the figure stands for that month.

5.4 Rating bands

The supplier score places each supplier in one of four bands.

| Band | Score | Meaning | Standard response |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 90 to 100 | Preferred | Eligible for new sourcing and for annual recognition |
| B | 75 to under 90 | Acceptable | Improvement plan for any KPI below target |
| C | 60 to under 75 | Concern | Review meeting with the purchasing and quality leads |
| D | Under 60 | Not acceptable | Entry to the escalation process below |

The band is also affected by overrides. Any single event in which a supplier-caused defect leads to a stop-ship of vehicles, or reaches customers in a quantity above 200 vehicles, puts the supplier in band D for at least 3 months regardless of the score.

5.5 Levels of escalation

Aurelia uses four levels. A supplier enters a level when the trigger in the table is met and remains at that level until the exit criteria in 5.7 are met.

| Level | Trigger | Aurelia actions | Supplier obligations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Watch | Band C for 2 consecutive months, or incoming PPM above 200 in one month | Written notice from the supplier quality engineer; monthly review call | Recovery plan within 15 working days |
| 2 Formal improvement | Band D for 1 month, or Level 1 with no improvement of at least 8 score points after 3 months | Plant visit within 10 working days; weekly progress review; supplier quality engineer on site up to 2 days a week | Dedicated project lead; weekly data submission; process review of the affected line |
| 3 Controlled shipping | Band D for 3 consecutive months, or a repeat of the same defect cause within 6 months | Additional inspection of the supplier's product, at the supplier's cost, by Aurelia or a third party; senior purchasing and quality directors meet the supplier's management | Inspection of 100 percent of the affected parts before shipment; daily result reporting; costs charged at EUR 62 per inspector hour |
| 4 Executive | Level 3 for 3 months with no improvement, or a failure that causes a vehicle stop-ship with customer safety exposure | Chief Purchasing Officer and Head of Quality meet the supplier's chief executive; sourcing review for all current and planned business | Executive recovery commitment; independent audit of the manufacturing site at the supplier's cost |

An improvement of at least 8 score points means the supplier score is 8 points higher than at the start of the period.

5.6 New business hold

A new business hold stops the award of new part numbers, new models and additional volume to a supplier. Aurelia applies it as follows.

1. Level 1: no hold. The buyer notes the status in the sourcing file and the supplier is told that a hold could follow.
2. Level 2: hold on new part numbers in the same commodity group as the affected parts. Quotations already submitted are evaluated, but no award is made.
3. Level 3: hold on all new business at the affected plant sites of the supplier. Existing nominated programmes continue, but the supplier is not invited to tender for new work.
4. Level 4: hold on all new business with the supplier across the whole company. Existing contracts are reviewed, and Aurelia may begin to develop a second source for any part for which the supplier is the only one.

The hold is lifted by the Sourcing Committee, which meets on the first Tuesday of each month. A hold imposed because of an override under 5.4 runs for a minimum of 3 months. The committee considers the supplier score, the status of corrective actions, any audit result, and the capacity of the supplier to launch new work safely.

5.7 Exit criteria and recognition

A supplier leaves a level when it reaches band B or better for 3 consecutive months and the root causes of the issues that led to the escalation have been verified as removed by an Aurelia supplier quality engineer on site. For Level 3 and Level 4, a further 3 months of data without a return to band C is needed. When a supplier steps down, the hold on new business is lifted at the next committee meeting.

Suppliers with band A for 12 consecutive months receive the Aurelia Quality Partner designation, a reduction of incoming inspection to skip-lot sampling at one in four deliveries, and priority in tender invitations. In the 2023 scorecard year, 41 of 212 production suppliers reached band A, 118 were in band B, 41 in band C and 12 in band D, and 5 were at Level 2 or higher at year end.

## 12. Traceability, records retention and audits

6.1 Purpose and principles

If a defect is found, Aurelia and its suppliers need to know within hours which vehicles contain the affected parts, and which stock can still be used. This section defines the traceability that suppliers must maintain, the periods for which records are kept, and the audit programme used to verify both. Three principles apply: every delivered part can be tied to a lot, every lot can be tied to its materials and process records, and every record can be retrieved in a stated time.

6.2 Lot traceability

The supplier defines a production lot for each part number. A lot must not exceed the quantities in the table, and a new lot must begin at the start of each shift, after every tool or fixture maintenance, and after every interruption longer than 60 minutes.

| Part category | Maximum lot size | Required lot link |
|---|---|---|
| Safety-relevant parts (brakes, steering, airbag control) | 1 shift or 1,000 pieces | Serial number on each part, linked to material batch, machine, tool, operator and test data |
| Electronic control units | 1 shift or 2,500 pieces | Serial number and date code on each unit, linked to board lot, software version and end-of-line result |
| Traction battery cells and modules | 1 shift or 500 modules | Cell lot and module serial number, linked to coating, formation and capacity data |
| Machined and cast components | 1 shift or 5,000 pieces | Lot label and date code, linked to raw material heat or melt number and heat-treatment batch |
| Trim, plastics and other parts | 1 day or 10,000 pieces | Lot label, linked to raw material batch and moulding tool |

Each delivery unit carries a label with the Aurelia part number, supplier code, lot number, production date, quantity and, for serialised parts, the serial number range. Labels are printed in a machine-readable format and are scanned on receipt at the Aurelia plants. Mixed lots in one container are not permitted. If a container holds parts from two lots, each lot is separated and marked.

Sub-suppliers are covered by the same rules. The supplier is responsible for passing the traceability requirement to its own sub-suppliers and must be able to show the lot link from the Aurelia part to the sub-supplier lot within the retrieval times in 6.4.

6.3 Records retention

Retention periods start at the end of the calendar year in which the part was delivered, unless stated otherwise. These are the minimum periods. Where the supplier's own law or contract requires longer, the longer period applies.

| Record type | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Serial number and lot genealogy for safety-relevant parts | 25 years |
| Serial number and lot genealogy for other series parts | 15 years |
| Process parameter records (for example torque, weld, cure, fill) | 15 years for safety-relevant parts, 10 years for others |
| End-of-line and final inspection results | 15 years for safety-relevant parts, 10 years for others |
| Material certificates and sub-supplier lot records | 15 years |
| Control plans, process flow diagrams and failure mode analyses | Duration of series supply plus 15 years |
| Calibration records of measuring equipment | 10 years |
| Corrective action and complaint records | 10 years |
| Internal audit and training records | 6 years |
| Retained samples from each lot | 2 years for standard parts, 5 years for safety-relevant parts |

Records may be kept on paper or in electronic form. Electronic records must be backed up at least daily, stored in a second location at least 20 kilometres from the first, and be readable throughout the full retention period. If a supplier ceases to produce for Aurelia, the records stay with the supplier for the remainder of the period, or are handed to Aurelia in a readable format, by agreement.

When the retention period ends, records are destroyed in a controlled way. A destruction log is kept for 5 years.

6.4 Retrieval times and trace exercises

Suppliers must be able to produce the records requested by Aurelia within the following times, counted from the request.

| Request | Time limit |
|---|---|
| List of lots and serial numbers delivered on a given date or in a given range | 2 hours |
| Identification of the lots in stock, in transit and at Aurelia sites | 4 hours |
| Material and sub-supplier lot link for one lot | 24 hours |
| Full genealogy record for one serial number | 24 hours |
| Complete record package for up to 10 lots | 5 working days |

To test this capability, each supplier of safety-relevant parts completes one trace exercise per year, and all other suppliers complete one every 2 years. Aurelia chooses a part delivered between 2 and 12 months earlier and asks for the full lot genealogy without notice. The supplier must identify at least 98 percent of the quantity produced in the lot, find every affected finished part in stock or in transit, and name the receiving plants. A result below 98 percent leads to a repeat exercise within 60 days and a score deduction in the corrective action indicator.

6.5 Audit schedule

Aurelia audits each supplier on a risk-based schedule. The risk class is set once a year from the supplier score, the part category, the supplier's performance in recent audits and the volume supplied. The following frequencies apply.

| Audit type | Scope | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| System audit | Quality management system and management responsibility | Every 3 years for class 1 suppliers, every 2 years for class 2, every year for class 3 |
| Process audit | One or more manufacturing processes, from receipt of material to shipment | Every 2 years for class 1, annually for classes 2 and 3 |
| Product audit | Inspection of finished parts against drawings and specification | Annually for safety-relevant parts, every 2 years for others |
| Traceability and records audit | Lot genealogy, retention, retrieval times | Annually for safety-relevant parts, every 2 years for others |
| Launch audit | Readiness for series production | Before the first series delivery of each new part number |
| Special audit | Triggered by an escalation to Level 2 or above, or a significant field event | As needed, within 15 working days of the trigger |

Class 3 suppliers are those with band C or D in the preceding 12 months, a safety-relevant part, or a single-source position. Class 1 is any supplier in band A with no safety-relevant parts. Class 2 covers all others. In the audit year 2023 Aurelia conducted 318 audits across 212 suppliers, an average of 1.5 per supplier.

6.6 Audit conduct and findings

Aurelia gives at least 10 working days notice of system and process audits. Product, traceability and special audits may be announced less than 10 working days before, or not announced. A typical audit team consists of two auditors from the Aurelia supplier quality department, and a system audit requires 2 to 3 days on site.

Findings are rated as follows.

1. Major: a requirement is not met in a way that could lead to defective parts reaching Aurelia, or a record cannot be produced within the retrieval times.
2. Minor: a requirement is met only in part, with no direct risk to the product.
3. Observation: an opportunity for improvement without a requirement being missed.

The supplier receives the audit report within 10 working days of the final meeting. For each major finding the supplier delivers a containment action within 5 working days and a corrective action plan within 20 working days. For each minor finding a plan is due within 30 working days. Aurelia verifies the implementation on site or by remote evidence within 90 days. More than 2 major findings in one audit, or a major finding repeated in two consecutive audits, is counted as an event in the scorecard and may lead to entry at Level 1 of the escalation process in the previous section.
