# DEV-121 HS-410 zinc-nickel minimum 6 um, Galvanica Eporediese

## 1. Request and justification

**1.1 Purpose of the deviation**

The HS-410 is a stamped low-carbon steel shield, 1.2 mm thick, mounted between the exhaust manifold and the brake-fluid reservoir bracket on the Aurelia Strada platform. It does not carry structural load. Its coating has one job: protect the base steel from corrosion over the vehicle life in an under-bonnet environment with road-salt splash and temperatures that cycle up to about 280 °C at the shield surface.

**1.2 What happened at the coating supplier**

Galvanica Eporediese runs a rack line with a zinc-nickel bath of 9,500 litres. During week 38 the automatic nickel replenishment dosing pump on that bath began delivering unevenly. The supplier's laboratory found it when its routine bath analysis showed nickel in the bath drifting between 1.1 and 1.9 g/l against a control band of 1.4 to 1.7 g/l. Plating continued while the pump was being diagnosed, and the supplier shortened plating time on affected racks to keep alloy composition inside the nickel window. A shorter plating time at constant current density gives a thinner layer. The supplier chose to protect alloy composition, which governs corrosion behaviour, over thickness.

The supplier replaced the pump and re-qualified the bath within eleven working days. The six coating batches listed in the traceability section of this document were run during the drift period. Production after re-qualification returned to the 8 to 12 µm range.

**1.3 Supply situation at Valsangone**

Aurelia Automobili calls off HS-410 shields on a weekly schedule. At the time the thin layer was found, the situation was as follows.

| Item | Quantity / value |
|---|---|
| Weekly customer call-off | 2,850 pieces |
| Pieces already shipped to customer in the previous week | 2,850 |
| Finished-goods stock at Valsangone (conforming) | 1,120 pieces |
| Pieces held at Galvanica Eporediese, coated, thin layer | 11,400 pieces |
| Raw stamped blanks waiting for coating | 3,600 pieces |
| Customer line stock cover, as reported by Aurelia | 4.0 working days |
| Customer line consumption | 570 pieces per working day |

The conforming stock of 1,120 pieces covers about two working days of customer consumption. The 11,400 coated pieces cover about twenty working days. If those pieces could not be shipped, Aurelia Automobili would run out of shields in roughly six working days, counting its own stock cover, and the vehicle line fed by this shield would stop. A line stoppage at the customer was costed by Aurelia's logistics team at approximately 38,000 EUR per hour of lost assembly.

**1.4 Alternatives considered**

Several routes were reviewed with the supplier, our quality team and our customer's supplier-quality engineer before the deviation was requested. They are summarised below.

| No. | Alternative | Time to first good parts | Cost impact | Outcome of review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Re-plate the 11,400 pieces at Galvanica Eporediese to add thickness | 9 working days | 0.21 EUR per piece rework, plus stripping risk | Rejected: adhesion risk after stripping and re-plating; second pass could not be controlled on a stamped shape with hems |
| B | Move the work to a second coater with free capacity | 7 to 9 weeks (tooling racks, first-article measurement, line trial) | Higher piece price, 0.34 EUR extra | Rejected as the sole route: far too slow to avoid the stoppage |
| C | Scrap and re-stamp | 4 weeks for blanks plus coating queue | 3.10 EUR per piece, 35,300 EUR total | Rejected: lead time, and blank steel allocation is fixed for the quarter |
| D | Sort by measured thickness and ship only parts at 8 µm or above | Immediate | Sorting labour | Applied for the part of the batch that qualifies, but only 1,620 of 13,020 measured pieces reached 8.0 µm |
| E | Ship the thin-layer parts with additional protection (oil film or sealer) | 3 working days | 0.04 EUR per piece | Considered as a supplement, tested together with the thin layer |
| F | Deviation for the thin layer, with added controls | Immediate | Added testing and sorting cost, about 6,900 EUR | Requested |

Alternative B was started in parallel as a longer-term resilience step and does not depend on the deviation. Alternatives A and C were rejected on technical risk and lead time. Alternative D was used first and reduced the affected population. Alternative E became part of the technical evidence in the risk assessment.

**1.5 Technical justification for requesting a thinner layer**

The request rests on four observations.

1. *Alloy composition is intact.* X-ray fluorescence readings on 5 pieces per rack gave nickel content between 12.8 and 15.1 percent on all six batches. The protective behaviour of zinc-nickel depends strongly on that range, so the layer is a correct alloy, only thinner.
2. *Early corrosion data are favourable.* Valsangone's own neutral salt spray cabinet was loaded with 24 pieces at the minimum measured thickness of 6.0 to 6.4 µm. After 480 hours no white corrosion products were seen on any piece, and the test continued. Our usual internal acceptance point for the standard layer is 240 hours to first white products and 720 hours to red rust.
3. *Part geometry is benign.* The HS-410 has no threads, no press-fit surfaces and no sliding contact. Torque-tension behaviour and friction coefficient, which are sensitive to coating thickness on fasteners, do not apply to this part.
4. *Heat exposure is moderate.* The shield sees a maximum of about 280 °C, which is within the range where zinc-nickel keeps its sacrificial behaviour.

**1.6 Scope boundaries of the request**

The request is limited to the six batches that were plated during the bath drift, a total of 11,400 pieces, plus the 1,620 pieces measured at 8.0 µm or above, which are already inside the drawing range and are listed in the traceability section only for completeness. The request does not extend to any later batch. Every piece shipped under the deviation must be identifiable by lot and by label so that it can be found again if the customer needs it.

**1.7 Additional measures proposed together with the request**

- 100 percent XRF screening on a 2-point reading of every container, with pieces under 6.0 µm separated and held back.
- Extension of the salt spray test on retained samples to 720 hours.
- A heat-ageing exposure of 48 hours at 300 °C followed by a salt spray run on 12 pieces.
- Marking of the container label with the deviation number so that the customer can recognise the parts on receipt.
- Corrective action at Galvanica Eporediese: a flow meter with alarm on the nickel dosing line and a hold on any rack where bath nickel leaves the control band.

History. This deviation follows DEV-109 (HS-410, zinc-nickel at least 7 um, valid until 2025-12-31). Status of DEV-109: expired.

## 2. Affected product and lots

**2.1 Product description**

The affected product is the HS-410 heat shield, a stamped part made from 1.2 mm cold-rolled low-carbon steel strip. Each piece weighs 186 g, measures 214 mm by 162 mm, and has two hemmed edges and four 6.5 mm mounting holes. After stamping and degreasing at Valsangone, the parts are racked in groups of 24 and sent to Galvanica Eporediese for zinc-nickel plating and a clear sealing passivation. They return in reusable plastic containers holding 120 pieces each, and are inspected on receipt before packing for Aurelia Automobili.

| Characteristic | Drawing requirement | Value on affected lots |
|---|---|---|
| Zinc-nickel layer thickness | 8 to 12 µm | 6.0 to 7.9 µm |
| Nickel content in alloy | 12 to 16 % | 12.8 to 15.1 % |
| Appearance | Uniform silver-grey, no blistering | Conforms |
| Adhesion (bend and tape test) | No flaking | Conforms |
| Sealing passivation | Present, clear | Present |

Only one characteristic, layer thickness, deviates. The other coating attributes, together with all dimensional features, were measured on the lots and are inside requirements.

**2.2 Identification system used for traceability**

Three identifiers connect a finished shield to its history.

1. *Valsangone lot number* (format VC-HS410-26xxx). One lot is a stamping run of one coil on one press shift. It ties the part to the steel coil, press tool and operator.
2. *Coating batch number at Galvanica Eporediese* (format GE-B-77xx). It ties the part to the plating bath record, rack positions, current, plating time and the bath analysis taken before and after the batch.
3. *Container label serial.* Each container of 120 pieces receives a unique serial on receipt. The serial range is recorded against the lot and batch so that a customer can trace a single container back to both.

The parts themselves are marked by a laser code on the flat tab, giving the press shift and the date code. This mark allows identification of an individual shield even after it is removed from its container.

Measurement repeatability, determined on 30 readings of the same spot, was a standard deviation of 0.15 µm. Edge readings were on average 0.4 µm lower than face readings, and the minimum values in the table above come mostly from edge locations.

These are the samples for the 720-hour salt spray extension and the heat-ageing test described in the risk assessment. A further 12 pieces per lot were sectioned for cross-section microscopy, which confirmed the XRF thickness within ±0.3 µm and showed a dense, continuous layer with no porosity reaching the base steel.

## 3. Risk assessment

**3.1 Method and scoring scale**

The risk assessment follows the failure mode and effects analysis method used at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A. Each failure mode is scored for severity (S), occurrence (O) and detection (D) on a scale of 1 to 10, and the risk priority number is RPN = S × O × D. Our internal action threshold is an RPN of 100 or any severity of 9 or 10 regardless of the product. The scores below are for the 11,400 pieces covered by this deviation, based on the thickness data, the salt spray tests to date, and the cross-section results.

The team consisted of the quality engineer, the process engineer for coatings, the laboratory supervisor, a representative from logistics, and the supplier-quality engineer of Aurelia Automobili who took part by video call.

**3.2 Function and requirements of the part**

The shield's function is thermal and splash protection of adjacent components. Its coating protects the steel against corrosion for the vehicle life, which Aurelia Automobili defines as 12 years in a salt-road climate. A corroded shield would not cause the vehicle to stop, but rust could stain nearby components, create flaking particles, or in the extreme lead to a rattle noise or a perforated shield that no longer shields. Corrosion is therefore a durability and customer-satisfaction risk, not a direct safety risk.

**3.3 Evidence gathered**

| Test | Samples | Result to date |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral salt spray, thin layer 6.0 to 6.4 µm | 24 pieces | No white products at 480 h; test running to 720 h |
| Neutral salt spray, thin layer 7.0 to 7.9 µm | 24 pieces | No white products at 480 h |
| Neutral salt spray, reference layer 9 to 11 µm | 12 pieces | No white products at 480 h |
| Cyclic corrosion test, 30 cycles | 12 pieces | Slight white products at edges from cycle 22 on the 6.0 µm pieces; none at 7 µm and above |
| Bend and tape adhesion | 60 pieces | No flaking |
| Cross-section microscopy | 12 pieces | Continuous layer, no porosity reaching steel |
| Heat ageing 48 h at 300 °C, then salt spray 240 h | 12 pieces | No red rust; white products on 2 of 12 pieces at the edge |

The cyclic test gave the first sign of weakness: the thinnest pieces start to show sacrificial products at edges after 22 cycles, which is later than the minimum expected life but earlier than the reference layer. This was taken into account in the scoring.

**3.4 Failure modes, effects and scores**

| No. | Failure mode | Effect | Cause | S | O | D | RPN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Early white corrosion at hemmed edges | Cosmetic stain, customer complaint | Thin layer at edge, minimum 6.0 µm | 4 | 4 | 3 | 48 |
| 2 | Red rust on shield within vehicle life | Rust staining on adjacent bracket | Layer consumed by sacrificial action | 6 | 3 | 4 | 72 |
| 3 | Coating flaking or poor adhesion | Particles, exposed steel | Substrate preparation fault during the drift period | 6 | 2 | 3 | 36 |
| 4 | Pieces below 6.0 µm shipped unnoticed | Higher corrosion risk on those pieces | Measurement escape, sampling error | 6 | 3 | 3 | 54 |
| 5 | Mix-up of deviation pieces with standard stock | Parts not recognised, no traceability | Labelling error | 5 | 2 | 3 | 30 |
| 6 | Layer lost at mounting-hole edges during assembly | Local exposed steel | Bolt flange contact, scraping | 4 | 3 | 5 | 60 |
| 7 | Loss of heat-shield function through perforation | Heat exposure of adjacent parts | Long-term corrosion through 1.2 mm steel | 8 | 1 | 5 | 40 |
| 8 | Alloy composition off range | Reduced corrosion protection | Bath nickel drift | 6 | 2 | 2 | 24 |

No failure mode exceeds the action threshold of RPN 100. No severity score of 9 or 10 was assigned, because the part has no safety function and failure would be gradual and visible.

**3.5 Rationale for key scores**

*Failure mode 1 and 2 (corrosion).* The occurrence score of 4 for early white corrosion reflects the cyclic test result. White products at an edge are the normal sign of zinc-nickel doing its job, but a customer may view them as a defect. Red rust, which would mean the layer is consumed, was given occurrence 3 because it did not appear in any sample including the heat-aged ones. Detection is 3 to 4 because the customer inspects the part on arrival and in the field, but corrosion appears late, after delivery.

*Failure mode 4 (thickness escape).* Detection was scored 3 given the 100 percent XRF screening with two readings per piece and a repeatability of 0.15 µm. An escape would require two consecutive wrong readings or an unrecognised calibration drift. The shift start calibration check against the 5.0 and 8.0 µm foils reduces this.

*Failure mode 6 (assembly scraping).* The customer's mounting uses a flanged bolt with a washer face that contacts the shield around each hole. A thinner layer has less margin if the flange scrapes. Detection was scored 5, because assembly damage would not be seen by Valsangone. A line test at Aurelia Automobili on 30 shields with the 6.0 µm layer is proposed to reduce the uncertainty.

**3.6 Residual risk with controls**

The scores above already assume the controls listed in the request. The following table shows the effect on the highest-scoring items if those controls were not in place.

| Failure mode | RPN with controls | RPN without 100 % XRF screening and container marking |
|---|---|---|
| 2. Red rust within vehicle life | 72 | 108 |
| 4. Pieces below 6.0 µm shipped | 54 | 162 |
| 6. Assembly scraping | 60 | 60 |
| 5. Mix-up with standard stock | 30 | 90 |

Without the screening and the marking, two failure modes would exceed the action threshold. With them, all are below it.

**3.7 Open technical points**

- The 720-hour salt spray result on the thinnest pieces was not yet complete when this assessment was written. A result of red rust before 720 hours would raise the occurrence of failure mode 2 from 3 to 5 and change the RPN to 120.
- The cyclic corrosion data come from 12 pieces. The sample is small, so the team applied a margin and scored occurrence conservatively.
- The measured edge readings are on average 0.4 µm below the face readings. The minimum of 6.0 µm was used as the worst case for all pieces in the scoring.

**3.8 Conclusion**

The layer is a correct zinc-nickel alloy, continuous in cross-section and well adhered. The thickness is 0.1 to 2.0 µm below the drawing minimum. Test evidence to date shows protection above the internal acceptance level for the first white products and no red rust on any sample. The part has no safety function, no threaded or sliding surface, and a mild thermal environment. The highest risk priority number is 72, which is below the action threshold of 100, with all identified controls in place. The residual risk for the 11,400 pieces is therefore assessed as low to moderate and limited to durability and appearance, and it can be managed by the screening, marking, extended testing and customer line trial described in this document.

## 4. Corrosion test plan

**1.1 Purpose and scope**

This section defines the corrosion test programme for HS-410 heat shields that carry a zinc-nickel layer thinner than the normal specification. The shields are produced at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A., coated by Galvanica Eporediese, and supplied to Aurelia Automobili. The programme is meant to show, with measured data, that the thinner layer still gives the corrosion protection the application needs. It covers neutral salt spray testing per ISO 9227, the number of samples, the points in the process at which they are taken, the acceptance criteria, and the way results are recorded.

The normal layer on HS-410 is 8 to 12 µm of zinc-nickel with a nickel content of 12 to 16 % by mass, followed by a transparent passivation. Under the deviation the layer is 5.0 to 7.0 µm, with the same nickel content range and the same passivation. Nothing else in the coating line is changed. The test plan therefore isolates thickness as the single variable.

**1.2 Test laboratory**

All salt spray testing is done by Laboratorio Prove Corrosione Canavese S.r.l., an independent test house in the Ivrea area. The laboratory holds accreditation for neutral salt spray testing and for coating thickness measurement. Valsangone selected it because it has no commercial link to the coating supplier, so the results are independent of Galvanica Eporediese.

The laboratory runs two chambers for this work, each with a usable volume of 1,000 litres. One chamber is assigned to the HS-410 programme for the full duration of a test run so that the conditions are not disturbed by other customers' parts. Valsangone's quality engineer visits the laboratory at the start of each run to witness specimen placement and chamber set-up.

**1.3 Test method and chamber conditions**

The test is a neutral salt spray test (NSS) carried out in line with ISO 9227. The chamber conditions are fixed for every run and are checked and logged by the laboratory.

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Test solution | Sodium chloride in distilled or deionised water, 50 g/l ± 5 g/l |
| Solution pH at collection | 6.5 to 7.2 |
| Chamber temperature | 35 °C ± 2 °C |
| Conductivity of water used | Not more than 20 µS/cm at 25 °C |
| Fallout rate | 1.5 ml/h ± 0.5 ml/h per 80 cm² horizontal collection area |
| Number of collectors | Minimum 2 per chamber, read every 24 h |
| Specimen angle | 20° ± 5° from vertical, significant surface facing upward |
| Spray pressure at nozzle | 0.07 to 0.17 MPa as set by the laboratory |
| Door openings | Only at the scheduled inspection times |

The laboratory also places three reference steel coupons in each run, as required by the standard, to confirm that the chamber is producing the expected corrosion rate. A run in which the reference coupons fall outside the standard's mass-loss range is repeated at Valsangone's cost only if the laboratory is at fault; otherwise the cost follows the normal test price.

**1.4 Sample sizes and sampling points**

The plan uses three kinds of specimen: whole HS-410 parts, flat test panels coated in the same bath load, and cut sections for thickness verification. Whole parts are the primary evidence because the geometry of the heat shield, with its embossed ribs and flanged edges, produces thickness variation that flat panels do not show.

| Test group | Specimen type | Quantity per run | Thickness target | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A – Lower limit | Whole parts | 6 | 5.0 to 5.5 µm | Worst case at the thinnest accepted layer |
| B – Typical deviated | Whole parts | 6 | 5.8 to 6.5 µm | Expected production condition |
| C – Upper deviated | Whole parts | 3 | 6.6 to 7.0 µm | Upper boundary of the deviated range |
| D – Normal reference | Whole parts | 3 | 8.0 to 12.0 µm | Comparison with the standard coating |
| E – Panels | Flat steel panels 100 × 150 mm | 6 | 5.0 to 7.0 µm | Bath load monitoring |
| F – Section checks | Whole parts cut after test | 3 | Mixed | Confirm thickness at corroded spots |

A full run therefore uses 27 pieces. Groups A to C are selected by the laboratory from a pool of 60 parts that have been measured for thickness by the supplier and re-measured at Valsangone. The thicknesses are marked on a record, not on the parts, so the laboratory reads them only during the evaluation.

Three runs are planned: an initial qualification run on parts from three separate coating batches, a confirmation run using parts from a later production week, and a periodic run for each calendar quarter in which deviated parts are produced. Each run draws its parts from a different set of coating batches than the run before it, so the same batch is never the only evidence.

**1.5 Specimen preparation and placement**

Parts are taken from the supplier's output after passivation and after the standard post-coating drying period of 24 hours. They are not cleaned, polished or touched with bare hands before testing. Valsangone ships them to the laboratory in individual sleeves with the batch number on the sleeve.

On arrival the laboratory checks each part for visible damage, photographs it, and fixes it to the rack with plastic-coated hooks or inert thread so that the part does not touch another part or the rack's metal. Cut edges and the contact points of the hooks are noted in the record. Parts hang with the outer face of the shield, the face seen from the road, tilted at the required angle. The inner face, which sits against the vehicle underbody, is tested in a second set of parts reserved for group A only, so the total of group A is 6 outer-face and 3 inner-face positions on the same rack, counted within the six parts by rotating them at the half-way point is not permitted; the laboratory instead uses six distinct parts, three in each orientation.

Parts are spaced at least 20 mm apart and arranged so that drip from one part does not fall on another.

**1.6 Inspection intervals**

The laboratory inspects the specimens at fixed times. At each inspection the rack stays closed in the chamber for no longer than 10 minutes, and parts are not rinsed or wiped.

| Inspection hour | Action |
|---|---|
| 24 h | Photograph all specimens; record any early corrosion |
| 96 h | Photograph; record |
| 120 h | Remove group E panels for white corrosion assessment |
| 240 h | Photograph; formal white corrosion reading of groups A to D |
| 480 h | Photograph; record |
| 720 h | Formal red rust reading; remove groups A to D for rinsing and drying |
| 960 h | Only for groups that are continued to the extended run, as set in 1.7 |

After 720 hours, parts are rinsed in running water at under 40 °C, dried with clean compressed air and examined under daylight-equivalent lighting at 1,000 lux. Corroded area is estimated with a transparent grid with 2 mm squares.

**1.7 Acceptance criteria**

The criteria are set separately for the first appearance of white corrosion products on the zinc-nickel layer and for the first appearance of corrosion of the steel base, which shows as red rust.

| Criterion | Group A to C (deviated) | Group D (normal reference) |
|---|---|---|
| White corrosion, 240 h | Not more than 5 % of the significant surface per part | Not more than 5 % |
| Red rust, 720 h | None on any part | None on any part |
| Red rust at edges, 720 h | Isolated spots under 1 mm², maximum 3 per part, only within 3 mm of a cut edge | Same |
| Mean white corrosion area, 480 h | Not more than 15 % across a group | Not more than 10 % |
| Reference coupons | Within the standard's range | Within the standard's range |

A test run is accepted when all parts in groups A, B and C meet the criteria above. The extended run to 960 hours is only used for information: groups A and D continue to 960 hours so that the margin between the deviated layer and the normal layer is documented. No acceptance limit applies to the extended hours, but red rust appearing in group A between 720 and 960 hours is recorded and discussed in the quality review.

**1.8 Handling of failures**

If a single part in group B or C shows red rust before 720 hours, the laboratory cuts the part, measures the layer at the spot of corrosion, and reports the local thickness. The quality engineer then decides, using the local thickness, whether the failure comes from a coating defect such as a pinhole or a poor-quality area, or from the thin layer itself. A failure from a defect is repeated with six additional parts from the same batch. A failure from the thin layer stops further shipment of parts with that thickness class until the cause is understood.

If two or more parts in group A fail, the lower thickness limit of 5.0 µm is considered unsuitable and the supplier's minimum thickness set point is raised by 0.5 µm until a further run is completed.

**1.9 Records and reporting**

The laboratory delivers a test record within 10 working days of the end of each run. It contains chamber logs, collector readings, reference coupon results, the photographs, the thickness readings, and the evaluation sheet for each part. Valsangone keeps the records together with the coating batch data for the length of the product's supply period and for at least 15 years after the last delivery.

## 5. Additional inspection

**2.1 Purpose and scope**

The thinner zinc-nickel layer leaves less margin for coating variation. For this reason, HS-410 shields coated under the deviation receive extra inspection beyond the normal incoming and in-process checks. This section sets out the inspection plan, the gauges and instruments, and the recording sheets used. The extra inspection happens at two places: Galvanica Eporediese before dispatch, and Valsangone's incoming inspection area.

The extra inspection is meant to do three things: confirm that every coating batch is within the 5.0 to 7.0 µm window, find any local areas that are thinner than the window, and confirm that the layer, nickel content and adhesion are otherwise the same as on normal parts.

**2.2 Inspection plan**

The plan is organised by batch. A batch is the output of one rack loading sequence from one bath, with a maximum size of 1,200 parts. Samples are taken at random from at least three containers of the batch.

| No. | Characteristic | Location | Sample per batch | Frequency | Method |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Layer thickness, 5 points per part | Galvanica | 20 parts | Every batch | X-ray fluorescence |
| 2 | Layer thickness, 5 points per part | Valsangone | 13 parts | Every batch | X-ray fluorescence |
| 3 | Nickel content in the layer | Galvanica | 3 parts | Every batch | X-ray fluorescence |
| 4 | Appearance of the surface | Galvanica | 100 % of the sample of 80 parts | Every batch | Visual |
| 5 | Appearance of the surface | Valsangone | 50 parts | Every batch | Visual |
| 6 | Adhesion | Galvanica | 3 parts | Every batch | Bend test and tape test |
| 7 | Thickness section check | Independent lab | 2 parts | One batch per month | Cross-section microscopy |
| 8 | Salt spray witness panels | Laboratory | 6 panels | Per run, see test plan | Salt spray |

If the sample from a batch of more than 600 parts is measured and one thickness reading falls outside the window, the sample is doubled. If a second reading falls outside, the whole batch is held, as described in 2.8.

**2.3 Gauges and instruments**

All thickness measurement uses X-ray fluorescence because it is non-destructive and can measure the zinc-nickel layer on a curved embossed surface. The instruments are calibrated with certified standards that bracket the deviated window.

| Instrument | Range | Resolution | Collimator | Used at | Check frequency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Benchtop X-ray fluorescence unit, type FX-2 | 0 to 30 µm | 0.01 µm | 0.3 mm round | Galvanica | Start of every shift |
| Benchtop X-ray fluorescence unit, type FX-2 | 0 to 30 µm | 0.01 µm | 0.3 mm round | Valsangone | Start of every shift |
| Reference foil set | 3.0, 5.0, 7.0, 10.0 µm | ± 0.1 µm | n/a | Both sites | Used at each start-up check |

The two X-ray fluorescence units are compared once a month on a set of ten parts that are measured at both locations. The difference between the two mean values must be no more than 0.3 µm. If it is greater, the unit with the older calibration is checked and re-set before further measurements.

**2.4 Thickness measurement procedure**

Thickness is measured at five fixed points on each part. Point 1 is the centre of the flat area of the outer face. Points 2 and 3 are on the top of the two main ribs. Point 4 is in the root of the largest embossing. Point 5 is on the flange within 10 mm of the edge. The points are shown on a sketch printed on the recording sheet and on a plastic template that is held against the part so that operators measure at the same places each time.

For each part, the operator records five readings, each a mean of a 15-second measurement. The part is accepted when the mean of its five readings is between 5.5 and 7.0 µm and no single reading is below 5.0 µm. A batch is accepted when all sampled parts meet this rule and when the batch mean is between 5.8 and 6.6 µm. The batch standard deviation across all readings should not exceed 0.6 µm; a higher value triggers a review of rack loading at the supplier.

The flange point and the rib root are the places where the layer is most likely to be thin. If the Valsangone readings at these two points are on average more than 0.8 µm lower than at the flat area, the supplier is asked to adjust the rack position or the anode arrangement before the next batch.

**2.5 Visual inspection**

Visual inspection is done under the lighting booth at a distance of 400 mm. The operator looks for uncoated spots, burns on edges, blistering, drip marks, passivation colour variation and white deposits. The accepted colour is the normal transparent to slightly iridescent finish. Because a thin layer can look lighter, the operator compares the part to a boundary sample kept in the booth. Parts that are visibly different from the boundary sample are set aside and measured at five points. Visual defects are counted by type on the recording sheet, with a limit of 2 % of the sample before the batch is held.

**2.6 Adhesion test**

At Galvanica, three parts per batch are bent over a 5 mm mandrel through 90°, and the bent area is examined at ×10 magnification for flaking. A tape test is then done at the flange: the tape is pressed on for 10 seconds, pulled at about 90°, and checked for removed coating. Any flaking or removal makes the result a fail, and the batch is held.

**2.7 Sampling at Valsangone**

When a deviated batch arrives, the incoming inspector confirms the batch number against the delivery note, checks that all containers carry the deviation label described in the labelling section, and takes 13 parts using a random-number sheet for container selection and position within the container. Thickness and visual checks are then made. A deviated batch is not moved to the production buffer until the thickness result is recorded and the batch result is accepted on the sheet.

**2.8 Findings outside the limits**

| Finding | Immediate action | Follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| Single part mean under 5.5 µm | Part set aside, sample doubled | Rack position checked |
| Two parts under limits in the doubled sample | Whole batch held, 100 % thickness sort proposed | Supplier cause analysis within 3 working days |
| Batch mean above 7.0 µm | Batch handled as normal-thickness lot only if it meets the normal range, otherwise held | Bath settings reviewed |
| Adhesion failure | Batch held | Pre-treatment check at supplier |
| Instrument check outside tolerance | Measurements since last good check repeated | Instrument re-calibrated |

There are three sheets. The thickness sheet has one row per part and five columns for the readings, a computed part mean, and an accept/reject box. The visual sheet has defect types in columns and the sample count in rows. The batch summary sheet gathers the thickness mean, standard deviation, number held, and signatures of the inspector and the quality engineer.

## 6. Labelling and segregation

**3.1 Principle**

Parts coated with the thinner zinc-nickel layer must never be mixed with parts of normal thickness, and must be identifiable at every step from the coating line to the delivery to Aurelia Automobili. The method rests on three measures: a distinct label on every container, a physically separate storage place at each site, and a recorded batch trace that links each delivered part to its coating batch and measurement data. The method is simple on purpose, so that an operator who has not read this document can still recognise deviated parts from the label colour alone.

**3.2 Label design**

Every container, whether a returnable crate, a cardboard box or a bin, carries a deviation label in addition to the normal product label. The label is made of self-adhesive paper in a bright orange that is not used for any other purpose in the plant, and is printed on the Valsangone label printer.

| Field | Content | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Header | "DEVIATED PART – THIN ZN-NI LAYER" | 16 mm bold |
| Part number | HS-410 | 14 mm |
| Coating batch | Galvanica batch number, 8 characters | 14 mm |
| Thickness range | 5.0 – 7.0 µm | 12 mm |
| Measured batch mean | From the thickness sheet, in µm | 12 mm |
| Quantity | Parts in the container | 12 mm |
| Barcode | Code 128 with batch and container number | 40 × 12 mm |
| Label size | A5, 148 × 210 mm | – |

Two labels are put on each container, one on the long side and one on the short side, so that the label is visible from any approach angle by a forklift driver. The label on the lid of a box is a third copy used when containers are stacked. Labels are applied by Galvanica at the end of the coating line, after the batch thickness result is entered, and the label stays on until the container is empty.

If a label is damaged or unreadable, the container is moved to the hold area and a new label is printed by Valsangone against the batch trace. A container is never relabelled on the basis of memory.

**3.3 Marking of individual parts**

HS-410 shields are not marked individually in the coating layer, because any mark would be removed or covered by the coating process. Instead, each part carries the pressed stamp that is already made at Valsangone during forming. This stamp contains the week code and the die number. The shield's coating batch is linked to the week code and die number by the batch trace in 3.9. This means that a single part removed from its container can still be assigned to a forming week, and from there to the coating batches processed that week.

In addition, the first and the last part of each container are marked with a small orange adhesive dot, 8 mm in diameter, on the non-visible side of the flange. The dot is removed at the assembly station when the part is placed in the fixture.

**3.4 Physical segregation at each site**

The segregation uses dedicated areas that are marked on the floor and shown on the layout boards.

| Site | Area | Marking | Capacity | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Galvanica Eporediese | Output rack of the coating line, dedicated position 4 | Orange floor frame 1.2 × 1.6 m | 6 crates | Line operators |
| Galvanica Eporediese | Dispatch bay D2 | Orange fence with floor sign | 24 crates | Dispatch staff |
| Valsangone | Incoming inspection bench 3 | Orange-edged table | 2 crates | Inspectors |
| Valsangone | Warehouse zone W-DEV, rows 1 and 2 | Orange floor lines and hanging sign | 48 crates | Warehouse staff |
| Valsangone | Line-side buffer at press cell 6 | Orange-edged shelf | 4 crates | Material handler |
| Valsangone | Hold cage | Wire cage with padlock | 10 crates | Quality only |

Normal-thickness HS-410 parts are never stored within 2 metres of the deviated area unless a physical barrier separates the two. Where the layout of the warehouse prevents the 2-metre distance, a rack frame with a solid divider is used.

**3.5 Material flow at the coating supplier**

At Galvanica, deviated production is run as a separate campaign, not alternated with normal production in the same shift. A campaign starts with the bath set to the shorter plating time and ends with a changeover check: the last rack of the earlier campaign is measured, the rack carriers are emptied, and the output position is cleared and inspected by the shift leader before the first rack of the new campaign is unloaded. The shift leader signs the changeover entry on the batch summary sheet.

Crates used for deviated parts are marked with an orange stripe on the frame. These crates are used only for deviated parts. When they are emptied at Valsangone, they go back to Galvanica in a separate return load.

**3.6 Receipt at Valsangone**

At goods-in, the receiving clerk checks the delivery note, the orange labels, and the number of crates. Crates with an orange stripe that carry no orange label, or orange-labelled crates without an orange stripe, are sent directly to the hold cage. After the extra inspection, the inspector places a green "checked" sticker next to the label. Only crates with this sticker may move from the incoming bench to the zone W-DEV.

The warehouse system holds deviated parts under a separate stock item code that is distinct from the normal HS-410 code. The system does not allow a transfer from the deviated code to the normal code, and a booking to the production order of a normal batch for the deviated code is blocked.

**3.7 Storage and stock rotation**

Deviated parts are stored in zone W-DEV by batch in the order of receipt, with the oldest batch nearest the aisle. Parts are issued in first-in, first-out order. A crate is not opened in the warehouse. A partly used crate returning from the line is closed with a strap, re-labelled with the remaining quantity, and returned to the front of its row. The warehouse supervisor counts the crates in W-DEV once a week and compares the count to the system stock, with a permitted difference of zero crates.

**3.8 Use in production**

Production orders for deviated parts are issued with their own order type, and the picking list shows the orange colour. At press cell 6 and at the assembly station, the material handler brings only the quantity needed for one shift. At the beginning of each shift the cell leader checks that the buffer shelf holds only orange-labelled crates when a deviated order is running, and the check is entered on the line check sheet.

**3.9 Packing, shipment and traceability**

Finished parts are packed in boxes of 40 shields, and each box and pallet receives the orange deviation label together with the standard shipping label for Aurelia Automobili. Pallets of deviated parts are placed on their own position in the loading bay and are never mixed on a pallet with normal parts. The delivery note lists the coating batch numbers and the thickness range.

The batch trace is an electronic table that links the forming week and die number, the coating batch, the thickness results, the salt spray run in which the batch was represented, the pallet number and the delivery note. A trace from any delivered pallet back to its coating batch can be completed in under 30 minutes, which is tested by the quality engineer with two sample trace exercises each quarter.

**3.10 Checks on segregation and mix-ups**

| Check | Who | Frequency | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Floor-area walk, all deviated zones | Quality engineer | Weekly | No normal parts in orange areas, no orange crates outside |
| Stock count in W-DEV | Warehouse supervisor | Weekly | Zero difference |
| Label legibility and barcode scan | Warehouse clerk | At each movement | Scan reads on first try |
| Trace exercise | Quality engineer | Quarterly | Under 30 minutes |
| Changeover check at Galvanica | Shift leader | Each campaign change | Output position empty |

If parts of unclear identity are found, the crate is moved to the hold cage and all crates of the same row are examined. The parts are measured at five points by X-ray fluorescence, with 20 parts per crate. Crates are returned to the normal flow only when every measured part is within the normal thickness range of 8.0 to 12.0 µm. Parts below that range are treated as deviated and relabelled in the hold cage under the supervision of the quality engineer.

## 8. Exit plan

**2.1 Objective**

The objective of the exit plan is to return HS-410 to the nominal ZnNi layer of 8 to 12 µm in all five measuring zones, with zone R3 as the critical zone. The root cause is that the current rack holds the part with its recess facing a contact bar. The bar shields the recess from current, so the layer there grows more slowly than on the outer face. The plan changes the rack, not the plating chemistry, so that the process window agreed with Galvanica Eporediese stays unchanged. All trials run in production-size tanks on the actual line.

**2.2 Acceptance criteria for a trial**

A rack variant counts as successful when it meets all of the following on a trial lot:

1. Zone R3 mean of at least 9.0 µm, and no single reading below 8.0 µm, over 30 measured parts (150 readings).
2. All other zones within 8 to 12 µm.
3. Process capability index (Cpk) of at least 1.33 on zone R3, calculated from the 150 readings.
4. Rack load of at least 40 parts per rack, so that the plating capacity does not fall by more than 10 %.
5. No visible contact marks within 3 mm of a functional edge, and rack mark position the same on every part.
6. Salt spray test on 10 parts reaching at least 720 hours without red corrosion.

**2.3 Rack variants**

Ilaria Moretti and Galvanica's Giorgio Albanese defined three first-generation variants. The reasoning was to open the recess to the field lines, to reduce the shielding by the bar, and to move the rack contact to a place that does not matter functionally.

| Variant | Design change | Parts per rack | Expected effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Contact bar moved 18 mm outward; parts tilted 12° | 44 | Small gain in R3 |
| B | Parts hung by the mounting tab with a spring clip; recess faces anode side | 40 | Large gain in R3, one clip mark on tab |
| C | Auxiliary anode (conforming shield) fitted behind the contact bar | 36 | Large gain in R3, more rack complexity |

Variant B and variant C each have a second-generation version, named B-rev and C-mod, which came from the first trial results.

**2.4 Trial results**

Each trial used 150 parts per variant, measured on 30 parts. Totals: 1,200 trial parts in all four trials.

| Trial | Variant | Parts | R3 mean (µm) | R3 min (µm) | R3 Cpk | Load per rack | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | A | 150 | 6.9 | 5.4 | 0.31 | 44 | Gain too small |
| T1 | B | 150 | 9.3 | 7.4 | 1.02 | 40 | Mean met, minimum not met |
| T1 | C | 150 | 10.1 | 8.6 | 1.58 | 36 | Criteria met except load |
| T2 | B (clip lengthened 6 mm) | 150 | 9.6 | 7.9 | 1.17 | 40 | Minimum just below limit |
| T2 | C-mod (shield simplified, 38 per rack) | 150 | 9.9 | 8.4 | 1.45 | 38 | Load still short |
| T3 | B-rev (clip moved, tilt 8°) | 300 | 9.8 | 8.3 | 1.41 | 40 | Criteria met on thickness |
| T4 | B-rev with revised dwell time of 2 minutes more | 150 | 10.0 | 8.7 | 1.52 | 40 | All criteria met |

Variant A was dropped after T1 because the tilt gave only a 0.8 µm gain. The trials showed that the largest effect on R3 came from facing the recess to the anode side. The tilt angle mattered less. The dwell-time change in T4 is an adjustment inside the existing process window and does not change the bath composition.

**2.5 Observations from the trials**

1. In T1, variant B left a clip mark of 2.5 mm diameter on the mounting tab. Aurelia's drawing allows contact marks on that tab, and the customer had confirmed the tab as an acceptable rack position in the second meeting.
2. Rack C, with its auxiliary anode, reached the best thickness but its load of 36 parts per rack would reduce plating throughput by 18 %, which is why it was not taken forward as the main route. It remains a fallback.
3. The spring clip of B wore after about 60 cycles in T3. Galvanica proposed a replacement interval of 40 cycles. This adds 1.5 minutes of rack maintenance per rack set.
4. Cross-sections on the T4 parts showed uniform layers of 9.4 to 10.6 µm in R3 with no change in the layer structure.

**2.6 Milestones**

Week 0 is the week of the first trial (T1). The weeks below are counted from that point.

| Milestone | Description | Target week | Success measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | T1 complete, variants A, B, C measured | 1 | Data for 450 parts |
| M2 | Variant choice and second-generation design | 3 | B-rev and C-mod drawn |
| M3 | T2 and T3 complete | 7 | Cpk of at least 1.33 on thickness |
| M4 | T4 complete with dwell-time adjustment | 9 | All six criteria met |
| M5 | Salt spray test of 10 T4 parts reaches 720 hours | 14 | No red corrosion |
| M6 | Three consecutive production batches on B-rev racks (7,920 parts) | 18 | R3 mean at least 9.0 µm, no reading below 8.0 µm |

## 10. Monitoring log

1. Scope and basis of the log

1.1 The log covers coated lots received in calendar weeks 28 to 40. Entries are grouped by the week in which the lot was received. Each entry shows the layer thickness measured on receipt, the result at the first inspection point of 240 hours, and the result at the final inspection point of 720 hours.

1.2 The acceptance criteria used throughout the log are the following. At 240 hours, no white corrosion products on any significant surface of the sampled parts. At 720 hours, no base metal corrosion on any significant surface. Edges within 2 mm of a cut or pierced edge are assessed separately and are not counted as a significant surface, in line with the drawing note.

1.3 The thinner layer has a nominal thickness of 8 µm, with a minimum individual reading of 6 µm and a maximum of 11 µm. The earlier standard layer was 12 µm nominal. The log therefore shows the thickness readings next to every salt spray result so that any trend can be seen early.

1.4 Each lot is sampled at the rate of 5 parts for thickness and 6 parts for salt spray: 3 parts are removed at 240 hours for inspection and 3 remain until 720 hours. Lot sizes range from 1,800 to 2,400 pieces, which corresponds to one to two coating racks batches per lot.

3. Summary of the monitoring period

3.1 A total of 26 lots, comprising 54,700 pieces, were received and sampled in the thirteen weeks covered. All 26 lots met the acceptance criteria at both 240 hours and 720 hours. No lot showed white corrosion products at 240 hours, and no sampled part showed base metal corrosion at 720 hours.

3.2 The lowest individual thickness reading across the period was 6.5 µm, on lot GE-3808-B in week 38. This is above the 6 µm minimum. The highest individual reading was 10.2 µm, on lot GE-3505-A in week 35, which is below the 11 µm maximum.

3.3 The mean of the lot means was 8.38 µm, with a standard deviation of 0.22 µm. The spread is narrow, and it matches the capability study that Galvanica Eporediese ran on its line before the first production lot.

3.4 No sampled part showed any visible change at the edges beyond the normal slight darkening of the layer that is seen on cut edges. This was recorded by the laboratory technician on the inspection sheet for each lot.

4. Observations and trends

4.1 The A lots, which are coated on the first shift, have a mean thickness of 8.49 µm. The B lots, coated on the second shift, have a mean of 8.27 µm. The difference of 0.22 µm is small and is within the expected variation of the rack positions, but it is tracked each week.

4.2 The salt spray chamber was checked each week with reference panels. Its temperature stayed between 34.6 °C and 35.4 °C, and the collected solution rate stayed between 1.1 ml and 1.8 ml per hour per 80 cm², which is inside the limits set for the chamber. No week was lost to a chamber fault.

4.3 The 720 hour inspections take thirty days to complete. For that reason a lot received in week 40 was read for the final time in week 44, and the 720 hour column was completed only when that reading was recorded. The table above shows the final values only.

4.4 The laboratory also keeps one retained part from each lot for 12 months. These parts are stored in the laboratory cabinet, labelled with the lot code and receipt date, and can be re-measured on request.

5. Responsibilities for the log

5.1 The incoming inspection technician enters the thickness readings and the salt spray readings on the same day as each reading is taken.

5.2 The laboratory supervisor checks the entries each Friday and initials the weekly page.

5.3 The quality engineer reviews the full log every four weeks and compares thickness and salt spray results against the supplier's own certificates for the same lots. Over the period the supplier thickness values and the Valsangone readings differed by no more than 0.4 µm on any lot.

5.4 The log is kept in the quality system folder for the HS-410 programme and is available to the production planner and the process engineer.
