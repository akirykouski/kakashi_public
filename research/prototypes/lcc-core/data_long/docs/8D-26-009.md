# 8D-26-009 Porosity in HB-620 mount bores, Fonderia Dora

## 1. D0 Symptom and emergency response

## 0.1 Purpose of this section

This section records how the porosity in the mount bores of the HB-620 gearbox mount housing was first noticed at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A., who was told and when, and what was done in the first 72 hours. The housings are die cast in AlSi9Cu3 by Fonderia Dora and machined at the Valsangone plant in Bergamo. The times below are written as hours after first notice (T+0). The clock time of first notice is given once in 0.3.

## 0.2 Part and process background

The HB-620 housing is a gearbox mount housing with two mount bores, called bore A (nominal 42.000 mm, H7) and bore B (nominal 28.000 mm, H7). Both bores are finish-bored on machining line 3 in one clamping. A steel bushing is pressed into each bore at the final assembly cell. The bore walls carry the bushing press fit and the torque reaction of the gearbox, so the drawing limits subsurface and surface pores in these two bores more tightly than in the rest of the casting.

Key process data at the time of notice:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Material | AlSi9Cu3 die cast |
| Casting weight (raw) | 2.84 kg |
| Finished weight | 2.61 kg |
| Machining stock in bores | 0.8 mm per side (bore A), 0.7 mm per side (bore B) |
| Weekly demand | approx. 1,150 pieces |
| Casting supplier cell | Fonderia Dora, die cast cell 4 (1,600 t machine) |
| Machining line | Valsangone line 3, cells M3 and M4 |
| Bore acceptance limit | no single pore above 0.5 mm on the bore wall; no more than 3 pores between 0.3 and 0.5 mm per bore |

## 0.3 First notice

The first notice came on Monday 9 March 2026 at 06:40 (T+0) from the first-shift operator on machining line 3, Nadia Ferraro. After finish boring of bore A on a housing from the third pallet of a delivery, she saw dark pits in the bore wall during her routine visual check under the cell lamp. She stopped the cell, kept the part, and called the shift leader.

Within the first 20 minutes the shift leader, Matteo Cattaneo, took the next 40 consecutive housings from the same pallet and had them checked at the cell with the handheld bore camera. The result was:

| Check at T+0 to T+0:50 | Result |
|---|---|
| Housings checked with bore camera | 40 |
| Housings with pores above the limit in bore A | 9 |
| Housings with pores above the limit in bore B | 4 |
| Housings with pores in both bores | 3 |
| Housings with no finding | 30 |
| Reject rate in this sample | 25.0 % (10 of 40 housings had at least one finding) |

The normal reject rate for this defect on the line over the previous 26 weeks was 0.4 %, so the sample was roughly 60 times higher than usual.

## 0.4 Description of the symptom

The pits were round to irregular, black inside, and between 0.4 mm and 1.8 mm across. On several parts they sat in a line along the parting plane side of bore A. In the worst housing, six pits were counted in one bore, the largest 1.8 mm wide and about 1.2 mm deep. A pit this deep reaches past the finish-bore depth and would leave a void under the bushing seat.

Two observations shaped the first thinking:

- The pits were not visible on the raw casting. They opened only after roughing and finish boring removed the 0.7 to 0.8 mm skin. This points to subsurface gas or shrinkage porosity rather than a surface defect.
- The affected housings carried the same casting date wheel marks, covering production on 4 and 5 March 2026.

## 0.8 Retained-sample comparison

The quality archive keeps five finished housings per delivery. The five samples from each of the three earlier deliveries (15 housings) were sectioned through both bores at T+8:00 to T+9:30. No pore larger than 0.3 mm was found in any of them. The three deliveries before the affected one therefore served as a reference of normal condition. Average pore count in bore A was 0.6 pores per section for the earlier deliveries and 4.2 pores per section for the ten rejected parts.

## 0.9 Interim assessment at the end of the first 72 hours

At T+72:00 the following was known:

- The defect appears after machining and cannot be seen on raw castings.
- At least lots 2, 3 and 4 are involved; lot 1 is unconfirmed.
- About 1,188 pieces from the four lots had already left the plant as finished parts.
- The normal reject rate had risen from 0.4 % to 25 % in the first sample.
- The cause is not yet known. Gas porosity and shrinkage porosity are both possible, and the supplier's record is being reviewed.

The work on the team, the full problem description, the sorting plan and the stock locations continues in D1 to D3.

### 3.4.1 Acceptance rule used in sorting

A housing passes when neither bore shows a pore wider than 0.5 mm, and each bore has no more than 3 pores between 0.3 mm and 0.5 mm. The rule follows the drawing limit stated in D0. Borderline housings go to a second inspector, and if the two inspectors disagree the housing is treated as rejected.

### 3.4.2 Inspection tools

The sorting stations use the handheld bore camera with 12x magnification and a ring light. Beatrice Longo checked the camera scale on 10 March with a 0.5 mm reference gauge and found it within 0.02 mm. A reference board with three known pit sizes (0.3, 0.5 and 0.8 mm) stands on each table.

### 3.4.3 Operator qualification

Eight inspectors were selected. Each one passed a test on a set of 30 housings, of which 11 carried known pits. All eight identified every housing with a pit above 0.5 mm, and the lowest score on the borderline cases was 9 of 10. One inspector scoring 7 of 10 was moved to marking and packing duties.

## 3.8 Clean-point marking

A clean point is a mark that lets anyone at the plant tell at a glance that a housing or a box has gone through the sorting described above. The mark has three parts.

| Element | What it looks like | Where it is placed | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Green dot | 8 mm paint marker dot, green | On the flat face next to bore B | Both bores passed the camera check |
| Red dot | 8 mm paint marker dot, red | Same place | Housing rejected |
| Box label | Green label 100 × 60 mm with date, shift letter, inspector number and piece count | Two short sides of each box | Box contains only green-dot housings |

In addition, the sorting shift puts a count slip into each box. The slip states the number of pieces, the casting lot, the shift and the signature of the inspector. A box without a slip and a label is treated as not sorted.

### 3.8.1 Marking rules

### 3.8.2 Start of the clean stock

The first pieces made from new castings with a changed foundry condition will be marked with a blue dot as well as the green dot once such castings exist, so that they can be told from sorted older stock. Until then, no blue marks are used.

## 4. Measurement results

This section records the measurements taken on HB-620 gearbox mount housings die cast in AlSi9Cu3 by Fonderia Dora and machined at Valsangone Componenti S.p.A. The defect under investigation is gas and shrinkage porosity exposed in the two mount bores (bore A and bore B, both finished to Ø42 H7) after finish boring. All measurements were taken between 2026-09-02 and 2026-11-06.

**1.1 Acceptance criteria used for evaluation**

The machined bore surface is evaluated against the following limits from the housing specification. The limits are applied per bore.

| Characteristic | Limit | Applied zone |
|---|---|---|
| Single pore diameter on machined bore surface | ≤ 0.8 mm | Full bore length (38 mm) |
| Number of pores ≥ 0.3 mm | ≤ 3 per bore | Full bore length |
| Pores in the 6 mm sealing land at the open end | none ≥ 0.3 mm | Sealing land |
| Distance between two pores ≥ 0.5 mm | ≥ 5 mm | Full bore length |
| Internal porosity by X-ray around bore (wall 7 to 11 mm) | Grade 2 or better on the plant's 1 to 5 reference scale | 3 mm radial band around bore |
| Pressure decay leak rate, 2.0 bar, 15 s test | ≤ 25 Pa in 15 s | Complete housing |

- Bore B is affected about three times as often as bore A.
- Pores are predominantly shrinkage in character and concentrate at the rib junction end of the bore.
- Density loss is local to the boss and not general to the casting.
- The measurement system can resolve the defect with acceptable confidence.

## 5. D4 Ishikawa analysis

This section analyses possible causes of porosity in the HB-620 mount bores using the six standard categories: man, machine, method, material, measurement and environment. The analysis combines the measurement data from the previous section with interviews and a two-day visit to Fonderia Dora on 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-23, attended by a Valsangone supplier quality engineer, a process engineer and a die casting specialist from an independent consultancy. Each potential cause was rated on evidence: **confirmed** (data shows it contributed), **possible** (plausible, not yet shown) or **excluded** (data shows it did not contribute).

**2.1 Man**

The casting cell at Fonderia Dora runs three shifts. The nonconforming rate was compared by shift for the 570 sampled parts, using the shift stamp on the pallet tag.

| Shift | Parts sampled | Nonconforming | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Early (06:00 to 14:00) | 196 | 17 | 8.7 % |
| Late (14:00 to 22:00) | 188 | 16 | 8.5 % |
| Night (22:00 to 06:00) | 186 | 17 | 9.1 % |

The rates are practically identical, so no shift-specific operator behaviour explains the defect. Training records for the nine operators on the cell were reviewed and all had completed the foundry's die casting operator course. One point was noted: the ladling and spray cycle for this die depends on a manual check of the spray nozzle pattern at each shift start, and the check is recorded only as a tick. Two operators described the squeeze pin water flow indicator as "not easy to read". This is a contributing condition rather than a cause of the change in September. Rating: **excluded as a primary cause**, with the readability point carried into the machine category.

**2.2 Machine**

The HB-620 housing is cast on a 1,100 t cold chamber machine with a two-cavity-equivalent single cavity die. The die has a local squeeze pin in the boss of bore B, intended to apply pressure to the last-solidifying region, and a dedicated cooling circuit to that pin and the boss insert.

The checks at the supplier gave the following values.

| Parameter | Specified | Recorded, DL-4402 | Recorded, DL-4466 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boss insert cooling flow, bore B | 6.5 l/min ± 0.5 | 6.4 l/min | 4.1 l/min |
| Boss insert cooling flow, bore A | 6.5 l/min ± 0.5 | 6.6 l/min | 6.3 l/min |
| Squeeze pin stroke | 18 mm | 18 mm | 18 mm |
| Squeeze pin delay after fill | 3.5 s | 3.5 s | 3.5 s |
| Intensification pressure | 620 bar | 618 bar | 612 bar |
| Boss insert surface temperature before spray | 180 to 220 °C | 204 °C | 251 °C |

The flow through the bore B insert circuit fell by 37 % between the first and the last of the lots reviewed, and the insert ran about 47 °C hotter. A hotter insert delays solidification of the boss and lengthens the time the squeeze pin has to work against a mushy zone that is partly already feeding-starved. The bore A circuit was unchanged, which fits the 74 % bias towards bore B. When the circuit was opened on 2026-10-23, the channel feeding the bore B insert was found to have a partial deposit layer from water treatment, reducing the effective bore by an estimated 30 %. Rating: **confirmed**.

**2.3 Method**

Three process method items were reviewed.

First, the die spray method. The spray cycle was 4.2 s of lubricant per shot in August. A parameter log shows that on 2026-09-09 the cycle was extended to 5.6 s to reduce soldering on the core pin of bore A. The longer spray further cools the surface but also leaves more residual film in the boss, and the shot-to-shot insert temperature trace is more erratic after the change. The extension was not accompanied by any check on the porosity of the boss. Rating: **possible, contributing**.

Second, the fast shot phase and fill time. Fill time was 38 ms in all lots, and the fast shot velocity was 3.4 m/s within a ±0.1 m/s band. No change. Rating: **excluded**.

Third, the in-process inspection method. The foundry's final inspection checks surface appearance and one X-ray part per lot, at a position that does not cover the bore B boss. The method could not detect the rising porosity. Rating: **confirmed as a detection gap**.

**2.4 Material**

The alloy is AlSi9Cu3 supplied as ingot to the foundry's central furnace and delivered by ladle to the holding furnace of the cell. Three material aspects were tested.

| Aspect | Method | Result, DL-4402 | Result, DL-4466 | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chemical composition (spark spectrometer) | Si, Cu, Fe, Mg, Mn | Si 9.1 %, Cu 2.9 %, Fe 0.8 %, Mg 0.28 %, Mn 0.31 % | Si 9.0 %, Cu 3.0 %, Fe 0.8 %, Mg 0.30 %, Mn 0.30 % | Within specification, no difference |
| Reduced pressure density index | Melt sample before cast | 3.1 % | 6.8 % | Gas content raised in DL-4466 |
| Remelt share in charge | Charge sheets | 38 % | 52 % | Higher return material |
| Oxide film index (K-mold) | Fracture rating | 14 mm | 29 mm | Raised in DL-4466 |

Composition is not the cause. However, the density index of the melt rose from 3.1 % to 6.8 %, and the remelt share in the charge rose from 38 % to 52 % after a change in the supplier's scrap handling. High remelt share raises oxide content and gas pick-up. This is a plausible amplifier because gas that is not removed adds to shrinkage voids, and the share of smooth round pores (18 %) shows some gas contribution. Rating: **possible, contributing**.

**2.5 Measurement**

Two measurement aspects need consideration: whether Valsangone's measurements overstate the defect, and whether the supplier's measurements missed it.

On the Valsangone side, the gauge study in section 1.9 shows 95 % agreement with the reference microscope, and no evidence of bias. An overestimate is excluded: of the 188 pores recorded, 11 were re-examined by a sectioned cut and all were confirmed as true voids, none as tool marks or chips. On the supplier side, the single X-ray part per lot is taken from the first five shots after a die change, when the die is still at thermal equilibrium, so the sample cannot represent the later production with the drifted cooling circuit. The foundry also has no routine check of cooling flow, which is read from an analogue flow meter once per shift and not trended. Rating: **excluded at Valsangone, confirmed at supplier as a detection gap**.

**2.6 Environment**

The factors considered were ambient temperature and humidity in the casting hall, the water supply to the die cooling and the machine hall layout.

| Factor | August | September | October | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hall temperature, mean | 31 °C | 27 °C | 21 °C | Cooler, not worse |
| Relative humidity, mean | 58 % | 63 % | 66 % | Small rise |
| Cooling water inlet temperature | 24 °C | 24 °C | 23 °C | Stable |
| Cooling water hardness | 17 °fH | 22 °fH | 27 °fH | Rising |

Hall temperature and humidity changes are too small to affect the casting. The cooling water hardness is different: it rose from 17 to 27 °fH after a change in the plant's softener regeneration schedule, which is consistent with the deposit found in the bore B insert channel. Rating: **confirmed as an upstream cause of the flow loss**.

|---|---|---|

| Machine | Reduced cooling flow to bore B insert, hotter boss | Confirmed |
| Method | Longer die spray cycle from 2026-09-09 | Possible, contributing |

## 6. D4 Five-why analysis and verification tests

This section follows the confirmed and possible causes from the Ishikawa evaluation through three five-why chains, and then describes the laboratory tests and trials used to verify each link. Chain 1 deals with the occurrence of porosity, chain 2 with the amplifying factors and chain 3 with the escape of the defect to Valsangone.

**3.1 Five-why chain 1: occurrence of shrinkage porosity at the bore B rib junction**

| Level | Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Why are there pores in the bore B surface after machining? | Shrinkage voids are exposed by boring | Videoscope, pore shape, section cuts |
| Why 1 | Why do shrinkage voids form at the rib junction? | The 14 mm section solidifies last and is not fed enough | Rib is the thickest section; X-ray grades up to 5 |
| Why 2 | Why is it not fed enough? | The squeeze pin works against a boss that is too hot and has already partly solidified at the surface | Insert surface 251 °C against 180 to 220 °C |
| Why 3 | Why is the boss too hot? | Cooling flow through the bore B insert fell from 6.5 to 4.1 l/min | Flow measurement on the die |
| Why 4 | Why did the flow fall? | A deposit layer reduced the channel cross-section | Channel opened on 2026-10-23, deposit found |
| Why 5 | Why did the deposit form? | Water hardness rose from 17 to 27 °fH and no descaling interval or flow trend monitoring existed for the die circuits | Water analysis, maintenance schedule |

Root cause 1 is therefore the lack of a controlled descaling and flow-monitoring routine for the die cooling circuits, combined with a rise in water hardness.

**3.2 Five-why chain 2: amplifying factors from melt and spray**

| Level | Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Why was the rate of nonconformity higher in lots DL-4451 and DL-4466 than flow loss alone would predict? | Gas and oxide content in the melt were higher, and the die surface was colder at the surface but hotter in the core of the insert | Density index 6.8 %, K-mold 29 mm |
| Why 1 | Why was gas content higher? | Degassing was less effective | Rotor degassing time was 4 min in the log, against 6 min in the standard |
| Why 2 | Why was degassing shortened? | The remelt share rose to 52 % and the furnace was refilled more frequently, so the cycle was cut to keep the cell supplied | Charge sheets, furnace log |
| Why 3 | Why did the remelt share rise? | Gate and runner return was not reduced when yield on a second die was changed | Material balance |
| Why 4 | Why was this not noticed? | There is no trigger limit for remelt share or density index in the process plan | Process plan review |
| Why 5 | Why is there no trigger? | The process plan was written for a stable year-long run and was never updated when the cell took on a second housing | Document review with foundry quality manager |

In the same chain, the longer spray cycle introduced on 2026-09-09 was found to increase the insert temperature variation by 11 °C peak to peak. This was recorded as a minor contributing factor and is addressed in the trials below.

**3.3 Five-why chain 3: escape of the defect to Valsangone**

| Level | Question | Answer | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem | Why did parts with porosity at the bore reach the machining line? | Neither the foundry nor Valsangone inspected the bore region before machining | Control plan review |
| Why 1 | Why did the foundry not detect it? | One X-ray part per lot from the first shots only | X-ray log |
| Why 2 | Why only the first shots? | The sampling position was chosen for die set-up approval and never revised for series production | Interview |
| Why 3 | Why did Valsangone incoming inspection not detect it? | The incoming plan checks dimensions and surface appearance, and the bore is raw cast and not accessible to the gauge | Incoming plan |
| Why 4 | Why was the bore not evaluated in incoming inspection? | Historical porosity rates were below 3 %, and the machining stage screens it | Scrap history, 12 months |
| Why 5 | Why was the rising trend not seen earlier? | Machining scrap was reported monthly and aggregated by cause group, so the porosity increase in September was not separated until October | Reporting format |

**3.4 Verification test 1: metallography of the affected region**

Six sectioned bore B boss samples (three from DL-4402, three from DL-4466) were mounted, polished and etched. Observations were made at 50x and 200x.

| Sample | Lot | Secondary dendrite arm spacing | Largest void (section) | Void character | Area fraction of voids |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M1 | DL-4402 | 21 µm | 0.18 mm | Small, interdendritic | 0.4 % |
| M2 | DL-4402 | 23 µm | 0.22 mm | Small, interdendritic | 0.5 % |
| M3 | DL-4402 | 22 µm | 0.15 mm | Small, interdendritic | 0.3 % |
| M4 | DL-4466 | 39 µm | 1.9 mm | Irregular, shrinkage | 3.1 % |
| M5 | DL-4466 | 42 µm | 1.4 mm | Irregular, shrinkage | 2.6 % |
| M6 | DL-4466 | 37 µm | 2.3 mm | Irregular with oxide film | 3.8 % |

The dendrite arm spacing is nearly doubled in DL-4466, which indicates a markedly slower solidification rate in the boss. This directly supports the finding of a hotter insert. Sample M6 showed oxide films lining the void walls, in line with the melt cleanliness result.

**3.5 Verification test 2: hardness and mechanical effect**

Hardness was measured on the machined face next to bore B, 5 measurements per part, on 10 parts per group.

| Group | Mean hardness (HBW 2.5/62.5) | Standard deviation | Minimum |
|---|---|---|---|
| DL-4402 | 91 | 2.1 | 87 |
| DL-4466, no pore in bore | 89 | 2.6 | 84 |
| DL-4466, with pore in bore | 82 | 4.8 | 73 |

The lower, more scattered hardness near the porous region confirms reduced local density. Two tensile bars taken from the boss region of DL-4466 gave 148 and 139 MPa against 196 and 201 MPa from DL-4402. This reduction matters because the bore carries bolt preload and cyclic load from the gearbox.

**3.6 Verification test 3: cooling circuit correction trial**

To prove the cause, the foundry flushed and descaled the bore B insert circuit on 2026-10-24 and restored flow to 6.5 l/min. Spray cycle and remaining parameters were left unchanged, so that only the flow was varied. Two trial runs of 200 parts each were made, with a separate melt batch in each, and 100 parts of each run were machined and inspected at Valsangone.

| Condition | Insert flow | Insert surface temperature | Parts inspected | Nonconforming | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before descaling (DL-4466 data) | 4.1 l/min | 251 °C | 80 | 12 | 15.0 % |
| After descaling, remelt 52 % | 6.5 l/min | 207 °C | 100 | 5 | 5.0 % |
| After descaling, remelt 35 % and 6 min degassing | 6.5 l/min | 205 °C | 100 | 2 | 2.0 % |

Restoring flow cut the rate from 15.0 % to 5.0 %, which is about two thirds of the problem. Adding the melt measures cut it further to 2.0 %, back in the background range seen in August. This is consistent with chain 1 as the main cause and chain 2 as the amplifier.

**3.7 Verification test 4: spray cycle comparison**

A short trial of 50 parts per condition compared the 5.6 s spray cycle with the earlier 4.2 s cycle, with cooling flow restored and the melt at normal state.

| Spray cycle | Insert temperature variation (peak to peak) | Parts inspected | Nonconforming |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.6 s | 19 °C | 50 | 2 |
| 4.2 s | 8 °C | 50 | 1 |

The difference in nonconforming parts is within sampling noise, so the longer spray cycle is a minor factor on its own and is not a root cause, although it increases temperature scatter.

**3.8 Verification of the detection gap**

The foundry's X-ray plan was replayed against retained X-ray images. A sampling position of one part per lot from the first five shots would have detected porosity grade 3 or worse in the boss in 0 of the 6 lots reviewed. A plan of 3 parts per lot, taken at start, mid and end of run, and including the bore B boss in the field of view, would have detected it in 4 of the 6 lots, including the three highest-rate lots. This confirms that the detection gap in chain 3 is real and that a repositioned X-ray sample would have given earlier warning.

**3.9 Conclusions from the analysis and tests**

- Root cause of occurrence: slow solidification of the bore B boss due to reduced insert cooling flow, caused by scale deposit from harder water and no descaling or flow trend routine.
- Amplifying cause: higher remelt share and shortened degassing, resulting in melt density index of 6.8 %.
- Minor contributing factor: longer spray cycle, with a measurable effect only on temperature variation.
- Root cause of escape: sampling and incoming inspection plans that did not examine the bore region, and monthly aggregated scrap reporting.

These conclusions are the basis for the corrective actions in the later sections of this report.

## 7. D5 and D6 Candidate corrective actions

## 1. D5 and D6 Candidate Corrective Actions

### 1.1 Basis for the action options
The actions below address the two verified cause groups for porosity in the two Ø42 H7 mount bores of the HB-620 gearbox mount housing, die cast in AlSi9Cu3 by Fonderia Dora on its 840 t cold-chamber press. Sectioning and CT scanning of 46 housings showed gas and shrinkage pores of 0.8 to 2.4 mm, clustered 6 to 10 mm below the cast bore surface. This is the region of the 19 mm boss, which is thick compared with the 8 mm nominal wall around it.

The cause groups are:

- **Occurrence cause 1: gas entrapment.** The vacuum valve channel on the die was partly blocked. Measured cavity vacuum was 180 to 260 mbar against a 80 mbar requirement. Spray volume of die lubricant was also high at the boss insert.
- **Occurrence cause 2: localized hot spot.** The boss insert ran 38 °C above the die average. Intensification pressure was applied late, so the last-solidifying zone was not fed.
- **Escape cause:** Fonderia Dora checked only surface appearance and one X-ray piece per shift. Valsangone incoming inspection checked dimensions only, so porosity was found at the machining cell after the bores were finished.

### 1.2 Options considered
Eight options were scored by the 8D team on effectiveness against each cause (1 to 5), cost, lead time and implementation risk. The team also weighed the effect each option has on the established process window.

| ID | Option | Cause addressed | Effectiveness (1–5) | Est. cost (EUR) | Lead time | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Clean and redesign vacuum channel; add vacuum sensor with shot-by-shot interlock | Gas | 5 | 9,800 | 3 weeks | Low |
| B | Reduce lubricant spray volume at boss insert from 1.8 to 1.1 mL per shot | Gas | 3 | 0 | 1 week | Low |
| C | Spot cooling (pulsed jet) in boss insert | Hot spot | 4 | 6,400 | 4 weeks | Medium |
| D | Squeeze pin in boss, 12 mm stroke | Hot spot | 5 | 14,200 | 7 weeks | Medium |
| E | Retune shot profile: earlier intensification, slow-shot 0.22 m/s | Both | 3 | 1,500 | 2 weeks | Medium |
| F | Add boss rib to reduce thickness to 14 mm | Hot spot | 5 | 38,000 | 16 weeks | High |
| G | Switch to semi-solid or low-pressure process | Both | 4 | 210,000 | 9 months | High |
| H | 100% X-ray at Fonderia Dora end of line | Escape | 2 | 31,000 per year | 4 weeks | Low |

Option F changes part geometry and option G changes the process route. Both were set aside because their lead times do not suit the delivery schedule, and because options A, C, D and E are expected to reach the target without them. Option H detects the defect but does not remove it, so it is kept only as an interim barrier.

### 1.3 Selected actions
The team selected a package of five actions, ranked by expected effect:

1. **A1 (option A):** Rebuild the vacuum channel with a larger 6 mm passage and a chill-vent block. Fit a pressure sensor with a shot-by-shot interlock that rejects any shot above 100 mbar at fill end.
2. **A2 (option B):** Cut the spray volume at the boss insert to 1.1 mL per shot and add a 2 s air purge before closing.
3. **A3 (option C):** Install pulsed spot cooling in the boss insert, targeting a 20 °C reduction in local temperature.
4. **A4 (option E):** Retune the shot profile with intensification starting 0.04 s earlier.
5. **A5 (option D):** Squeeze pin, held in reserve. It is built only if trials of A1 to A4 do not meet the criteria in section 1.5. The pin and its insert are ordered at risk because of the 7-week lead time.

Two escape-side actions complete the package:

6. **A6:** Valsangone adds a CT or X-ray sampling plan at incoming inspection for the bore zone: 5 pieces per lot for the first 20 lots, then 3 pieces per lot.
7. **A7:** Fonderia Dora moves bore-zone X-ray from one piece per shift to five pieces per shift, using a defined acceptance image set.

### 2.5 Lessons learned

1. **A dimension-only incoming check cannot catch internal defects.** The pores lay 6 to 10 mm below the cast surface and became visible only when the bores were machined. Valsangone now matches incoming inspection to the failure mode of the part, not only to its drawing dimensions.
2. **Vacuum is a process characteristic, not a maintenance item.** A monthly manual check allowed a partly blocked channel to run for an estimated 5 weeks. Continuous measurement is the lesson, and it is the basis of control F1.
3. **A thick boss needs thermal control.** The 38 °C temperature excess at the boss insert was never measured. Thermocouples at known hot spots are now a standard question in die design reviews.
4. **A change in spray settings is a process change.** The spray volume had been raised from 1.2 to 1.8 mL to reduce soldering and was not rechecked against porosity. Any such change now needs a short CT check on 10 pieces.
5. **Sampling frequency of one piece per shift is too low for this defect.** A pore cluster present in about 5% of shots can be missed for many shifts. The foundry's new frequency is based on that detection probability.
6. **Early containment worked best when it used a quick method.** Borescope checks at 3 minutes per piece were slower than the X-ray station at 40 seconds. Valsangone keeps one X-ray station reserved for containment.

### 2.6 Training and communication
Dora operators and shift leaders will be trained on the new spray and vacuum controls on 26 November 2026, with 14 people expected. Valsangone incoming inspectors and cell operators (9 people) are trained on the CT and X-ray image set on 20 November 2026. A one-page summary of lessons 1 to 6 goes to the Valsangone supplier quality team and the Fonderia Dora plant manager by 18 December 2026. The foundry design review checklist gets two new questions: boss thickness ratio and vacuum monitoring. The design owner is E. Valli, with a planned date of 29 January 2027.
