# Aurelia supplier quality manual, rev 4 (excerpt)

## 1. Scope, applicability and order of precedence

**1.1 Purpose of this manual**

1.1.1 This manual sets out the quality expectations that Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") places on companies that supply it with production goods and services. It describes how Aurelia and its suppliers plan, build, measure and improve product so that vehicles leave the Modena assembly plant and the Foggia powertrain plant with the level of quality that customers expect.

1.1.2 The manual is written for supplier quality managers, program managers, plant managers and the engineers who work with Aurelia Supplier Quality Engineers (SQEs). It is not a substitute for the technical requirements on a drawing or specification. It sits alongside those requirements and explains the quality system around them.

1.1.3 The target performance levels used throughout the manual are: 0 supplier-caused line stoppages per program quarter, a delivered-quality result of no more than 25 defective parts per million (ppm) for electronic assemblies and no more than 50 ppm for all other commodities, and on-time delivery of at least 98.5 percent measured by line-side receipt.

**1.2 Who this manual applies to**

1.2.1 The manual applies to every legal entity that holds a purchase order, a long-term supply agreement or a nomination letter from Aurelia for production material, production parts, subassemblies, or services that act directly on product. The manual applies to the entity as a whole and to each manufacturing site that ships to Aurelia or to an Aurelia-designated logistics hub.

1.2.2 Applicability is determined by supplier category. Table 1 shows how the main categories are treated.

**Table 1 – Supplier categories and applicability**

| Category | Description | Example | Applies in full | Applies in part |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Production parts and assemblies shipped to Aurelia plants | Seat frames, wiring harnesses, brake calipers | Yes | – |
| B | Raw and semi-finished material used in A parts | Steel coil, aluminium castings, resin granulate | Yes | – |
| C | Special processes on product | Heat treatment, plating, painting, welding | Yes | – |
| D | Services on product | Sorting, rework, containment, end-of-line testing | Yes | – |
| E | Logistics and packaging | Returnable containers, sequencing centres | – | Delivery and traceability parts only |
| F | Aftersales parts | Service parts for vehicles in the field | Yes | – |
| G | Indirect goods and services | Tooling consumables, office supplies, catering | – | Not applicable unless named in the purchase order |

1.2.3 Aurelia classifies each supplier into a category at nomination. A supplier may belong to more than one category, in which case the stricter set of expectations is used for each activity. The category is recorded in the supplier master record and can be requested from the SQE at any time.

1.2.4 The manual applies to prototype, pre-series and series phases. Where a requirement is stated as applying only to series production, the text says so.

**1.3 Sites, programs and volumes**

1.3.1 The manual applies to all current and future vehicle programs. At the date of this edition, Aurelia runs three vehicle platforms: the compact crossover platform (annual plan of 84,000 vehicles), the grand tourer platform (annual plan of 12,500 vehicles) and the electrified sedan platform (annual plan of 46,000 vehicles). The same quality expectations apply to all three. Differences in volume affect how Aurelia sizes sampling plans and audit frequency, not the basic obligations of the supplier.

1.3.2 Suppliers shipping to Aurelia from more than one site must be able to identify, on every delivery document and on every container label, the site of manufacture. A supplier that moves production between its own sites carries the same obligations at each site.

1.3.3 Aurelia may assign a supplier a tier rating of 1, 2 or 3, where tier 1 indicates a supplier of safety-relevant or regulation-relevant parts or one that ships more than EUR 20 million per year. The tier rating influences audit frequency (tier 1 annually, tier 2 every 24 months, tier 3 every 36 months) and is not a ranking of the supplier's importance as a business partner.

**1.4 Sub-tier suppliers and subcontractors**

1.4.1 The contracted supplier remains responsible for the quality of everything it ships, including material and processes sourced from its own suppliers. Aurelia does not release the contracted supplier from any obligation because work has been subcontracted.

1.4.2 The supplier must pass on to its sub-tier suppliers every quality requirement in this manual that is relevant to the purchased item or process. The supplier must keep evidence that it has done so, and must be able to show it to an Aurelia SQE within five working days of a request.

1.4.3 Where Aurelia has nominated a sub-tier supplier (a directed-buy arrangement), the contracted supplier still manages the sub-tier supplier's performance, unless the purchase order states otherwise in writing.

1.4.4 Distributors and trading companies that do not manufacture are covered by this manual in the same way as manufacturers. They must identify the original manufacturer of each item and keep traceability back to it.

**1.5 Relationship to purchase orders**

1.5.1 A purchase order is the commercial and technical instruction under which a supplier delivers goods or services to Aurelia. It normally consists of the order form, the general terms of purchase, a drawing or specification package, and a reference to this manual.

1.5.2 This manual is incorporated into each purchase order by reference. By accepting a purchase order, or by shipping against it, the supplier accepts the quality expectations in this manual as part of the contract.

1.5.3 The manual does not set prices, quantities, delivery dates, payment terms or liability limits. Those matters belong to the purchase order and the general terms of purchase. Where this manual describes a quality activity that carries a cost, such as a launch audit or a containment action, the commercial treatment of that cost is as stated in the purchase order or the supply agreement.

1.5.4 A purchase order may add program-specific requirements, for example a tighter ppm target for a particular part number, an extended warranty period, or a special characteristic list. Such additions apply in the way set out in the order of precedence below.

1.5.5 Where a purchase order refers to "the current edition" of the manual, the edition meant is the one published on the Aurelia supplier portal on the date the purchase order was issued, unless the purchase order names a specific edition.

**1.6 Order of precedence**

1.6.1 If two documents give conflicting requirements, the document higher in Table 2 prevails. The lower document continues to apply to the extent that it does not conflict.

**Table 2 – Order of precedence**

| Rank | Document | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Applicable law, type-approval regulations and mandatory standards | Safety, emissions, homologation |
| 2 | Purchase order commercial terms and the supply agreement | Price, quantity, liability, term |
| 3 | Aurelia drawings, engineering specifications and material specifications | Dimensions, tolerances, performance |
| 4 | Program-specific quality requirements written into the purchase order | Special characteristics, ppm targets |
| 5 | This supplier quality manual | Quality system and process expectations |
| 6 | Customer-specific interpretations of international quality system standards | Generic system requirements |
| 7 | Supplier's own procedures and instructions | Internal methods |

1.6.2 If the supplier believes two requirements conflict and cannot tell which prevails, the supplier must ask its SQE in writing before proceeding, and must not decide the matter alone. The SQE will answer in writing, normally within ten working days.

1.6.3 Law and regulation always prevail. If a requirement of Aurelia would lead the supplier to break a law that applies at the supplier's site, the supplier must tell its SQE promptly so that the requirement can be reviewed.

1.6.4 A requirement in the supplier's own procedures that is stricter than this manual is allowed and is the supplier's own responsibility to meet.

**1.7 Language, units and format**

1.7.1 The governing language of this manual is English. Where an Italian translation is provided for convenience, the English text prevails in the event of a difference.

1.7.2 Dimensions are in millimetres, mass in kilograms, temperature in degrees Celsius, torque in newton metres, and currency in euros unless a purchase order states otherwise. Dates follow the format day-month-year, with the month written in letters on all quality documents sent to Aurelia.

1.7.3 Quality records sent to Aurelia must be legible, identify the part number and drawing level, and carry the supplier name and site.

**1.8 Matters outside this manual**

1.8.1 The following are outside the scope of this manual and are covered by separate Aurelia instructions: environmental and social conduct obligations, information security requirements, customs and trade compliance, and intellectual property terms.

1.8.2 Nothing in this manual limits any right of Aurelia under the purchase order, the supply agreement or applicable law.

**1.9 Interpretation and contact**

1.9.1 Questions about the scope or interpretation of this manual go to the responsible SQE. If the SQE cannot resolve the matter, it goes to the Supplier Quality Manager for the relevant plant. The central supplier quality office in Modena can be reached through the supplier portal help desk, which is staffed on working days between 08:00 and 17:30 Central European Time.

## 2. Definitions and abbreviations

**2.1 How to use this glossary**

2.1.1 Terms in this section have the meaning given here wherever they appear in the manual, in a purchase order that refers to the manual, or in correspondence with an Aurelia SQE. Where a term also has a general meaning in everyday speech, the meaning below is the one that applies.

2.1.2 The definitions are written for practical use. They are not a replacement for the industry guides from which many of the terms come. Where this manual gives a numeric threshold, that threshold is Aurelia's own and may be stricter than the industry guide.

**2.2 Core quality planning and approval terms**

2.2.1 **APQP (Advanced Product Quality Planning).** A structured method for planning a product and its process from concept to series production. Aurelia expects APQP to be run as a cross-functional activity with a named team leader, a timing plan, and regular reviews. Aurelia uses five phases: planning, product design and development, process design and development, product and process validation, and launch with feedback and corrective action.

2.2.2 **PPAP (Production Part Approval Process).** The set of evidence a supplier provides to show that its process can produce parts that meet every drawing and specification requirement at the quoted production rate. Aurelia asks for the submission level named in the purchase order. Level 3 is the default and means the supplier submits the warrant together with product samples and complete supporting data.

2.2.3 **PSW (Part Submission Warrant).** The cover form of a PPAP submission. It identifies the part, the drawing level, the reason for submission, the submission level and the supplier site, and it carries a declaration by a named supplier representative that the results are true.

2.2.4 **Control plan.** A written description of the controls used at each process step to keep product within requirements. It lists the process step, machine or tool, characteristic, specification, measurement method, sample size and frequency, and reaction plan. Aurelia expects separate control plans for prototype, pre-launch and production phases.

2.2.5 **PFMEA (Process Failure Mode and Effects Analysis).** A structured analysis of how each process step could fail, the effect of each failure, its causes, and the controls in place. Ratings for severity, occurrence and detection are given on a scale of 1 to 10.

2.2.6 **DFMEA (Design Failure Mode and Effects Analysis).** The equivalent analysis for the design of a product. A supplier with design responsibility prepares it and shares it with the Aurelia design engineer on request.

2.2.7 **Process flow diagram.** A drawing that shows every step of the manufacturing process from receipt of material to shipment, including rework, inspection and storage steps.

2.2.8 **Special characteristic.** A product feature or process parameter that Aurelia has identified as having a particular effect on safety, regulatory compliance, fit, function or appearance. Aurelia marks these on drawings with its own symbols, and the supplier must carry the marking forward into the PFMEA, control plan and work instructions.

**2.3 Measurement and statistics terms**

2.3.1 **MSA (Measurement System Analysis).** A group of studies that judge how much of the variation seen in measured data comes from the measurement system itself rather than from the product.

2.3.2 **Gauge R&R (Repeatability and Reproducibility).** An MSA study that separates the variation caused by one appraiser using one gauge (repeatability) from the variation between appraisers (reproducibility). Aurelia's reference study uses 10 parts, 3 appraisers and 3 trials.

2.3.3 **%GRR.** The share of the tolerance or total variation consumed by the measurement system. Aurelia reads results as follows: under 10 percent is acceptable, 10 to 30 percent is conditionally acceptable depending on the importance of the characteristic and cost of improvement, and over 30 percent is not acceptable for a special characteristic.

2.3.4 **ndc (number of distinct categories).** The number of groups a gauge can reliably tell apart in the process variation. Aurelia expects a value of 5 or more.

2.3.5 **SPC (Statistical Process Control).** The use of control charts and related methods to monitor a process over time and detect shifts before nonconforming product is made.

2.3.6 **Cp and Cpk.** Capability indices that compare the spread of a stable process with the tolerance. Cp ignores the position of the process mean. Cpk takes it into account. Both are short-term measures taken from a stable run.

2.3.7 **Pp and Ppk.** Performance indices calculated from the overall variation of a longer data set. Aurelia uses them for initial studies, for which at least 100 consecutive parts are measured.

2.3.8 **Capability targets.** Table 3 shows the targets used by Aurelia unless a purchase order sets a different value.

**Table 3 – Capability targets**

| Characteristic type | Initial study (Ppk) | Ongoing (Cpk) | Minimum sample |
|---|---|---|---|
| Special characteristic, safety or regulatory | ≥ 1.67 | ≥ 1.33 | 100 consecutive parts |
| Special characteristic, fit or function | ≥ 1.67 | ≥ 1.33 | 100 consecutive parts |
| Standard characteristic, variable data | ≥ 1.33 | ≥ 1.00 | 50 consecutive parts |
| Attribute characteristic | Not applicable | Zero defects in sample | 300 parts |

**2.4 Problem solving and performance terms**

2.4.1 **8D.** An eight-discipline report format for solving a problem, from team formation and problem description through containment, root cause, corrective action, verification and prevention of recurrence.

2.4.2 **Containment.** Any action taken to stop suspect product from reaching Aurelia or the vehicle, for example sorting at the supplier site, at a logistics hub or at the Aurelia line.

2.4.3 **Root cause.** The underlying reason a problem occurred and was not detected. Aurelia expects both an occurrence root cause and an escape root cause.

2.4.4 **Corrective action.** An action that removes the root cause of an existing problem.

2.4.5 **Preventive action.** An action taken before a problem occurs, based on risk or on lessons from other parts or processes.

2.4.6 **ppm (parts per million).** The number of defective parts per million parts delivered, calculated over a rolling 12-month period using parts rejected at Aurelia plants and in the field within the warranty period.

2.4.7 **Escape.** A nonconforming product that left the supplier site undetected.

2.4.8 **Line stoppage.** An interruption of the Aurelia assembly line that lasts more than 3 minutes and is caused by a supplier part or supplier delivery.

2.4.9 **First pass yield (FPY).** The share of units that pass every inspection and test in a process on the first attempt, without rework or repair.

2.4.10 **Poka-yoke.** A mistake-proofing device or method that prevents an error or makes it immediately visible.

**2.5 Supply chain and onboarding terms**

2.5.1 **SQE (Supplier Quality Engineer).** The Aurelia engineer responsible for the quality relationship with a named group of suppliers.

2.5.2 **Nomination.** Aurelia's written decision to award a program or part to a supplier, issued before the purchase order.

2.5.3 **RFQ (Request for Quotation).** A document that invites a supplier to quote for a defined scope.

2.5.4 **SAQ (Supplier Assessment Questionnaire).** The questionnaire a prospective supplier completes before Aurelia considers a nomination.

2.5.5 **Capacity study.** An analysis comparing the supplier's available production time and equipment output with the volume Aurelia plans to buy, including a margin for peaks.

2.5.6 **OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness).** The product of availability, performance rate and quality rate for a machine or line, expressed as a percentage.

2.5.7 **Run-at-rate.** A demonstration in which the supplier produces parts at the planned production rate over a defined period, normally 8 hours, using series tooling, series people and series methods.

2.5.8 **Launch.** The period from the start of series production until the supplier meets its quality and delivery targets for 13 consecutive weeks.

2.5.9 **Traceability.** The ability to identify the origin, processing history and destination of a part, batch or material lot. Aurelia expects lot-level traceability as a minimum and serial-level traceability for parts that carry a safety-relevant special characteristic.

2.5.10 **Lot.** A defined quantity of product made under the same conditions from the same material batch within one production run.

## 3. Supplier selection and onboarding

**3.1 Principles**

3.1.1 Aurelia selects suppliers on the basis of demonstrated ability to deliver the required quality, volume and cost over the life of a program, not on price alone. The selection process is run jointly by Purchasing, Supplier Quality, Logistics and the relevant engineering department.

3.1.2 A company that wishes to be considered for nomination must go through the steps in this section. Aurelia may shorten a step for a company with which it already has a supply relationship of three years or more and a delivered quality result of no more than 25 ppm, but does not skip the capacity and financial checks.

3.1.3 Taking part in the process creates no commitment on either side. A nomination is made only by written nomination letter.

**3.2 Selection stages**

3.2.1 Selection runs through six stages, shown in Table 4 with typical durations. Durations assume that the supplier answers requests within the time stated.

**Table 4 – Selection and onboarding stages**

| Stage | Activity | Aurelia lead | Typical duration | Supplier input |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Initial screening | Purchasing | 2 weeks | Company profile, references |
| 2 | Supplier Assessment Questionnaire | Supplier Quality | 3 weeks | Completed SAQ with evidence |
| 3 | Capacity and financial checks | Purchasing and Finance | 3 weeks | Capacity data, accounts |
| 4 | Site visit | Supplier Quality | 1 to 2 days on site | Access, staff, records |
| 5 | Selection decision and nomination | Sourcing Committee | 2 weeks | Quotation, clarifications |
| 6 | Onboarding and launch planning | Supplier Quality | 8 to 12 weeks | Named contacts, APQP timing plan |

**3.3 Initial screening**

3.3.1 Screening starts with a company profile covering legal name, ownership, sites, number of employees, main customers by sector, annual revenue for the last three years, and the product ranges the company manufactures.

3.3.2 The company must give at least two customer references from the automotive or a comparable regulated sector, with named contacts, who may be called by Aurelia.

3.3.3 Aurelia checks the company against public sanctions lists and against its own internal list of companies with which it does not trade. A match ends the process at this stage.

**3.4 Supplier Assessment Questionnaire**

3.4.1 The SAQ has 96 questions in eight blocks: management and organisation, planning and product development, purchasing and sub-tier control, production and process control, measurement and testing, problem solving, logistics and traceability, and continual improvement.

3.4.2 Each question is scored from 0 to 4. A score of 0 means no practice exists, 2 means a practice exists but is not consistently followed, and 4 means a fully working practice with evidence. Answers must be supported by documents, records or photographs, which the supplier uploads to the supplier portal.

3.4.3 The overall result is expressed as a percentage of the 384 available points. Table 5 shows how Aurelia reads the result.

**Table 5 – SAQ result bands**

| Result | Band | Normal next step |
|---|---|---|
| 85% or higher | A | Proceed to capacity, financial checks and site visit |
| 70% to 84% | B | Proceed, with a written improvement plan for every block scoring below 70% |
| 55% to 69% | C | Proceed only if the program has no alternative source and the Sourcing Committee agrees |
| Below 55% | D | Process ends; the company may reapply after 12 months |

3.4.4 A block score of 50 percent or lower in the production and process control block or the measurement and testing block places the supplier in band C regardless of the overall result.

3.4.5 The supplier must give truthful answers. Aurelia may check any answer during the site visit, and a material discrepancy between an answer and the observed situation is treated as a reason to end the process.

**3.5 Capacity checks**

3.5.1 The supplier provides a capacity study for the parts under consideration. The study must be based on realistic assumptions and must show for each constraining operation the cycle time, number of cavities or stations, planned shifts per week, planned OEE, and the volume the operation can make in a year.

3.5.2 Aurelia compares the result with the planned annual demand of the program. The supplier's planned capacity must be at least 120 percent of the peak annual demand supplied by Aurelia. For parts on the grand tourer platform, where volumes are low and tooling is shared, a margin of 110 percent is accepted.

3.5.3 The study must take into account demand from all of the supplier's customers on shared equipment. Aurelia may ask for a statement of total loading for each shared machine.

3.5.4 The following assumptions are used unless the supplier shows evidence for a different value: a working year of 46 weeks, a maximum of 5 working days per week at the planned shift model, and an OEE of no higher than 85 percent for an established process or 75 percent for a new process.

3.5.5 The supplier must also name the resources that limit capacity in the event of a demand increase of 20 percent, such as tooling, gauges, skilled operators, or sub-tier supply, and describe how and how fast the limit could be lifted.

3.5.6 Capacity is verified again for series production through the run-at-rate demonstration defined in Section 2.

**3.6 Financial checks**

3.6.1 Aurelia Finance reviews the supplier's financial standing using the last three annual accounts and, where available, an independent credit report. The supplier consents to this review by taking part in the process.

3.6.2 Table 6 shows the main indicators that Aurelia reviews and the levels at which Finance asks for further explanation.

**Table 6 – Financial indicators**

| Indicator | Level that triggers further questions |
|---|---|
| Equity ratio | Below 20% |
| Current ratio | Below 1.1 |
| Net debt to EBITDA | Above 4.0 |
| Operating margin | Below 2% in two consecutive years |
| Revenue share from Aurelia after nomination | Above 30% of total revenue |
| Days of payables outstanding | Above 90 days |

3.6.3 A trigger does not by itself exclude a company. It leads to a discussion with the supplier and, where useful, to measures such as a parent company guarantee, a shorter payment period, or a tooling ownership arrangement.

3.6.4 Information provided for financial checks is treated as confidential and is shared only with the people involved in the selection decision. Aurelia and the supplier will normally sign a non-disclosure agreement before accounts are exchanged.

**3.7 Site visit**

3.7.1 A site visit takes place at every manufacturing site that would ship to Aurelia. The visit is carried out by an SQE, with a manufacturing engineer or purchasing representative where the commodity requires it. Aurelia gives at least 10 working days of notice and sends an agenda.

3.7.2 The visit lasts one day for a site with fewer than 150 employees and two days for a larger site. It covers a walk through the whole process from goods-in to dispatch, review of records for at least three current parts, interviews with operators and supervisors, review of the measurement laboratory, and a review of the supplier's handling of nonconforming product.

3.7.3 The supplier provides a safe working environment for the visiting team, access to all areas related to the parts under consideration, and a private room for discussion.

3.7.4 Aurelia writes a site visit report within 10 working days. It lists observations in three levels: major findings, minor findings and opportunities. The supplier answers each major finding with a dated action plan within 15 working days of receiving the report.

**3.8 Selection decision and nomination**

3.8.1 The Sourcing Committee meets every second week. It reviews the SAQ result, the capacity study, the financial summary, the site visit findings and the quotation, and decides to nominate, to ask for further information, or not to nominate.

3.8.2 Aurelia tells the supplier of the outcome in writing within 5 working days of the decision. A supplier that is not nominated may ask for a short summary of the reasons.

**3.9 Onboarding**

3.9.1 After nomination, the supplier must name a quality contact, a program manager and an escalation contact for each site, with telephone numbers that are answered outside normal working hours during launch.

3.9.2 The supplier is given access to the supplier portal and is trained in its use. Aurelia runs an onboarding workshop of one day, in which the SQE explains the program timing, reporting formats, the performance measures in Section 2, and the contacts at the receiving plants.

3.9.3 Within 4 weeks of nomination, the supplier presents an APQP timing plan that covers every deliverable up to start of production, with named owners and due dates. Aurelia reviews the plan with the supplier and the two parties agree review meetings, which are normally held every two weeks until start of production.

3.9.4 The supplier must also confirm its packaging and labelling method, its delivery lead time, and its arrangements for a safety stock of at least 5 working days for the first 13 weeks of series production.

## 4. Advanced product quality planning and launch

## 4.1 Purpose and scope

4.1.1 This section describes how suppliers plan, verify and launch parts, assemblies and materials for Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. programs. It applies to every supplier that delivers production-intent parts to an Aurelia plant, including Tier 2 and Tier 3 sources that the supplier nominates.

4.1.2 Aurelia builds low-to-medium volume vehicles, with a planned peak of 38 vehicles per day at the Modena assembly plant and 22 per day at the Cuneo powertrain plant. Part volumes are small compared with mass-market programs, so Aurelia places more weight on first-time quality, early detection and stable processes than on high-volume statistics.

4.1.3 The supplier appoints one named launch leader for each program. The launch leader holds authority over tooling, process, quality and logistics resources at the supplier and attends all gateway reviews in person or by video.

## 4.2 Planning phases

4.2.1 Aurelia divides quality planning into five phases. Timings are measured back from start of production (SOP) and are typical for a new vehicle program. Shorter programs compress the durations but keep the order of the phases.

| Phase | Name | Typical timing before SOP | Principal outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Plan and define | 36 to 30 months | Feasibility study, preliminary bill of materials, special characteristics list draft, initial risk assessment |
| P2 | Product design and development | 30 to 20 months | Design FMEA, verification plan, prototype build plan, gauge concept |
| P3 | Process design and development | 20 to 10 months | Process flow, process FMEA, pre-launch control plan, measurement system plan, packaging concept |
| P4 | Product and process validation | 10 to 2 months | Pilot build, capability studies, run-at-rate, sample part package |
| P5 | Launch, feedback and improvement | 2 months before to 6 months after SOP | Launch containment results, lessons learned, ramp-up to full rate |

4.2.2 Each phase output is prepared by a cross-functional team that includes at minimum design, manufacturing engineering, quality, purchasing and logistics representatives from the supplier. Aurelia supplier quality engineers (SQEs) join the team from P2 onward.

4.2.3 The supplier keeps a single timing plan that shows every deliverable, owner and due date. The plan is shared with the Aurelia SQE at least every four weeks during P3 and P4 and every two weeks during P5.

## 4.3 Gateways

4.3.1 A gateway is a formal review held at the end of a phase. The gateway criteria below are the minimum. Aurelia may add program-specific criteria in the quality agreement for the program.

| Gateway | Held at end of | Minimum criteria |
|---|---|---|
| G1 | P1 | Feasibility commitment for all drawing requirements; capacity plan for 120% of peak demand; named launch leader |
| G2 | P2 | Design FMEA covers 100% of functions; every special characteristic carries a symbol on the drawing; prototype parts measured with 100% of dimensions reported |
| G3 | P3 | Process FMEA and pre-launch control plan cover every operation; gauge R&R studies planned for each special characteristic; tooling ordered with delivery dates before pilot build |
| G4 | P4 | Pilot build of at least 300 parts from production tooling; capability targets in section 5 met; run-at-rate completed; packaging trial finished |
| G5 | P5 | Launch containment exit criteria in 4.5 met; ramp-up to full rate demonstrated at the planned shift pattern |

4.3.2 A gateway is scored by criterion as green (fully met), amber (met with a dated recovery plan) or red (not met). A program may not enter the next phase with any red criterion. Amber criteria must carry an owner and a closing date no later than the next gateway.

4.3.3 Gateway reviews last no more than three hours. The supplier circulates the review pack five working days beforehand, so the Aurelia SQE can read it before the meeting.

## 4.4 Run-at-rate

4.4.1 Run-at-rate (R@R) demonstrates that the supplier's production line makes good parts at the quoted rate, with production tooling, production gauges, production operators and production material. R@R takes place in P4, before G4.

4.4.2 The standard R@R run is the larger of eight continuous hours or 300 consecutive parts. For assemblies with a quoted rate below 40 parts per shift, the run covers one full shift. The Aurelia SQE may attend or review the run by live video.

4.4.3 Target rate is the quoted peak rate for the part number. For example, a seat frame quoted at 24 parts per hour is run at 24 parts per hour, which gives 192 parts in an eight-hour run; the 300-part minimum then extends the run to 12.5 hours or two shifts.

4.4.4 The supplier records the following values for every R@R.

| Metric | Definition | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Rate achieved | Good parts divided by run hours | 100% of quoted rate |
| First-time-through (FTT) | Parts good at first pass divided by parts started | 95% or higher |
| Overall equipment effectiveness | Availability × performance × quality | 85% or higher |
| Scrap | Parts scrapped divided by parts started | 2.0% or lower |
| Unplanned downtime | Minutes stopped without a plan | 5% of run time or lower |
| Quality of output | Parts failing any control plan check at final inspection | 0 |

4.4.5 If the rate or FTT target is not met, the supplier identifies the constraint, corrects it and repeats the full run. A partial repeat is not accepted. Two runs that miss the targets cause the program lead at Aurelia to review the supplier's capacity plan.

4.4.6 Parts made during R@R are labelled and kept separate. The supplier measures every special characteristic on a sample of 30 parts taken evenly across the run and reports the results with the R@R summary.

## 4.5 Launch containment

4.5.1 Launch containment is an added layer of inspection that protects the Aurelia line during early production. It starts with the first shipment after G4 and continues until the exit criteria are met.

4.5.2 Aurelia defines two levels.

| Level | Who inspects | Scope | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Containment 1 (C1) | Supplier's own trained inspectors, separate from production | 100% check of every special characteristic and every feature with a history of defects in pilot build; results recorded by part serial or lot | Default for the first 12 weeks after SOP |
| Containment 2 (C2) | Independent third-party inspector at the supplier, plus C1 | C1 scope plus audit of 20 parts per shipment against the full drawing | Entered after a defect found at Aurelia or in C1 shows an escape |

4.5.3 Containment inspectors are given a work instruction with limit samples and photographs. They are not the same people who run the process, and they report to the supplier's quality manager rather than the production manager.

4.5.4 Exit from C1 requires all of the following.

- 12 consecutive weeks with zero defective parts found at the Aurelia plant for features that are covered by containment.
- A defect rate in the C1 check below 500 ppm over the last four weeks.
- Capability indices at or above the ongoing targets in section 5 for all special characteristics, based on at least 125 readings.
- Full ramp to planned rate with the same shift pattern for at least four weeks.

4.5.5 Exit from C2 returns the part to C1 for a further 8 weeks. The shorter period applies only when the cause has been verified by the Aurelia SQE on site.

4.5.6 Cost of containment, including third-party inspectors, is borne by the supplier.

## 4.6 Launch measures

4.6.1 During P5 the supplier reports weekly to the Aurelia SQE: shipped quantity, parts per million (ppm) found at Aurelia, containment findings, FTT, premium freight events and open actions. Aurelia's launch target for the first six months is 50 ppm or lower at the vehicle plant and zero line stoppages caused by the part.

4.6.2 A lessons-learned meeting takes place within six weeks after the end of P5. Learnings are fed into the supplier's standard work for the next program.

## 5. Measurement systems and statistical process control

## 5.1 Purpose and scope

5.1.1 This section sets out the minimum requirements for measurement systems and for the statistical control of processes that make Aurelia parts. It applies to all gauges, test equipment and software used to accept product or control a process at special characteristics, as well as to any other feature the control plan lists for measurement.

5.1.2 The supplier is responsible for the quality of its own measurement data. Aurelia may request the raw data behind any study and may repeat a study at the supplier's site using its own equipment.

## 5.2 Measurement system planning

5.2.1 For every characteristic listed in the control plan, the supplier writes a measurement system plan that names the gauge, resolution, range, operator group, calibration interval and location of use.

5.2.2 Gauge resolution must be no coarser than one tenth of the tolerance. For a feature with a tolerance of ±0.05 mm (total 0.10 mm), the gauge reads to 0.01 mm or finer. Where a finer gauge is not available, the supplier explains the limit in the plan and treats the measurement as variable data only if at least five distinct values appear within the process spread.

5.2.3 Calibration traces to national standards through an accredited laboratory. Default calibration intervals are 12 months for hand gauges, 6 months for fixed gauges used at special characteristics, and 12 months for coordinate measuring machines with a verification check every week using a certified artifact.

5.2.4 A gauge found out of tolerance at calibration triggers a look-back to the last good check. The supplier assesses all parts measured in that period and records the result.

## 5.3 Gauge repeatability and reproducibility

5.3.1 A gauge R&R study is made for every gauge used at a special characteristic, and for any other gauge when the control plan sets it. The standard design is 10 parts, 3 operators and 3 trials (90 readings). For automated gauges with no operator influence, 10 parts and 10 trials are used.

5.3.2 Parts for the study are chosen to span the full process spread, not only the tolerance. At least two parts lie close to each specification limit.

5.3.3 Results are expressed as a percentage of tolerance (%GRR to tolerance) and a percentage of total variation (%GRR to study variation). Aurelia limits are shown below.

| Result | %GRR to tolerance | Number of distinct categories (ndc) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suitable | 10% or lower | 10 or more | Any use, including special characteristics |
| Conditional | Above 10% to 20% | 5 to 9 | Non-special characteristics only; special characteristics need a written improvement plan with a date |
| Unsuitable | Above 20% | Below 5 | Not used for acceptance or process control |

5.3.4 For safety-related characteristics (Class S in section 6) the limit is 10% to tolerance with no conditional range. If the measurement system cannot meet this, the supplier adds a second independent measurement or a 100% automated check with a master part.

5.3.5 Bias is checked against a certified master and must not exceed 5% of tolerance. Linearity is checked at five points across the gauge range; the slope must not give a bias above 5% of tolerance at any point. Stability is tracked with a control chart of a master part measured daily; the chart must show no points outside control limits.

5.3.6 Attribute gauges, such as go/no-go gauges and visual inspection, are evaluated with an attribute agreement study of 50 parts, 3 appraisers and 3 trials. Kappa for each appraiser against the reference must be 0.90 or higher. Overall effectiveness of the check must be 90% or higher, with a miss rate (defective parts passed) of 2% or lower.

5.3.7 A study is repeated when the gauge is repaired, replaced or moved to a different fixture, and at least every 24 months for special characteristics.

## 5.4 Capability targets

5.4.1 Aurelia uses two indices. Ppk is calculated from long-term data in the initial run at the end of P4 and uses the overall standard deviation. Cpk is calculated from rational subgroups during series production and uses the within-subgroup standard deviation.

| Characteristic class | Initial Ppk (pilot build) | Ongoing Cpk (series) | Minimum data |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety and regulatory (Class S) | 2.00 or higher | 1.67 or higher | 125 readings in 25 subgroups of 5 |
| Key function (Class K) | 1.67 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 125 readings in 25 subgroups of 5 |
| Fit and appearance (Class F) | 1.33 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 100 readings in 20 subgroups of 5 |
| Other controlled features | 1.33 or higher when listed in the control plan | 1.00 or higher | As set in the control plan |

5.4.2 Capability is calculated only after the process is shown to be stable on a control chart and the data fit a distribution. When data are not normal, the supplier uses a documented non-normal method (for example, a fitted distribution with percentiles at 0.135% and 99.865%) and states the method on the study sheet.

5.4.3 For characteristics with a one-sided tolerance, or with a form-and-position tolerance that has a natural limit at zero, only the relevant side is evaluated.

5.4.4 Where a process cannot reach the target, the supplier uses 100% inspection with a suitable measurement system at that characteristic until the process is improved. Use of 100% inspection does not remove the need for a capability improvement plan.

## 5.5 Sampling and control charts

5.5.1 Series production uses variable control charts for every special characteristic that is measured by variable data. The default chart is X-bar and R with a subgroup of five consecutive parts. For low-volume lines (below 100 parts per shift) the supplier may use an individuals and moving range chart with one reading per 10 parts.

5.5.2 Default sampling frequency is shown below. The control plan may set a higher frequency.

| Production rate | Subgroup size | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Above 500 parts per hour | 5 | Every 30 minutes |
| 100 to 500 parts per hour | 5 | Every hour |
| Below 100 parts per hour | 3 to 5 | Every 2 hours, and at every start-up, tool change and shift start |
| Batch or lot processes | 5 per lot, drawn from start, middle and end | Every lot |

5.5.3 Control limits are calculated from the first 25 subgroups of stable production and are recalculated only when the supplier can show a known, lasting shift in the process. Limits must never be set equal to specification limits.

5.5.4 Operators react to the following signals:

- one point beyond a control limit;
- seven consecutive points on one side of the centre line;
- six consecutive points rising or falling;
- two out of three points beyond two sigma on the same side;
- fourteen points alternating up and down.

5.5.5 Any signal triggers the reaction plan for the characteristic (see 6.5). Charts are reviewed by a process engineer weekly, and the review is recorded on the chart or in the quality system.

5.5.6 For attribute characteristics, a p-chart or np-chart is used with a sample size large enough to expect at least one defect per sample at the target rate. Aurelia asks for zero defects at special characteristics, so for these a 100% check is used instead of a chart.

## 5.6 Data retention

5.6.1 The supplier keeps all control charts, gauge studies, capability studies and calibration records for 15 years after the end of series production for the part, or longer where the quality agreement says so. Data must be retrievable by part number and production date within two working days of an Aurelia request.

## 6. Special characteristics and control plans

## 6.1 Purpose and scope

6.1.1 Special characteristics are product features or process parameters whose variation has a strong effect on safety, regulatory conformity, vehicle function, fit or customer perception. Aurelia identifies them on the drawing or model, and suppliers identify more of them from their own design and process risk work.

6.1.2 This section sets the classes and symbols that Aurelia uses, how they are shown on drawings, how they are carried into the control plan and how the supplier reacts when a control fails.

## 6.2 Classes and symbols

6.2.1 Aurelia uses three classes. Each class has a drawing symbol, an Aurelia symbol for use in supplier documents, and a minimum level of control.

| Class | Drawing symbol | Meaning | Minimum control |
|---|---|---|---|
| S | Letter S inside a hexagon | Safety or regulatory conformity. Failure can hurt a person or breach a vehicle regulation | Ppk 2.00 and Cpk 1.67; error-proofing or 100% automated check where practical; traceability by serial number |
| K | Letter K inside a diamond | Key function. Failure causes loss of main vehicle function, durability or performance | Ppk 1.67 and Cpk 1.33; statistical control chart; traceability by lot |
| F | Letter F inside a circle | Fit, finish and appearance. Failure causes assembly difficulty or visible defect | Ppk 1.33 and Cpk 1.33; sampling per control plan; traceability by day |

6.2.2 A process parameter that controls a Class S or K product characteristic, such as a cure temperature, a torque, or a press force, is carried as a process special characteristic and takes the same class as the product characteristic it protects.

6.2.3 Suppliers may add their own internal symbols for features that Aurelia has not marked. Supplier symbols must differ in shape from the three Aurelia symbols and must be explained on the control plan legend. A supplier symbol never replaces an Aurelia symbol.

## 6.3 Marking on drawings and models

6.3.1 Aurelia places the class symbol directly next to the dimension, tolerance or note it governs. The symbol is 5 mm high on A3 sheets at a plot scale of 1:1, and not smaller than 3.5 mm on any sheet.

6.3.2 Rules for placement:

- For a linear or angular dimension, the symbol sits immediately to the left of the dimension value, on the dimension line.
- For a geometric tolerance, the symbol sits outside the left end of the feature control frame, touching it.
- For a datum feature that controls a special characteristic, the symbol is placed beside the datum triangle.
- For material, surface or process notes (for example, a hardness range of 58 to 62 HRC), the symbol precedes the note text.
- For a feature that appears in several views, the symbol is shown only in the view where the tolerance is dimensioned.

6.3.3 On 3D models with product and manufacturing information, the class is stored as an attribute of the annotation and is displayed as a text label (S, K or F) with the same placement rules. If the drawing and the model disagree, the supplier asks the Aurelia design engineer to clarify before production tooling is ordered.

6.3.4 Each drawing carries a special characteristics table in the title block area. The table lists the characteristic number, class, nominal, tolerance and the reference zone on the sheet. A drawing with ten or more special characteristics also carries the table as a separate sheet.

6.3.5 Example: a brake caliper bracket drawing lists characteristic 7 as a bore diameter of 32.000 mm with a tolerance of +0.016/0 mm, Class S, zone C4. In the control plan this appears as characteristic 7, Class S, with a 100% air-gauge check at 0.001 mm resolution.

## 6.4 Control plans

6.4.1 The supplier writes a control plan for each part family, covering three stages: prototype, pre-launch and production. The plan follows the process flow step by step, so that each operation has a matching row.

6.4.2 Every row of the control plan states the following.

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| Operation | Number and name from the process flow |
| Machine and tool | Equipment, fixture, tool number |
| Characteristic | Product characteristic or process parameter, with the drawing characteristic number |
| Class | S, K, F or supplier symbol, or blank |
| Specification | Nominal and tolerance, or limits |
| Evaluation method | Gauge, test equipment, with the gauge number from the measurement system plan |
| Sample size and frequency | For example, 5 parts every hour, or 100% |
| Control method | Chart, error-proofing, setup check, visual standard |
| Reaction plan | Short action steps, or the reference to the reaction table in 6.5 |

6.4.3 Every Class S characteristic has at least one control that is independent of the operator, such as error-proofing, an automated gauge or a machine monitor. The error-proofing device is tested at the start of each shift with a known bad part or a simulated failure, and the test is recorded.

6.4.4 The control plan is consistent with the process FMEA. Every failure mode with a severity rating of 9 or 10, or with severity 7 or 8 and occurrence above 3, has a row in the control plan with a prevention or detection control. The supplier reviews the control plan against the process FMEA at least every 12 months and after any customer complaint or internal escape.

6.4.5 A control plan is written in a form that operators can understand. Rows that apply on the shop floor are shown at the workstation in the language of the operators, with photographs of acceptable and unacceptable examples.

## 6.5 Reaction plans

6.5.1 A reaction plan defines what the operator and the supervisor do when a control signals a problem. It is written before production starts and trained to every operator at the station.

6.5.2 Reaction steps follow a fixed pattern.

| Step | Action | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Stop the process or the machine cycle at the point of detection; mark the last good part | Immediately |
| 2 | Segregate the suspect parts: all parts since the last good check, plus the part in the gauge | Within 15 minutes |
| 3 | Tell the shift supervisor and, for Class S or K, the quality engineer on duty | Within 15 minutes |
| 4 | Sort suspect parts with 100% inspection by an inspector who is not the operator | Before the parts leave the area |
| 5 | Find the cause and correct the process; run a confirmation sample of 5 consecutive parts | Same shift |
| 6 | Restart and record the event on the chart or in the log | After confirmation |

6.5.3 For Class S, the supplier's quality manager is told within 2 hours. When any parts have left the plant, the quality manager decides on a recall of material from the supply chain, including any parts already shipped to Aurelia, and contacts the Aurelia SQE without delay.

6.5.4 After a chart signal of two or more out-of-control points within one shift, the process is not restarted until a process engineer has signed the log.

6.5.5 Reaction plans never allow an operator to adjust a Class S process without a supervisor present, and never allow parts that are suspect to be reworked unless the rework instruction is part of the control plan.

6.5.6 Every quarter, the supplier counts reaction plan events by characteristic and shows the top five in its quality review. A characteristic with more than three events in a quarter receives a corrective action to its process.

## 7. Packaging, labelling and identification

4.1 Purpose and scope

This section sets out how suppliers of production material to Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") must pack, label and identify parts so that they arrive undamaged, clean, traceable and ready for line-side use. It applies to all series-production parts, spare parts and pre-series parts delivered to the plants at Modena, Cassino and Kragujevac, and to the Aurelia distribution centre at Piacenza. Where a part has its own packaging specification, that specification takes precedence over the general rules below for the items it covers.

The supplier is responsible for the packaging of its own parts and for the packaging of its sub-suppliers. Aurelia does not accept packaging damage, label errors or contamination as a justification for late delivery or for sorting costs.

4.2 Packaging planning

4.2.1 Each supplier must prepare a packaging plan for every part number before the first pre-series delivery. The plan states the container type, the quantity per container, the stacking arrangement, the protective inserts and the expected transport route.

4.2.2 Standard quantities per container are set jointly with Aurelia Logistics Planning. The target is a container that one operator can handle safely and that matches the consumption of the line for between 1 and 4 hours. Containers that exceed 15 kg gross weight for manual handling must be agreed explicitly with the plant ergonomics team.

4.2.3 Mixed-part containers are not permitted unless the packaging plan shows separate compartments and each compartment carries its own label.

4.2.4 The supplier must run a transport test on new packaging, simulating at least 600 km of road transport, and keep the results available for review on request.

4.3 Returnable containers

4.3.1 Aurelia operates a pool of returnable containers. Pool containers remain the property of Aurelia or of the pool operator named in the packaging plan. Suppliers must not use them for other customers, modify them, or fill them with parts other than those assigned.

4.3.2 The supplier is responsible for the containers on its premises. Each supplier receives a monthly container account statement showing issued, received and returned quantities by container type. Differences must be reported to Aurelia within 10 working days of receiving the statement.

4.3.3 Empty containers must be returned within 14 calendar days of receipt. Containers held longer than 30 days without a documented reason are treated as lost.

4.3.4 Damaged or lost containers are charged at the replacement values shown below. A reasonable allowance for normal wear is built into the pool, so no charge applies to containers with wear that does not affect function.

| Container type | Dimensions (L x W x H, mm) | Typical load capacity (kg) | Replacement value (EUR) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small load carrier, type K1 | 400 x 300 x 175 | 15 | 14 |
| Small load carrier, type K2 | 600 x 400 x 280 | 20 | 24 |
| Large load carrier, type G1 | 1,200 x 800 x 970 | 600 | 185 |
| Pallet frame, type G2 | 1,200 x 1,000 x 1,000 | 800 | 240 |
| Special rack (part-specific) | per drawing | per drawing | per rack record |

4.3.5 Containers must be stacked only to the height stated in the packaging plan. For type G1 the maximum is three high in the warehouse and two high in transport, unless the plan states otherwise.

4.3.6 Expendable packaging (cartons, wooden pallets, film) may be used only where the packaging plan allows it. The supplier bears the cost of disposal at Aurelia sites when it has used expendable packaging without agreement, at a flat rate of EUR 38 per pallet.

4.4 Labels

4.4.1 Every container, and every pallet or rack carrying several containers, must bear one part label and one transport label. Labels are printed on white adhesive stock, size 210 x 148 mm for large containers and 105 x 148 mm for small containers.

4.4.2 Labels are placed on the upper right-hand corner of the long side that faces the aisle, between 400 mm and 1,200 mm above the floor. Where two labels are required on a single pallet, they are placed on two adjacent sides.

4.4.3 Label content is mandatory in the order shown in the table below. Missing, truncated or incorrect fields are treated as label errors.

| Field | Content | Format | Barcode required |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Aurelia part number | 10 characters, alphanumeric | Yes |
| 2 | Part description | up to 40 characters | No |
| 3 | Quantity in container | integer, up to 5 digits | Yes |
| 4 | Supplier name and supplier code | 5-digit Aurelia code | Yes |
| 5 | Delivery note number | up to 12 characters | Yes |
| 6 | Batch or lot number | up to 12 characters | Yes |
| 7 | Production date | DD.MM.YYYY | No |
| 8 | Design level index | 2 characters | No |
| 9 | Unique container serial number | 18 digits | Yes |
| 10 | Plant and unloading point | per order | No |

4.4.5 Each container serial number is unique for at least 10 years and must never be reused for another container.

4.4.6 The text on labels must be legible without aids from a distance of 50 cm, using a minimum font height of 3 mm for the part number and 2 mm for other fields. Handwritten corrections are not allowed.

4.5 Barcodes

4.5.1 Barcodes must be printed in Code 128 symbology or as a two-dimensional data matrix code, as stated in the packaging plan. The narrowest element (X-dimension) must be at least 0.38 mm for linear codes.

4.5.2 Each barcode must be surrounded by a quiet zone of at least 10 times the X-dimension and must be printed with a contrast of at least 70 percent between bars and background.

4.5.3 Aurelia verifies code quality on receipt using handheld scanners. A barcode that fails to scan on the first attempt in three tries is recorded as a label error. The supplier must check code quality at the point of printing, and a quality grade of B or better is the target for every label.

4.5.4 The data content of the barcode must match the printed text exactly. Where barcode and text differ, Aurelia will rely on the barcode for booking the goods, and the supplier remains responsible for any resulting stock error.

4.6 Traceability and identification of parts

4.6.1 Each part must carry a permanent identification mark where the drawing requires it, showing supplier code, production date or lot code, and cavity or tool number where relevant.

4.6.2 The lot number on the label must allow the supplier to identify, within 4 hours of a request, the production line, shift, raw material batch and inspection records for the parts. Aurelia may test this ability without notice, up to twice per year per supplier.

4.6.3 First-in, first-out must be maintained at the supplier and shown by the production date. Containers with more than one lot must be avoided; if unavoidable, each lot must be physically separated and labelled separately.

4.6.4 Parts released after rework or sorting must be clearly distinguished. The supplier adds a green round sticker of 25 mm diameter to each container, with the date of rework and the name or initials of the person responsible.

4.7 Cleanliness and protection

4.7.1 Parts must arrive clean, dry and free of corrosion, dust and loose particles. Where the drawing or packaging plan states a technical cleanliness requirement, the supplier must prove it by a particle analysis and report the maximum particle size and the total particle mass per 1,000 cm² of inspected surface.

4.7.2 Typical cleanliness classes used by Aurelia are shown below. The class applicable to each part is stated on the drawing.

| Cleanliness class | Maximum particle size (µm) | Maximum particle mass (mg per 1,000 cm²) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 200 | 0.5 | Fuel and brake system components |
| B | 400 | 1.5 | Transmission and drive components |
| C | 600 | 4.0 | Interior and chassis parts |
| D | no requirement | visual only | Exterior trim and general parts |

4.7.3 Clean parts must be packed in sealed bags or covered with lids immediately after final cleaning. Packing must take place in a clearly marked area where the air is monitored and where personnel wear suitable clothing.

4.7.4 Protective inserts must be made of materials that do not shed fibres or leave residues on the part surface. Inserts are replaced when they show wear or contamination.

4.7.5 Containers must be stored indoors at the supplier, off the floor, and at a temperature between 5 °C and 40 °C. Outdoor storage of packed parts for more than 24 hours is not allowed.

4.8 Packaging and labelling deficiencies

4.8.1 Aurelia records packaging and label errors found at goods receipt and at the line. Each supplier receives a monthly figure for errors per 1,000 containers delivered. The target is 2.0 or fewer.

4.8.2 A handling charge of EUR 45 per delivery applies to labelling errors that need rework on site, plus EUR 1.20 per container re-labelled. Where Aurelia must repack parts, the supplier is charged EUR 52 per hour of labour, with a minimum charge of one hour.

4.8.3 Where a supplier exceeds 5.0 errors per 1,000 containers in two consecutive months, the supplier must send a written action plan within 10 working days and take part in a packaging review with the Aurelia logistics quality engineer at the supplier site.

## 8. Logistics, delivery performance and scheduling

5.1 Purpose and scope

This section describes how Aurelia communicates demand to its suppliers and how it expects suppliers to plan, ship and report deliveries. Its aim is a stable, predictable flow of material to the Modena, Cassino and Kragujevac plants and to the Piacenza distribution centre, with minimal stock and no line stoppage caused by missing parts.

The supplier is responsible for the delivery of the right part, in the right quantity, in the right packaging, at the right place, in the agreed time window. It remains responsible for the delivery performance of its carriers and its sub-suppliers.

5.2 Electronic data interchange

5.2.1 Every series supplier must exchange order data electronically with Aurelia. Suppliers need a tested connection before the first series delivery. The Aurelia EDI team performs a connection test that takes about 5 working days and requires the supplier to send and receive each message type at least three times without error.

5.2.2 The messages in use are listed below.

| Message | Direction | Purpose | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery forecast | Aurelia to supplier | Medium-term demand by week | Weekly, Monday by 08:00 |
| Delivery schedule (call-off) | Aurelia to supplier | Firm quantities by day and time window | Daily, by 14:00 |
| Just-in-sequence call | Aurelia to supplier | Vehicle sequence for selected modules | Every 20 to 60 minutes |
| Advance shipping notice | Supplier to Aurelia | Content of each shipment | At departure |
| Receipt confirmation | Aurelia to supplier | Booked goods | Within 2 hours of unloading |
| Invoice data | Supplier to Aurelia | Billing information | Monthly, by working day 3 |

5.2.3 The advance shipping notice must be sent no later than 15 minutes after the vehicle leaves the supplier, and for shipments with a transit time under 2 hours it must be sent before departure. The data in the notice must match the labels on the containers: part number, quantity, delivery note number and container serial numbers.

5.2.4 The supplier must monitor its EDI connection every day and respond to a transmission failure within 1 hour. If the failure lasts more than 4 hours, the supplier and the Aurelia EDI team agree on a fallback by telephone and e-mail. Fallback documents are accepted only for a limited time and the supplier must clear the problem within 2 working days.

5.3 Forecasts and capacity

5.3.1 Forecasts are non-binding indications of expected demand. They cover 26 weeks and are refreshed every week. The first 4 weeks are described as the frozen horizon, and the following 4 weeks as the flexible horizon.

5.3.2 Suppliers must plan capacity to cover the forecast peak plus a flexibility reserve. The reserve is a minimum of 10 percent above the forecast for weeks 5 to 8 and a minimum of 20 percent above the forecast for weeks 1 to 4 compared with the quantities in the weekly delivery schedule issued 4 weeks earlier.

5.3.3 Each supplier must report its capacity status every quarter, and at once if it cannot meet the forecast quantities. The report states the weekly capacity per part number, the bottleneck, and the actions planned.

5.3.4 Before a model launch, the supplier takes part in a capacity run of 8 hours at the planned peak rate. The target for the run is a demonstrated rate of at least 105 percent of the peak daily quantity.

5.4 Delivery schedules and delivery windows

5.4.1 The delivery schedule is binding for the quantity and the date it states. The supplier must deliver exactly the called quantity, with no allowance for over-delivery or under-delivery unless Aurelia agrees in writing in advance. A delivery counts as on time only when the full quantity arrives inside the window.

5.4.2 A delivery window is the period in which the vehicle must arrive at the unloading point. Windows are set per plant and per unloading point. The standard windows are given below.

| Delivery type | Window length | Earliest arrival | Latest arrival |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard truck delivery, Modena | 60 minutes | 30 minutes before window start | At window end |
| Standard truck delivery, Cassino | 60 minutes | 30 minutes before window start | At window end |
| Standard truck delivery, Kragujevac | 120 minutes | 60 minutes before window start | At window end |
| Line-side delivery (daily multiple) | 30 minutes | 10 minutes before window start | At window end |
| Just-in-sequence delivery | 10 minutes | none | 10 minutes before required line time |

5.4.3 A vehicle that arrives before the earliest arrival time may be refused or made to wait. Waiting time is not charged to Aurelia. A vehicle that arrives after the window is unloaded as soon as capacity allows, and the delivery is recorded as late.

5.4.4 The supplier plans transit time with a safety margin appropriate to the route. For the road route from northern Italy to Modena, a minimum buffer of 3 hours is expected. For the route from Kragujevac suppliers to the plant, the buffer is a minimum of 6 hours, owing to border formalities.

5.4.5 Stock requirements: the supplier holds a finished goods safety stock sufficient for 3 working days for standard parts, and 5 working days for parts with long transport routes, unless the supply agreement says otherwise.

5.5 Delivery performance measurement

5.5.1 Aurelia measures delivery performance by supplier and plant every month. The main indicators are on-time delivery, quantity accuracy and the number of line interruptions caused by the supplier.

5.5.2 The targets and the escalation levels are as follows.

| Indicator | Target | Level 1 (written notice) | Level 2 (action plan) | Level 3 (management review) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| On-time delivery | 98.5 % or above | below 98.5 % | below 96.0 % | below 92.0 % |
| Quantity accuracy | 99.5 % or above | below 99.5 % | below 98.5 % | below 97.0 % |
| Line interruption minutes | 0 | any | above 20 min per month | above 60 min per month |
| ASN accuracy | 99.0 % or above | below 99.0 % | below 97.5 % | below 95.0 % |

5.5.3 At Level 2, the supplier presents an action plan within 5 working days. At Level 3, the supplier's plant manager attends a review at Aurelia and a monthly follow-up is held until the indicators have been back at target for 3 consecutive months.

5.5.4 The supplier receives its scorecard by working day 5 of the following month. The supplier may contest a data point within 10 working days, with evidence such as gate records or transport documents.

5.6 Premium freight

5.6.1 Premium freight means any transport at a higher cost or higher speed than the standard transport route, including special vehicles, courier shipments and air freight, used to recover a delivery.

5.6.2 Where premium freight is needed because of a cause within the supplier's control, the supplier bears all costs. These causes include late production, insufficient capacity, quality problems at the supplier, missing packaging, and late or wrong shipping data.

5.6.3 Where the cause is a change in Aurelia demand beyond the flexibility range in clause 5.3.2, Aurelia bears the cost, but only if the supplier has told the Aurelia planner as soon as it knew it could not meet the call.

5.6.4 Premium freight must be authorised in advance by the Aurelia logistics planner, who issues an authorisation number. Costs for which no number was issued will not be reimbursed, but will be charged to the supplier if the cause lies with the supplier.

5.6.5 Each supplier receives a monthly premium freight report. The target is zero events. A supplier with more than 3 premium freight events in a quarter, or with premium freight costs above EUR 5,000 in a quarter, must present a root cause analysis and a corrective action plan to Aurelia within 10 working days.

5.7 Line stoppages

5.7.1 If a supplier failure stops or is likely to stop an Aurelia line, the supplier must call the Aurelia plant logistics desk at once, at any hour, using the emergency number in the supplier contact sheet.

5.7.2 For each full minute of line stoppage caused by the supplier, Aurelia charges the line cost shown in the supply agreement. As a general guide, a stoppage at Modena final assembly is charged at EUR 410 per minute, at Cassino at EUR 285 per minute, and at Kragujevac at EUR 190 per minute. Aurelia may charge additional costs it can show, such as overtime and rescheduling.

5.7.3 After each stoppage, the supplier sends a short written explanation within 24 hours and a full analysis within 10 working days.

## 9. Nonconforming product and problem solving

6.1 Purpose and scope

This section defines how Aurelia and its suppliers handle nonconforming product, from the moment a problem is found to the moment its cause has been removed. It applies to production parts, service parts and tooling delivered to any Aurelia site, and to nonconformities found at Aurelia, at logistics partners, at dealers and in the field. The aim is to protect the customer, to contain the problem quickly and to prevent it from happening again.

6.2 Definitions

6.2.1 A nonconformity is any failure of a part, a process, a label or a delivery to meet the drawing, specification, order or this manual.

6.2.2 A complaint is Aurelia's formal notice to the supplier of a nonconformity. Complaints are sent electronically through the Aurelia supplier portal and by e-mail to the contact named in the supplier contact sheet.

6.2.3 Parts per million (ppm) is the number of nonconforming parts per million parts delivered, calculated by plant and by supplier on a rolling 12-month basis.

6.3 Complaint handling and response times

6.3.1 Aurelia assigns each complaint a severity class based on the effect of the problem. Class 1 means a safety-relevant or legally relevant effect, or a stoppage of a vehicle line. Class 2 means a defect that the customer would notice, or a part that cannot be assembled. Class 3 means a minor defect with no effect on function or assembly.

6.3.2 The supplier must meet the response times below. The time starts when the complaint is sent through the portal. Working days are counted by the Italian calendar, and for Class 1 the time is counted in clock hours, including weekends.

| Step | Class 1 | Class 2 | Class 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge complaint and name a contact | 2 hours | 1 working day | 2 working days |
| Containment at supplier, in transit and at Aurelia | 24 hours | 2 working days | 5 working days |
| Initial analysis and interim report (D1 to D3) | 48 hours | 3 working days | 5 working days |
| Root cause and corrective action plan (D4 to D6) | 10 working days | 15 working days | 20 working days |
| Final report (D7 to D8) | 30 working days | 40 working days | 60 working days |

6.3.3 Where a complete root cause analysis cannot be done within the time, the supplier must tell Aurelia in writing before the deadline, giving the reason and a new date. A later date is accepted only by Aurelia's quality engineer, and a maximum of one extension of 10 working days is allowed for each report.

6.3.4 The supplier must return suspect parts it holds, and parts returned by Aurelia for analysis, with a clear note of the complaint number. Aurelia provides return parts for analysis within 5 working days of a request, in quantities up to 10 parts.

6.4 Containment

6.4.1 The supplier must immediately check all stock at its premises, in transit, at logistics partners and at Aurelia, and must separate all suspect parts.

6.4.2 Containment measures must be effective for the full affected population. The supplier states the first good lot, shown by a lot number, serial number or date, and Aurelia uses this information to check its stock.

6.4.3 Until the cause is removed, the supplier must carry out additional inspection of 100 percent of each delivery, and must mark each container as inspected using the green sticker described in the packaging rules. Aurelia may ask for the inspection to be carried out by an external service at the supplier's expense, where the supplier's own containment is not sufficient. This is known as controlled shipping, and it lasts until the supplier proves 3 consecutive lots, or 4 weeks of delivery, without a recurrence.

6.4.4 Parts sorted or reworked at Aurelia, at a logistics partner or at a service provider on behalf of Aurelia are recorded by hours and quantity. These records are the basis for any charge.

6.5 Problem solving report format

6.5.1 Suppliers must report in the 8D format, using the Aurelia template available from the portal. Each report is written in English or Italian. Free-text sections must be specific. General statements such as "operator error" or "will pay more attention" are not acceptable causes or actions.

6.5.2 The content of each discipline is defined below.

| Discipline | Content required |
|---|---|
| D1 Team | Names, functions and contact details of at least three people, including a team leader with authority over the process |
| D2 Problem description | Is and is-not description: what, where, when, how many, with photographs and measured values |
| D3 Containment | Actions, stock quantities by location, responsible person, date and first good lot |
| D4 Root cause | Cause for occurrence and cause for escape, each with evidence from testing and a verified chain of why-questions |
| D5 Corrective actions | Chosen actions for both causes, with expected effect and planned date |
| D6 Implementation and verification | Evidence that the actions were put into place and measured effect, such as capability data and audit results |
| D7 Prevention | Transfer to similar parts, processes and plants; update of the risk analysis, control plan and work instructions |
| D8 Closing and recognition | Summary of the effect, lessons learned, and release of the team |

6.5.3 A report must show both why the defect was produced and why it was not detected before shipment. A report that deals with only one of these is returned to the supplier for revision and the response time continues to run.

6.5.4 Where a root cause analysis shows that the cause lies at a sub-supplier, the supplier remains Aurelia's single point of contact and is responsible for the sub-supplier's actions and for the cost.

6.5.5 Reports must be backed with data. Where a measuring system is part of the cause, the supplier provides evidence of the measuring system's repeatability and reproducibility, and the total variation attributed to the measuring system must be 10 percent or less of the tolerance.

6.6 Supplier performance indicators

6.6.1 Aurelia calculates the ppm value of each supplier every month. Parts found nonconforming at Aurelia, on the line or in the field count towards the figure, as long as the supplier is responsible.

6.6.2 Targets and escalation steps are as follows.

| Supplier ppm (rolling 12 months) | Rating | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 20 | Excellent | None |
| 21 to 50 | Good | Routine monitoring |
| 51 to 150 | Needs improvement | Improvement plan within 15 working days |
| 151 to 500 | Unsatisfactory | Quality review at the supplier and weekly status meetings |
| Above 500 | Critical | Management review at Aurelia and controlled shipping |

6.6.3 For each supplier, Aurelia also tracks the number of complaints, the rate of reports sent on time (target 95 percent or higher), and the number of repeat complaints, which are complaints with the same root cause within 24 months. Each repeat complaint is reviewed by Aurelia's supplier quality manager.

6.7 Chargebacks

6.7.1 The supplier bears the costs that arise from nonconforming product for which it is responsible. Aurelia charges only costs that it can show with records.

6.7.2 The following cost elements and rates apply, unless the supply agreement states other values.

| Cost element | Rate or basis |
|---|---|
| Administrative handling fee per complaint, Class 3 | EUR 150 |
| Administrative handling fee per complaint, Class 2 | EUR 350 |
| Administrative handling fee per complaint, Class 1 | EUR 900 |
| Sorting and rework at Aurelia | EUR 52 per labour hour |
| Production line stoppage | Line cost per minute as stated in the logistics rules |
| Part cost of scrapped material | Purchase price of the part, plus 12 percent handling |
| External laboratory analysis | Cost invoiced to Aurelia, without addition |
| Late 8D report | EUR 100 per working day, up to a maximum of EUR 1,500 per report |

6.7.3 Aurelia sends the supplier a debit note with supporting records, such as hours, quantities and invoices. The supplier has 20 working days to object with evidence. A debit note without objection is settled with the next payment.

6.7.4 Costs arising from vehicle recalls, field actions and warranty claims are dealt with under the quality and warranty provisions of the supply agreement, and are not covered in this manual.

6.7.5 Where a supplier considers that a complaint is not justified, it must still meet the containment and acknowledgement times. A disagreement does not stop the clock. The supplier states its reasons with measurement data, and Aurelia's quality engineer reviews them within 5 working days.

6.8 Escalation and cooperation

6.8.1 If a supplier repeatedly fails to meet the response times in this section, Aurelia may ask for a review of the supplier's problem solving process on site. The review is carried out by the Aurelia supplier quality engineer and takes about one day.

6.8.2 Suppliers are asked to keep a standing team for problem solving, to train their employees in the 8D method, and to name a deputy for each contact, so that complaints sent outside normal hours, or during holidays, are dealt with without delay.

6.8.3 Aurelia and the supplier hold a joint quality meeting at least twice a year to review the ppm trend, repeat complaints and open actions, and to agree the priorities for the following six months.

## 10. Warranty and field returns

4.1 Purpose and scope

This section sets out how Aurelia Automobili S.p.A. ("Aurelia") handles parts returned from the field, how no-trouble-found (NTF) results are analysed, how warranty costs are recovered from suppliers, and how return parts are stored and released. It applies to every supplier of series-production parts, service parts and accessories fitted to vehicles sold under the Aurelia brand, including parts supplied through a sub-tier source. The aim is to find the true cause of each field failure quickly, to charge cost to the party that caused it, and to keep the evidence needed to prove it.

4.2 Warranty periods and coverage

Unless the supply agreement states otherwise, the supplier's liability follows the vehicle warranty that Aurelia gives to the end customer, plus a supplier tail. The standard periods are shown below.

| Part category | Vehicle warranty | Supplier liability period | Mileage limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body, trim and chassis hardware | 36 months | 48 months | 80,000 km |
| Powertrain mechanical | 48 months | 60 months | 100,000 km |
| Electrical and electronic modules | 36 months | 54 months | 90,000 km |
| Battery and high-voltage components | 96 months | 108 months | 160,000 km |
| Service and accessory parts | 24 months | 30 months | not applicable |

The period starts on the vehicle's in-service date as recorded in the Aurelia dealer system. For service parts it starts on the date of fitting. A part replaced under warranty carries the remaining period of the original warranty or 12 months, whichever is longer.

4.3 Return flow

A dealer who replaces a part under warranty tags it with the claim number, vehicle identification number, mileage and fault description. Tagged parts are sent to one of three Aurelia return centres: Modena, Brno or Leipzig. The return centre carries out the following steps.

1. Goods receipt and scan of the part label within 2 working days of arrival.
2. Visual check and photograph of the part as received, before any cleaning.
3. Assignment to a supplier and a part family, using the part number and date code.
4. Release to the supplier or to the Aurelia analysis laboratory, depending on the part class.

The supplier receives a return notice through the supplier portal within 5 working days of goods receipt. Parts must not be cleaned, bench-tested or opened by anyone before the analysis plan has been agreed, because this destroys evidence of the failure condition.

4.4 Analysis and response times

The supplier must analyse each returned part and give a written finding. Targets are set by the part class and are counted in calendar days from the date the part is made available to the supplier.

| Return class | Description | Initial finding | Full analysis with cause | Corrective action plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Safety-relevant or vehicle immobilised | 3 days | 10 days | 15 days |
| B | Function lost, vehicle drivable | 7 days | 20 days | 30 days |
| C | Comfort, noise or appearance | 10 days | 30 days | 45 days |

The finding must state the failure mode, the failure location on the part, the process step or design feature that caused it, and the evidence used. Statements such as "cause unknown" or "customer misuse" are accepted only when backed by measured data and photographs. A supplier that needs more time must ask in writing before the target date and give a reason. Aurelia may grant up to 10 extra days.

4.5 No-trouble-found analysis

A part is recorded as NTF when the supplier's first bench test does not reproduce the fault the dealer described. NTF is not a final result. It is a trigger for a deeper analysis, because intermittent faults often hide behind a normal bench result. The following steps apply to every NTF part.

1. Repeat the test under the conditions recorded in the claim: temperature, supply voltage, vibration and humidity where known.
2. Read stored fault memory and operating-hour counters from the part, where it has them.
3. Run a thermal cycling test of at least 50 cycles between -40 °C and +105 °C, or the cycle defined in the part specification if it is more demanding.
4. Inspect connectors, seals and solder joints under at least 20x magnification, and perform a cross-section where an indication is seen.
5. Compare the part with 5 retained samples from the same production week.

If these steps still give no fault, the supplier submits an NTF record with the test data attached. Aurelia reviews the record together with vehicle data, including the diagnostic trouble codes and the repair history of the vehicle. Where the vehicle data points to a software, wiring or diagnostic-logic issue in the vehicle, the cost stays with Aurelia. Where it points to the part, the supplier carries the cost even though the bench test was normal.

Aurelia tracks NTF as a rate for each part family. The NTF rate is the number of returned parts with no fault found divided by all returned parts of that family in a rolling 6-month period. The reference value is 30 percent for electronic modules and 15 percent for mechanical parts. A supplier whose NTF rate exceeds the reference value for 2 consecutive months must submit a test-capability review, covering test coverage, equipment accuracy and the operator method. A repeat-fit rule also applies: if the same part number is returned from the same vehicle twice with an NTF result, the third return is analysed jointly at the Aurelia laboratory.

4.6 Cost recovery

When the analysis or the vehicle data shows that the part caused the failure, Aurelia recovers cost from the supplier. The recoverable items are listed below.

| Cost element | Basis of charge | Example rate |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement part | Purchase price at the time of supply | Price list value |
| Dealer labour | Official repair time multiplied by the dealer labour rate | EUR 78 per hour |
| Diagnosis time | Actual time up to a cap | Up to 1.5 hours per claim |
| Transport and towing | Invoice value | At cost |
| Return handling and analysis | Fixed fee per part | EUR 42 per part |
| Goodwill and courtesy vehicle | Share of invoice value when the part is the cause | 100 percent |

For an individual claim, Aurelia sends a debit note once the cause has been assigned. For a batch problem, where a defect affects a population of vehicles, the supplier carries the full cost of the containment and service action. Aurelia and the supplier agree a recovery estimate, which is then adjusted against actual claims every quarter. A supplier may dispute a charge within 30 days of the debit note. The dispute must include data showing that the part was not the cause. Charges not disputed within 30 days are settled by deduction from the next payment run.

A flat rate applies to low-value claims. Where the total cost of a claim is below EUR 60, Aurelia and the supplier may agree a monthly lump sum based on the claim rate of the previous 12 months, so that individual analysis is not needed for each part.

4.7 Return parts: storage, release and disposal

Return centres keep every returned part, in its tagged bag and in a controlled rack, for at least 12 months after goods receipt. Parts with safety relevance (class A) are held for 36 months. Parts released to a supplier must be returned to Aurelia within 30 days unless Aurelia states in writing that the supplier may keep them. A part used for destructive testing must be photographed in each step, and the remains must be returned or disposed of as instructed. The chain of custody, from dealer to return centre to supplier and back, is recorded in the portal with date, name and condition. A part with a broken chain of custody cannot support a dispute by either party. Parts are scrapped only after the retention period has ended and after the supplier has confirmed there is no further analysis planned.

## 11. Supplier performance scorecard and escalation

5.1 Purpose

Aurelia measures every active supplier on the same set of indicators so that quality, delivery and responsiveness can be compared fairly and acted on early. This section describes the scorecard, the key performance indicators (KPIs), the levels of escalation, and the new business hold. The scorecard is calculated monthly and published on the supplier portal by the 8th working day of the following month. Suppliers are expected to review it each month and to react to any indicator that is outside the target.

5.2 Scorecard structure

The overall score is a number from 0 to 100. It combines five groups of indicators. The weights below apply to production suppliers. For service-parts suppliers the delivery group carries 30 points and the cost group carries 5 points.

| Group | Weight (points) | Main indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Delivered quality | 35 | Parts per million (PPM) defective, number of line stoppages |
| Field quality | 25 | Claims per 1,000 vehicles, warranty cost per vehicle |
| Delivery | 20 | On-time delivery rate, premium freight events |
| Responsiveness | 12 | Timeliness of corrective action, quality of analysis |
| Cost and commercial | 8 | Price-reduction delivery, invoice accuracy |

A supplier receives a rating based on the total score: A for 90 to 100 points, B for 75 to 89, C for 60 to 74, and D for below 60.

5.3 Key performance indicators and targets

The table below gives the standard targets. Individual agreements may set tighter values for critical parts.

| KPI | Definition | Target | Limit for escalation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incoming PPM | Defective parts found at Aurelia plants per million delivered, rolling 3 months | 20 or below | Above 100 |
| Line stoppage | Minutes of assembly line stopped because of the supplier part | 0 | More than 15 minutes in a month |
| Field claim rate | Claims per 1,000 vehicles in the first 12 months in service | 1.5 or below | Above 4.0 |
| On-time delivery | Deliveries within the agreed window of plus or minus 1 day | 98.5 percent or above | Below 95.0 percent |
| Premium freight | Number of expedited shipments caused by the supplier | 0 | 2 or more in a quarter |
| Corrective action timeliness | Percentage of required responses delivered by the due date | 95 percent or above | Below 80 percent |
| Analysis quality | Score from 1 to 5 given by Aurelia engineers to each analysis | 4.0 or above | Below 3.0 |

PPM is calculated on the number of parts delivered, not the number of deliveries. A defect found at an Aurelia plant and traced to a sub-tier source counts against the direct supplier. Defects that are the result of an Aurelia drawing error or incorrect handling at the plant are removed from the calculation after review by the Aurelia supplier quality engineer.

5.4 Levels of escalation

Escalation has four levels. A supplier enters a level when a trigger is met and leaves it when the exit conditions have been met. The levels are intended to increase management attention in proportion to the risk to Aurelia production and customers.

| Level | Name | Typical trigger | Required supplier action | Aurelia action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Standard | Score 75 or above, no KPI over the limit | Normal monthly review | Annual review meeting |
| 1 | Watch | Score 60 to 74, or any one KPI over the limit for 2 months | Written improvement plan within 10 working days | Monthly call with the supplier quality manager |
| 2 | Formal action | Score below 60 for 2 months, or a repeat issue with the same cause | Plan led by the supplier's plant manager; weekly status to Aurelia | On-site review within 20 working days; increased inspection at the supplier plant or the Aurelia plant |
| 3 | Executive | No measurable improvement after 90 days at level 2, or a serious customer-relevant failure | Plan led by the supplier's chief executive; resident quality support | Meeting with the Aurelia purchasing director; costs of extra inspection charged to the supplier |

A single event can move a supplier directly to level 2 or 3 where it has caused a vehicle stop-sale, a safety-relevant field action, or more than 24 hours of lost vehicle production.

5.5 Additional inspection

From level 2 onward, Aurelia may require one of two controlled-shipping stages. In the first stage the supplier adds its own extra inspection at its plant and sends the inspection records with each delivery. In the second stage a third-party inspector is placed at the supplier plant or at an Aurelia sorting site. The supplier pays the cost of this inspector, at the rate of EUR 46 per hour as of the date of this manual, plus any travel cost. Controlled shipping continues until the supplier has delivered 3 consecutive months with no defect found by the extra inspection and a PPM value below the limit.

5.6 New business hold

A new business hold stops Aurelia from awarding new parts, new vehicle programmes or additional volume to the supplier. It does not stop deliveries or service of existing parts. The hold rules are as follows.

1. A supplier at level 2 is placed on a conditional hold. New quotations are accepted, but no award is made without the written decision of the purchasing director and the head of quality.
2. A supplier at level 3 is placed on a full hold. No quotation request is sent, and the supplier is removed from the sourcing lists for new programmes.
3. A supplier with a rating of D in 2 consecutive quarters is placed on a conditional hold, even if no escalation trigger has been met.
4. The hold applies to all Aurelia plants and to all product lines of the supplier legal entity. Where a quality issue is confined to one site of the supplier, the hold applies to that site only.

The supplier is told of the hold by letter from the head of purchasing and is given the reason, the data and the exit conditions.

5.7 Exit from escalation and hold

To leave level 3 and move to level 2, the supplier must show 3 consecutive months with all KPIs inside the limits. To leave level 2 and move to level 1, 3 months with all KPIs inside the limits and a successful on-site review are required. Leaving level 1 requires 3 months with a score of 75 or above. A new business hold is lifted when the supplier has returned to level 1 or lower and has held that level for at least 6 months. Aurelia keeps a record of every change of level and the reasons for it. A supplier may ask for a review of its score within 15 working days of publication by sending evidence of a data error to the supplier quality engineer; the corrected score is published in the following month.

## 12. Traceability, records retention and audits

6.1 Purpose and principle

If a defect is found in the field, Aurelia must be able to say within hours which vehicles contain the affected parts, and the supplier must be able to say which material, machine, people and process conditions were behind them. This section sets the minimum rules for lot traceability, the retention periods for quality records, and the schedule of audits that Aurelia carries out at supplier sites. The rules apply to the supplier and are to be passed on to its sub-tier sources for any element that appears in the traceability chain.

6.2 Lot traceability requirements

Every part must carry a mark that links it to a production lot. The mark is applied to the part, or where this is not possible to the smallest package, and must stay legible for the life of the part. The mark shows at least the supplier code, the Aurelia part number, the date of manufacture and a lot or serial number. Parts of safety relevance and electronic modules must be marked with a unique serial number in a machine-readable 2D code.

A lot is the quantity produced from one batch of raw material, on one machine or line, in one setup and in no more than one shift. Lot size must not exceed the limits below unless Aurelia agrees a different figure.

| Part type | Maximum lot size | Marking | Traceability depth |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safety-relevant parts | 1 part (serial) | 2D code on part | Raw material, process data, test result, operator |
| Electronic modules | 1 part (serial) | 2D code on part | Component lots, board lot, test result |
| Machined and cast parts | 1,000 parts or 1 shift | Date code and lot on part | Raw material heat or batch, machine, tool change |
| Plastic and trim parts | 2,500 parts or 1 shift | Date code on part or box | Granulate batch, tool, machine |
| Fasteners and standard parts | 10,000 parts | Label on box | Supplier lot, delivery note |

Traceability must work in two directions. Forward tracing identifies every delivery that contains a given raw material lot or component lot. Backward tracing identifies, from a part number and date code, the lots of material and components used. The supplier must prove this ability on request. The required response time for a trace query is 4 hours for the first answer and 24 hours for a full list of affected parts and the stock location of each. A supplier must also run its own trace test twice a year using a randomly selected finished part, and the exercise must reach all the way to raw material in no more than 8 hours.

6.3 Data linked to each lot

For each lot the supplier keeps a record that contains the following items.

1. Identification of the raw material or component lots, with the certificate of the material source.
2. The machine or line, the tool or fixture number, and the date and time of setup.
3. Process parameters that are specified as critical on the control plan, with values or a statement that the values were inside limits.
4. Results of in-process checks and final tests, including measured values for characteristics marked as special.
5. Identification of the rework or sorting performed, with quantity and date.
6. Quantities produced, scrapped, shipped, and the delivery notes used.

Paper or electronic records are both accepted. Electronic records must be backed up at least daily, stored at a second location, and readable throughout the retention period even when software or equipment is replaced.

6.4 Records retention periods

The retention period is counted from the end of series supply of the part to Aurelia, unless a different start is shown in the table. If a record has a retention period of the end of supply plus a number of years, the supplier must keep the record until that date.

| Record type | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Lot traceability records for safety-relevant parts | End of supply plus 20 years |
| Lot traceability records for other parts | End of supply plus 15 years |
| Control plans, process flow diagrams and failure analysis of the process | End of supply plus 15 years |
| Final inspection and test results | 15 years from date of production |
| Measurement system and gauge calibration records | 10 years from date of calibration |
| Corrective action records and field return analyses | End of supply plus 15 years |
| Internal and external audit records | 10 years from date of the audit |
| Training and qualification records of operators on special processes | 10 years from end of employment or qualification |
| Delivery notes and packing lists | 10 years from date of delivery |
| Retained samples (see 6.5) | 3 years from date of production, or 5 years for safety-relevant parts |

If Aurelia opens a quality investigation or a legal matter, it may ask the supplier to keep certain records for longer. Such a request is made in writing, and the supplier must keep the named records until Aurelia releases them in writing. At the end of the retention period the supplier destroys the records in a way that prevents their reuse, and keeps a short log of what was destroyed and when.

6.5 Retained samples

For every production lot of safety-relevant parts the supplier keeps 5 retained samples. For other parts the supplier keeps 3 samples per week of production. The samples are stored in a labelled and controlled area at room conditions, and they are used for comparison with parts returned from the field. Retained samples must be made available to Aurelia within 5 working days of a request.

6.6 Audit schedule

Aurelia audits supplier sites on a schedule that depends on risk. The risk class is set by the supplier quality manager once a year using the scorecard rating, the criticality of the supplied parts and the result of the previous audit. The standard frequency is as follows.

| Risk class | Typical supplier | Process audit | System audit | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| High | Safety-relevant parts, level 2 or 3 escalation, rating D | Every 6 months | Every 12 months | 3 to 4 days |
| Medium | Electronic modules, rating C | Every 12 months | Every 24 months | 2 to 3 days |
| Low | Standard parts, rating A or B | Every 24 months | Every 36 months | 1 to 2 days |

A system audit examines the quality management system, including the control of records, the trace test and the handling of non-conforming material. A process audit follows a part through the production steps and checks the controls on the shop floor against the control plan. Audits are normally announced at least 15 working days in advance. Aurelia may also make an unannounced visit, with 24 hours notice, where a serious quality issue has occurred or where records have not been available in the time required.

6.7 Audit conduct and follow-up

Each audit ends with a closing meeting at which the auditor presents findings. Findings are graded as major, minor or observation. A major finding means a requirement is not met or a risk to the customer exists. The supplier must send an action plan within 10 working days of the closing meeting for a major finding and within 20 working days for a minor finding. Evidence that actions have been carried out must be delivered by the date set in the plan, which is normally within 90 days for a major finding. A supplier's audit score is included in the scorecard and affects its risk class for the following year. The supplier must give auditors access to production areas, test equipment, records and, with reasonable notice, to its own sub-tier sources that are named in the traceability chain.
