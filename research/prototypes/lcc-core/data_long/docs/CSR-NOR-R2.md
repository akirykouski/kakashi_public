# Nordhavn supplier requirements, rev 2 (excerpt)

## 1. Scope, applicability and order of precedence

1.1 Purpose of this manual

This manual sets out the customer specific requirements of Nordhavn Trucks AB ("Nordhavn") for organisations that supply production material, service parts, tooling or related services for Nordhavn trucks and vans. It supplements the quality management system requirements that each supplier already operates under its own certification. It does not replace them. Where Nordhavn asks for something beyond the baseline, this manual says so explicitly and gives the reason, the expected evidence and the contact responsible.

Nordhavn builds heavy trucks at its Södertälje plant (about 118 vehicles per day on two shifts), medium trucks and chassis cabs at its Gdańsk plant (about 64 per day) and light vans at its Örebro plant (about 210 per day). Because these plants share suppliers, a single set of requirements is used for all three.

1.2 Who the manual applies to

The manual applies to every legal entity that holds, or is being considered for, a purchase relationship with Nordhavn for the items listed in 1.3. It applies to the entity named on the purchase order and to every manufacturing site that produces, finishes, tests or packs the item. The supplier is responsible for making sure its own sub-tier suppliers meet the requirements that affect the product, and for passing those requirements down through its own purchasing terms.

The manual does not apply to suppliers of office supplies, canteen services, travel, or general facilities maintenance, unless they work on a production line or in a production area. Such suppliers follow the site access rules of the plant they visit.

1.3 Categories of supply covered

Suppliers are grouped into categories. The category determines how much of the manual applies and how often Nordhavn reviews the supplier. The group is set by Nordhavn Purchasing when the supplier is first entered in the supplier system, and the supplier is told its category in writing.

| Category | Description | Typical annual spend with Nordhavn (SEK) | Share of manual that applies |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Safety-relevant or function-critical production parts (brake components, steering parts, cab structure, electronic control units) | Above 40 million | Full |
| B | Other series production parts and assemblies | 8 to 40 million | Full, except the extended on-site audit programme |
| C | Standard catalogue parts, fasteners, consumables used in production | 1 to 8 million | Core requirements only |
| D | Service parts and accessories | Any | Core requirements plus packaging and labelling |
| E | Production tooling, gauges and test equipment | Any | Tooling and measurement requirements only |
| F | Logistics, sequencing, sorting and rework services | Any | Service requirements only |

The "core requirements" are those on supplier identification, quality planning, product and process evidence, measurement, nonconforming product handling, traceability and record retention. Each later chapter marks any requirement that applies to a smaller group of categories.

1.4 Relationship to purchase orders

A purchase order is the commercial and technical instruction to deliver. This manual is the standing quality framework that sits behind every purchase order. The two are read together in the following way.

1.4.1 The manual is incorporated by reference into each purchase order, framework agreement and call-off schedule that Nordhavn issues. The supplier does not need to sign it again for each order. Placing a delivery against an order is treated as acknowledgement that the supplier has the current edition of the manual and will follow it.

1.4.2 A purchase order may add requirements specific to one part, one programme or one delivery. Such additions apply only to that order and do not change how the manual reads for any other order.

1.4.3 The purchase order carries the quantity, price, delivery date, delivery address, part number and drawing level. The manual never overrides those commercial fields.

1.4.4 The manual carries the quality planning, evidence and audit requirements. Where a purchase order is silent on a quality topic, the manual applies in full.

1.4.5 A supplier that believes the manual and a purchase order cannot both be met must raise the question with the Nordhavn buyer named on the order before it ships. Delivering first and raising the question afterwards does not alter the order of precedence below.

1.5 Order of precedence

When two documents give different instructions on the same point, the higher item in the following list prevails. A supplier must not choose between them on its own judgement.

| Rank | Document | What it governs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Applicable law and regulation in the country of manufacture and the country of delivery | Legal duties of every party |
| 2 | The product drawing, model data and technical specification named on the purchase order | Product definition and acceptance criteria |
| 3 | Special requirements written on the purchase order for that order only | One-off or programme-specific instructions |
| 4 | This manual | Quality planning, evidence, audit and performance expectations |
| 5 | The framework agreement and general terms of purchase | Commercial and legal terms that are not quality topics |
| 6 | The supplier's own procedures and standard terms | Internal methods of the supplier |

The supplier's own standard terms of sale do not apply to Nordhavn orders, even if they are printed on an order acknowledgement, a delivery note or an invoice. Where the supplier's own procedure is stricter than this manual, the supplier follows its own procedure, because the manual sets a minimum.

1.6 Relationship to the supplier's certification and to other customers

A supplier is expected to hold a current third-party certificate of its quality management system, issued by a certification body that is itself accredited. Nordhavn accepts the certificate as evidence of the baseline system. A certificate is not evidence that the supplier meets the additional requirements in this manual, and Nordhavn audits against those additions separately.

Suppliers that serve other vehicle makers use one set of processes for all customers wherever possible. Nordhavn does not require a dedicated line, a dedicated team or a separate quality system. It does require that Nordhavn-specific requirements are visible in the supplier's own planning documents so that operators and inspectors can see them.

1.7 Language, units and format

The working language of the manual and of all supplier submissions is English. Drawings may remain in the language in which they were issued. Dimensions are in millimetres, mass in kilograms, torque in newton metres and temperature in degrees Celsius, unless the drawing states otherwise. Dates are written in the form year-month-day. Submissions are made through the Nordhavn Supplier Portal; email attachments are used only when the portal is unavailable and only when the buyer has agreed.

1.8 Retention and confidentiality

The supplier keeps quality records relating to Nordhavn products for the life of the vehicle programme plus 15 years, or for the period set by law if longer. Drawings, specifications and data supplied by Nordhavn remain the property of Nordhavn, are used only to fulfil Nordhavn orders and are not passed to a third party without the written consent of the Nordhavn buyer.

1.9 Contacts and interpretation

Questions about how the manual applies are sent first to the Supplier Quality Engineer assigned to the supplier. If that person cannot settle the point within 5 working days, the question moves to the Supplier Quality Manager of the relevant plant, and then to the Head of Supplier Quality at group level. Interpretations given in writing by the Head of Supplier Quality apply to all suppliers in the same category.

## 2. Definitions and abbreviations

2.1 How to use this glossary

The terms below have the meaning given here wherever they appear in this manual and in Nordhavn purchase orders. Where an industry term is used more widely than Nordhavn uses it, only the Nordhavn meaning applies. Terms are grouped by topic so that related ideas can be read together. A supplier that uses different names internally is free to do so, but submissions to Nordhavn must use the terms below.

## 3. Supplier selection and onboarding

3.1 Principles

Nordhavn chooses suppliers on evidence of capability, capacity, financial stability and willingness to work to the requirements in this manual. Price is considered only among suppliers that have passed the technical and commercial checks described here. A supplier is told at the start which steps apply to its category and how long each is expected to take. The target for the whole route, from the first questionnaire to the supplier's entry in the supplier system, is 14 weeks for category A and B, and 6 weeks for categories C to F.

3.2 Stages of selection

| Stage | Activity | Owner at Nordhavn | Target duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Introduction and confidentiality agreement | Purchasing | 1 week |
| 2 | Self-assessment questionnaire | Supplier Quality | 2 weeks |
| 3 | Capacity check | Purchasing and Logistics | 2 weeks |
| 4 | Financial check | Finance | 2 weeks |
| 5 | Site visit and process review | Supplier Quality | 3 weeks |
| 6 | Selection panel decision | Purchasing, Supplier Quality, Engineering | 1 week |
| 7 | Set-up in the supplier system and portal training | Purchasing | 3 weeks |

Stages 3 and 4 may run at the same time. Stage 5 is normally skipped for categories C and F when the supplier already holds a current certificate of its quality system and a satisfactory result from the capacity check.

3.3 Self-assessment questionnaire

The questionnaire is sent through the portal and has 96 questions in 8 sections: organisation and ownership, quality management, product planning, production control, measurement, logistics and traceability, people and training, and environment and workplace safety. The supplier answers each question with a score of 0 to 4 and attaches evidence for every answer above 2. The supplier's managing director or plant manager signs the questionnaire to confirm that it is complete and accurate.

Nordhavn scores each section on a maximum of 100 points and weights the sections as follows.

| Section | Weight |
|---|---|
| Quality management | 20% |
| Product planning | 15% |
| Production control | 20% |
| Measurement | 15% |
| Logistics and traceability | 10% |
| People and training | 10% |
| Environment and workplace safety | 5% |
| Organisation and ownership | 5% |

A weighted total of 80 or more allows the supplier to continue without conditions. A total from 65 to 79 allows it to continue if the supplier agrees a written improvement plan with milestones inside 90 days. A total below 65, or a score below 50 in quality management or production control, ends the route. The supplier may reapply after 6 months.

3.4 Capacity check

Nordhavn compares the supplier's demonstrated capacity with the forecast demand for the part family, plus a reserve. The supplier provides, for each relevant machine group, the number of machines, shifts per day, planned working days per year, cycle time, and the efficiency it has achieved in the last 12 months. Nordhavn calculates available capacity and compares it with peak demand.

The standard requirement is that available capacity is at least 125 percent of the peak weekly forecast, where the forecast is taken from the highest 4-week average in the programme plan. For category A parts the requirement is 140 percent. The supplier must also show how it would add capacity if the programme plan were to rise by 20 percent, and how many weeks that would take.

| Item | Category A | Categories B and C | Categories D to F |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minimum capacity against peak weekly forecast | 140% | 125% | 110% |
| Maximum planned machine utilisation | 80% | 85% | 90% |
| Time to add 20% capacity | 12 weeks or less | 16 weeks or less | 20 weeks or less |
| Days of finished stock held for Nordhavn | 5 | 3 | 2 |
| Single-machine dependency | Backup route needed | Backup route needed for the bottleneck | Not assessed |

Where a part depends on a single tool or a single machine, the supplier must show a recovery plan with the number of days needed to resume output from a standby tool or from a second site.

3.5 Financial check

Nordhavn Finance reviews the supplier's last 3 annual accounts, or fewer for a young company, together with the most recent interim figures. Where the supplier is part of a group, accounts for both the entity and the group are required. Finance also obtains an independent credit rating on a scale of 1 (strongest) to 10.

The measures looked at, and the usual thresholds, are shown below.

| Measure | Usual threshold | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Equity ratio | At least 25% | Lower values need a parent guarantee |
| Current ratio | At least 1.1 | Current assets divided by current liabilities |
| Operating margin, 3-year average | At least 3% | Negative result in any one year is discussed |
| Share of revenue from Nordhavn | No more than 30% | Higher values are discussed with Purchasing |
| Independent credit rating | 6 or better | Rating of 7 or worse leads to a closer review |
| Net debt to earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation | No more than 3.5 | Based on the last full year |

A supplier that does not meet a threshold is not excluded automatically. Finance writes a short assessment and may propose a safeguard, such as a payment arrangement, a parent guarantee or a second source. Financial information is kept confidential and is seen only by Finance, the head of Purchasing and the panel members.

3.6 Site visits

A site visit is made by a team of two or three people: a Supplier Quality Engineer, a representative of Purchasing and, for category A, an engineer from the Nordhavn product group. The visit normally lasts 2 days. The supplier receives the agenda at least 10 working days in advance, together with the list of documents and people it should have ready.

The team follows the production route for a part similar to the one to be supplied, from incoming goods to dispatch. It reviews the control of the process, calibration and storage of gauges, handling of nonconforming material, labelling, cleanliness, housekeeping and the safety of people working at the line. It also talks with operators, to see whether instructions are understood and used.

Each observation is scored on the same 0 to 4 scale as the questionnaire. The visit report gives a score for each of the 8 sections and highlights any difference between what the supplier stated in the questionnaire and what the team saw. A difference of more than 15 points in any section is discussed with the supplier's management before the panel meets.

3.7 Selection panel and set-up

The panel looks at the questionnaire result, capacity check, financial assessment, site visit findings and the supplier's quotation. It records the reasons for its decision in writing and tells the supplier within 5 working days. The supplier is then entered in the supplier system under its category, receives a unique supplier number, and is given access to the portal. Two named people from the supplier, one for quality and one for logistics, attend a half-day portal training given by Nordhavn.

3.8 Supplier obligations during onboarding

The supplier must give truthful and complete answers, host the visit without restriction on areas relevant to the Nordhavn product, name a quality contact who speaks English, and meet the milestones in any improvement plan it has agreed. Nordhavn will not pay for the supplier's time or travel during selection, unless the buyer has agreed this in writing before the stage begins.

## 4. Advanced product quality planning and launch

This section sets out how suppliers to Nordhavn Trucks AB plan, prove and launch production parts for our truck and van programs. It applies to all suppliers of production parts, assemblies and sub-assemblies delivered to the plants in Södertälje, Gothenburg and Ostrava.

**1.1 Scope and principles**

Every supplier must run a cross-functional launch team for each new part number. The team must include, as a minimum, quality, manufacturing engineering, logistics, purchasing and a named launch manager. The launch manager is the single point of contact for the Nordhavn Supplier Quality Engineer (SQE) and must be reachable during plant working hours throughout the launch period.

The launch plan must be built backwards from the Nordhavn Start of Production date (SOP). Nordhavn publishes the SOP and the build-volume ramp for each program. The supplier plan must show every gateway date, the owner of each deliverable and the time buffer to the next gateway. A buffer of less than 10 working days between the last validation activity and the run-at-rate event is treated as a planning risk and must be discussed with the SQE at the next gateway review.

**1.2 Phases and gateways**

Nordhavn uses six phases separated by five gateways. The supplier must hold an internal review before each gateway and send the gateway package to the SQE at least 7 calendar days before the gateway meeting.

| Phase | Name | Gateway at phase end | Typical timing before SOP | Main deliverables |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Concept and nomination | G1 | SOP minus 36 to 30 months | Feasibility study, preliminary risk list, target cost confirmation |
| 1 | Planning | G2 | SOP minus 30 to 24 months | Launch plan, preliminary process flow, preliminary list of special characteristics |
| 2 | Product and process design | G3 | SOP minus 24 to 14 months | Process FMEA, draft control plan, gauge plan, tooling order evidence |
| 3 | Validation | G4 | SOP minus 14 to 6 months | Initial sample parts, capability studies, run-at-rate, packaging trial |
| 4 | Launch and ramp-up | G5 | SOP minus 6 months to SOP plus 3 months | Safe launch execution, ramp-up volumes, logistics proof |
| 5 | Stabilization | Closure review | SOP plus 3 to SOP plus 9 months | Exit from launch containment, lessons learned |

At each gateway the SQE records one of three outcomes: green (continue), yellow (continue with a time-bound action list) or red (stop and re-plan). A yellow outcome requires an action list with owners and due dates, and the supplier must review that list weekly with the SQE until every item is closed. A red outcome stops further tooling and capacity spending that is charged to the program until a repeat gateway meeting has taken place.

**1.3 Gateway entry criteria**

The following criteria are used by the SQE when reviewing a gateway package. They are minimum criteria and the SQE may add program-specific items in writing before the gateway.

1.3.1 At G3, the process flow must cover every operation from incoming material to shipping, and every special characteristic must be linked to at least one process step and one control method. The process FMEA must have an action plan for every failure mode with a severity of 9 or 10 and for every failure mode with a severity of 7 or 8 combined with an occurrence of 4 or higher.

1.3.2 At G4, at least 90 percent of production tooling must be on site and the remaining tooling must have a confirmed delivery date no later than 4 weeks before the run-at-rate event. Measurement systems used for special characteristics must have completed their studies before parts are measured for capability.

1.3.3 At G5, the supplier must show that the run-at-rate result met the targets in section 1.4, that packaging has been tested through a complete delivery loop to the receiving plant, and that trained operators are available on every shift planned for the ramp.

**1.4 Run-at-rate**

The run-at-rate event proves that the production process can make good parts at the quoted rate using production tooling, production gauges, production operators and material from the planned sub-suppliers. It is not a laboratory exercise and it must not use hand-finished parts or special staffing.

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Minimum duration | 8 continuous hours or 300 parts, whichever takes longer |
| Target rate | 100 percent of the quoted hourly rate for the peak week of the Nordhavn ramp plan |
| Minimum accepted rate | 95 percent of target rate, measured as good parts divided by planned parts |
| First-time-through yield | 97.0 percent or higher at the final inspection |
| Overall equipment effectiveness at bottleneck | 85 percent or higher during the run |
| Scrap and rework | Recorded by cause code; rework above 3.0 percent of parts needs an action plan |
| Cycle time evidence | Time study for each operation, showing the slowest operation at 90 percent or less of takt time |
| Witness | SQE or a delegate may attend in person; the supplier must provide a live data feed if attendance is not possible |

If the supplier makes the same part number on several lines, each line that is planned for Nordhavn volume must complete its own run-at-rate. A line that supplies less than 20 percent of the planned volume may be excused from a full-length run if the SQE agrees to a shorter run of 4 hours, but the targets in the table remain the same.

When the run does not reach the targets, the supplier must analyze the loss by cause, correct the process and repeat the run. The repeat must start from a clean line, with a new shift handover, and must not reuse data from the first run.

**1.5 Launch containment**

Launch containment, called Safe Launch at Nordhavn, is an additional layer of control that applies from the first production delivery. Its aim is to find process weaknesses before they reach the assembly line. Safe Launch is carried out by the supplier at the supplier site and is separate from any inspection at the Nordhavn goods-receiving area.

| Level | Applies to | Controls | Minimum duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Safe Launch 1 | All parts in the first 3 production batches | 100 percent inspection of all special characteristics at a separate inspection point; first-piece and last-piece checks at every shift; daily data summary to the SQE | 3 batches or 4 weeks, whichever is longer |
| Safe Launch 2 | Parts that meet Safe Launch 1 exit criteria | Sampling at twice the normal control plan frequency; weekly data summary to the SQE; certified inspector on each shift | 12,000 parts or 90 calendar days, whichever is longer |
| Normal control | Parts that meet Safe Launch 2 exit criteria | Frequency of the control plan | Ongoing |

1.5.1 The exit from Safe Launch 1 requires 3 consecutive batches with no nonconformance on a special characteristic, a Cpk of 1.67 or higher for every special characteristic that is measured by variable data, and no open actions from the G5 review.

1.5.2 The exit from Safe Launch 2 requires 90 consecutive days with no Nordhavn line stoppage that is caused by the part, fewer than 50 parts per million rejected at the Nordhavn line, and no open customer complaint on the part number.

1.5.3 Safe Launch inspection records must be kept at the supplier and must be shown to the SQE on request. Records must carry the batch number, the inspector, the date and time, the gauge identification and the measured value for each variable characteristic.

1.5.4 Parts that have passed through Safe Launch inspection must be identified on the box label with the letters SL and the level number, so that the Nordhavn goods-receiving area can recognize them.

**1.6 Ramp-up performance measures**

During phases 4 and 5 the supplier must report these measures to the SQE every week: delivery performance against the call-off schedule (target 98 percent on time and in full), parts per million rejected at Nordhavn, number of open actions, premium freight events and the stock on hand expressed in days of Nordhavn demand. The minimum safety stock during ramp-up is 5 working days of peak demand unless the SQE and the Nordhavn logistics planner agree a different figure in writing.

## 5. Measurement systems and statistical process control

This section defines the requirements for measurement systems, capability studies and ongoing process monitoring. It applies to every characteristic that is listed on a supplier control plan for a Nordhavn part, with the strictest rules reserved for special characteristics.

**2.1 General requirements for measurement systems**

2.1.1 Every gauge, instrument and test fixture used to accept product must have a unique identification number, a calibration record and a named owner. The calibration interval must be set by risk and use. The default interval is 12 months for hand tools such as calipers and micrometers, 6 months for fixtures that are used on more than one shift, and 12 months for coordinate measuring machines with a verification check each week using a reference artefact.

2.1.2 Calibration must be traceable to a national standard through a laboratory with a documented scope of accreditation that covers the quantity, range and uncertainty used. The expanded calibration uncertainty must not exceed 10 percent of the tolerance of the characteristic that is measured.

2.1.3 The resolution of a gauge must be 10 percent or less of the tolerance. For a feature with a total tolerance of 0.20 mm the smallest displayed step must therefore be 0.02 mm or finer. A resolution of 5 percent or less is preferred for special characteristics.

2.1.4 Gauges that are found out of calibration at the time of recalibration require a documented look-back to the last good calibration. The look-back must list the part numbers and batches that were measured with the gauge in that period and must state the result of the impact assessment.

**2.2 Variable gauge studies**

A gauge repeatability and reproducibility study (gauge R&R) is required for every gauge that measures a special characteristic and for every gauge that is new to the control plan. The study must be done with production parts that cover the full process spread, not with parts that are chosen only from the middle of the tolerance.

| Study element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Study design | 10 parts, 3 appraisers, 3 trials (90 readings); 5 parts, 2 appraisers, 3 trials if only one appraiser is qualified |
| Method | Average and range or analysis of variance; analysis of variance is required where an interaction between part and appraiser is suspected |
| Denominator for percent | Tolerance for characteristics with a two-sided tolerance, total process variation for characteristics that are monitored by chart |
| Acceptable result | Gauge R&R of 10 percent or less |
| Conditional result | Above 10 percent and up to 30 percent, only for non-special characteristics and only with a written justification covering cost and risk |
| Not acceptable | Above 30 percent |
| Number of distinct categories | 5 or higher |
| Special characteristics | Gauge R&R of 10 percent or less on tolerance; no conditional result is permitted |

The study must be repeated when the gauge is repaired, when the method is changed, and at least every 24 months for gauges on special characteristics. It must be repeated sooner when the process capability result is influenced by measurement variation, which is the case when the observed Cpk is more than 0.20 lower than the Cpk that is expected from process data taken with a better instrument.

2.2.1 Bias, linearity and stability must be assessed for gauges that measure special characteristics. Bias must not exceed 5 percent of the tolerance at any reference point. Stability is shown with a control chart of a master part measured every shift for at least 25 shifts.

**2.3 Attribute gauges and visual inspection**

2.3.1 Go and no-go gauges must be verified against a master at the interval in the calibration plan, with a default of 3 months for gauges in daily use.

2.3.2 Attribute and visual inspection must be assessed with an agreement study using at least 50 samples, 3 appraisers and 2 trials. The samples must include at least 30 percent defective parts near the limit. The kappa value between each appraiser and the reference decision must be 0.90 or higher, and the effectiveness against the reference must be 90 percent or higher.

2.3.3 Boundary samples, in the form of physical or photographic limit samples, must be on every inspection station where appearance is judged. Limit samples must be replaced at least every 12 months or when they show wear.

**2.4 Capability targets**

Capability is shown with Ppk for the initial study and Cpk for ongoing monitoring. The targets are given by the classification of the characteristic.

| Characteristic class | Initial study Ppk | Ongoing Cpk | Initial study size |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC-A, safety and regulatory | 1.67 or higher | 1.67 or higher | 125 consecutive parts, or 25 subgroups of 5 |
| SC-B, function critical | 1.67 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 100 consecutive parts, or 20 subgroups of 5 |
| SC-C, fit and assembly | 1.33 or higher | 1.33 or higher | 100 consecutive parts, or 20 subgroups of 5 |
| Non-special | Not required unless in the control plan | 1.00 or higher where charted | As defined in the control plan |

2.4.1 Initial studies must be run on parts from the production process with production tooling and with one cavity or one fixture per study. For multi-cavity tools each cavity must be studied separately, and the weakest cavity result is the result that is reported.

2.4.2 Data must be tested for normality. Where the data are not normally distributed, the supplier must use a method that is suitable for the distribution, such as a transformation or a percentile method, and must state the method in the study report. The use of a Cpk formula that is built for normal data on skewed data is not permitted.

2.4.3 A process whose Cpk is between 1.00 and the target must have 100 percent inspection of the characteristic until the Cpk meets the target. A process whose Cpk is below 1.00 must have 100 percent inspection and a corrective action plan with a date for the return to target.

2.4.4 For characteristics that are measured only by attribute data, capability is shown by a run of 300 consecutive parts with no nonconformance for SC-C and 500 consecutive parts for SC-A and SC-B.

**2.5 Sampling and control charting**

2.5.1 Control charts are required for every SC-A and SC-B characteristic that is measured by variable data. For SC-C and non-special characteristics, charts are required where the Cpk is below 1.67 or where the control plan states it.

2.5.2 The default chart is an average and range chart with a subgroup of 5 consecutive parts. For slow processes with fewer than 50 parts per shift, an individuals and moving range chart may be used, with a reading each hour.

| Characteristic class | Default sampling frequency | Subgroup or sample size | Chart type |
|---|---|---|---|
| SC-A | Every 30 minutes, and at start of each shift and after each tool change | 5 | Average and range |
| SC-B | Every hour | 5 | Average and range |
| SC-C | Every 2 hours | 3 to 5 | Average and range or individuals |
| Non-special | As defined in the control plan | As defined in the control plan | Not required |

2.5.3 Control limits must be calculated from at least 25 subgroups of stable data and must be recalculated only after a documented process improvement. Specification limits must never be drawn on an average chart as if they were control limits.

2.5.4 The following signals require action: one point beyond a control limit, 7 consecutive points on one side of the center line, 7 consecutive points rising or falling, and 2 of 3 points in the zone beyond two standard deviations on the same side. The reaction for each signal must follow the plan in the next section on special characteristics.

2.5.5 For processes with short runs, such as fewer than 300 parts per batch, the supplier may use a target chart that plots deviation from nominal. The method must be documented and used consistently.

**2.6 Data retention and access**

The supplier must keep gauge studies, capability studies and control charts for the life of the part plus 15 years. Data must be available to the SQE within 2 working days of a request, in a format that allows the raw values to be reviewed and not only the summary figures.

## 6. Special characteristics and control plans

This section defines how Nordhavn classifies special characteristics, how they are marked on drawings and models, and how they must be handled in supplier control plans, including the reaction plan when a control fails.

**3.1 Classification**

Nordhavn classifies a characteristic as special when its variation can affect vehicle safety, compliance with a legal requirement, a key vehicle function, or the ability to assemble the part at the Nordhavn line. The engineering group of Nordhavn assigns the classification and shows it on the drawing or the 3D model. Suppliers must also name any additional characteristics that their own process analysis shows to be critical, and must send the proposal to the SQE with the reason.

| Class | Name | Symbol on Nordhavn drawings | Supplier-internal equivalent | Typical examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SC-A | Safety and regulatory | Inverted triangle with letter A | Any symbol, but mapped in the control plan | Brake line burst pressure, steering joint torque, fuel line leak rate, seat-belt anchor strength |
| SC-B | Function critical | Diamond with letter B | Any symbol, but mapped in the control plan | Injector seat flatness, sealing surface roughness, sensor connector pin position |
| SC-C | Fit and assembly | Square with letter C | Any symbol, but mapped in the control plan | Bracket hole position, panel gap dimension, thread depth of a mounting boss |
| Standard | Not special | No symbol | Not applicable | All other dimensions and notes |

3.1.1 When a supplier uses its own symbols, the control plan must contain a key that links each supplier symbol to the Nordhavn class. Nordhavn symbols must appear on supplier drawings that are derived from Nordhavn drawings, without change of shape or letter.

3.1.2 A characteristic with more than one class is given the highest class. For example, a hole position that is both a function characteristic and a safety characteristic is SC-A.

**3.2 Marking on drawings and models**

3.2.1 On two-dimensional drawings, the symbol is placed directly beside the dimension or the geometric tolerance frame that it describes, at a height of 5 mm on an A3 sheet, and drawn with the same line weight as the dimension text. The symbol must not be placed in a general note unless the note applies to every feature of a stated type.

3.2.2 Each special characteristic must also be listed in the special characteristics table in the drawing title area. The table lists the item number, the symbol, the nominal value, the tolerance, the unit, and the required capability class. A drawing may carry at most 40 special characteristics in one table; larger lists are split across two sheets.

3.2.3 For three-dimensional models, the symbol is shown in the product and manufacturing information layer, attached to the feature, and is repeated in the exported two-dimensional view. If the two sources disagree, the supplier must ask the Nordhavn engineering contact for a ruling before producing parts and must not choose between them.

3.2.4 For process parameters, such as a tightening torque or a heat treatment depth, the symbol is shown on the process sheet in the same way as on a drawing and the letter is followed by the letter P, as in A-P or B-P.

| Marking element | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Symbol position | Beside the dimension or frame, to the right | 24.50 ±0.05 with inverted triangle A |
| Symbol size | 5 mm height on A3, 3.5 mm on A4 | As stated |
| Table in title area | Item, symbol, nominal, tolerance, unit, capability class | Item 14, B, 8.20 mm, ±0.03, mm, Ppk 1.67 |
| Process parameter | Letter followed by P | A-P for torque of 85 Nm, tolerance 6 Nm |
| Model-based data | Symbol attached to feature in the PMI layer | As stated |

**3.3 Linking the control plan**

3.3.1 Every special characteristic on the drawing must appear on the control plan, in the process flow and in the process FMEA, and must keep the same item number in all three. A control plan line is required for each characteristic and each operation that affects it.

3.3.2 The control plan must list, for each special characteristic: the symbol and class, the specification and tolerance, the measurement method and gauge identification, the sample size and frequency, the control method, the person responsible for the check, the record location and the reaction plan.

3.3.3 For SC-A characteristics, the control method must include at least one error-proofing device or an automatic 100 percent check where this is technically possible. The error-proofing device must be verified with a known bad part at the start of each shift, and the result must be recorded.

3.3.4 For SC-A characteristics, the process FMEA must show a detection rating of 3 or lower for the planned controls, or the supplier must add an additional control until that rating is reached.

**3.4 Control plan content by class**

| Element | SC-A | SC-B | SC-C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preferred control | Error-proofing or automatic 100 percent check | Automatic check or chart-based control | Chart or periodic check |
| Verification of control | Each shift with a known bad part | Every week with a master part | Every month |
| Record keeping | Value of each part for 15 years after the end of production | Chart and sample data for 15 years | Chart and sample data for 10 years |
| Operator training | Written test, repeated every 12 months | Written test, repeated every 24 months | Instruction on station |
| Traceability | Serial number or batch with date code and station | Batch with date code | Batch |

**3.5 Reaction plans**

A reaction plan states what the operator, the team leader and quality must do when a control signals a problem. It must be written in the language of the operator and displayed at the workplace. Each reaction plan must be tested by a drill at least once a year.

3.5.1 For every signal, the first action is the same: stop the process or the affected station, and protect the next operation from receiving suspect parts. For SC-A characteristics the operator must stop within 1 part of the signal. For SC-B the operator must stop within 5 parts, and for SC-C within one subgroup.

3.5.2 The team leader must be on the station within 10 minutes for SC-A and within 30 minutes for SC-B and SC-C. Quality must be informed within 30 minutes for SC-A and within 2 hours for the other classes.

3.5.3 Containment must reach back to the last check that gave a good result. All parts made since that check must be quarantined in a red-marked area, then sorted by 100 percent inspection or scrapped. Material at the following operations, in the warehouse and in transit must be traced and checked within 24 hours.

3.5.4 If suspect parts may already have been shipped, the supplier must inform the Nordhavn SQE within 24 hours with the part number, the batch numbers, the quantity shipped and the planned containment steps.

3.5.5 The process may restart only after the cause has been found or a temporary protection is in place, and after three consecutive good parts or subgroups have been measured and recorded.

| Trigger | Immediate action | Containment extent | Release condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single part outside specification, SC-A | Stop within 1 part; escalate to quality in 30 minutes | Back to last good check; all stock and shipped goods | Cause confirmed; 3 good parts; quality sign-off |
| Single part outside specification, SC-B | Stop within 5 parts | Back to last good check | Cause confirmed or temporary control; 3 good parts |
| Chart signal inside specification | Adjust or investigate; increase sample to every 15 minutes | Parts since last in-control point | 5 consecutive subgroups in control |
| Gauge failure or drift | Stop use of gauge; use backup gauge | Parts measured since last good calibration or check | Gauge verified with master |
| Capability below target | 100 percent inspection | All parts until capability meets the target | Study of 50 parts at the target value |

3.5.6 Where a reaction plan has been used more than 3 times in 30 days for the same characteristic, the supplier must start a structured problem-solving process and name a lead who reports to the SQE every week until the cause is removed.

## 7. Packaging, labelling and identification

## 6.1 Purpose and scope

This chapter sets out how Nordhavn Trucks AB ("Nordhavn") requires production and service parts to be packed, labelled and identified when delivered to its plants in Södertälje, Gothenburg and Östersund and to the Tallinn distribution centre. It applies to every supplier that ships series parts, spare parts or pre-series parts. The aim is that every container can be identified in two seconds at the line, handled safely by one operator, and received into the plant system without manual keying.

Where a part-specific packaging instruction exists, it takes precedence over the general requirements below for that part number. Suppliers must not substitute packaging materials or container types on their own initiative.

## 6.2 Returnable containers

Returnable packaging is the default for all parts with an annual volume above 2,000 pieces. Nordhavn owns the pool of standard containers and supplies them against a pool account held in the supplier's name.

### 6.2.1 Standard container range

| Code | Type | External size (L x W x H, mm) | Max gross mass | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NB-1210 | Folding plastic pallet box | 1200 x 1000 x 970 | 600 kg | Medium plastic and sheet-metal parts |
| NB-1208 | Half-size folding box | 1200 x 800 x 970 | 450 kg | Brackets, small assemblies |
| NB-6440 | Stackable plastic crate | 600 x 400 x 280 | 15 kg | Fasteners, clips, small electrical parts |

Containers must be filled only to the part quantity stated in the packaging instruction. Typical quantities are 24 pieces for the NB-6440 and 8 pieces for the NB-CV1. Overfilling that causes a lid not to close, or a stack to lean by more than 20 mm over its height, is treated as a packaging deviation at receipt.

### 6.2.2 Handling, stacking and pool management

Loaded NB-1210 boxes may be stacked three high in transport and four high in the Nordhavn warehouse. Racks may not be stacked unless the instruction explicitly shows it. Pallets must carry no loose overhang and must be strapped or wrapped so that the load stays in place during a 0.5 g braking test.

The supplier is responsible for the containers on its pool account from the date of dispatch of the empty container until it is booked back at a Nordhavn plant. Pool statements are issued on the first working day of each month. The supplier must reconcile the statement within five working days and report any difference greater than 20 containers or 2 percent of the account balance, whichever is lower. Containers that are damaged, contaminated or lost through the supplier's handling are charged at the replacement values in the container price list. As a guide, an NB-1210 is valued at SEK 2,150 and an NB-6440 at SEK 180.

Suppliers must not use Nordhavn containers for other customers, for outdoor storage, or as tooling or scrap bins. Containers must be returned empty, free of old labels, dunnage fragments and loose debris.

### 6.2.3 One-way packaging

One-way packaging is permitted only where the packaging instruction calls for it, for example for low-volume parts or for shipments from outside Europe. One-way cartons must be made of corrugated board with a minimum burst strength of 1,000 kPa, must be palletised on a 1200 x 800 mm pallet in good condition, and must not exceed a gross mass of 20 kg per carton for manual handling or 800 kg per pallet. The pallet height including goods must not exceed 1,200 mm unless otherwise stated.

## 6.3 Labels

Each container, and each pallet carrying more than one container, must have a label on the two short sides, at the position shown in the packaging instruction, between 400 and 1,200 mm above the floor so that it can be read and scanned during forklift handling. Labels must be printed on thermal transfer material, size A5 (210 x 148 mm), with black print on white background. Handwritten corrections are not allowed.

## 8. Logistics, delivery performance and scheduling

## 7.1 Purpose and scope

This chapter describes how Nordhavn communicates demand to suppliers, how deliveries are to be planned, and how delivery performance is measured. It applies to all suppliers of production material. Nordhavn assembles about 310 trucks and 95 vans per working day across its plants, and the line is sequenced up to four hours before build. A late or incomplete delivery therefore stops production quickly, and the requirements below are strict for that reason.

## 7.2 Electronic data interchange

All suppliers must exchange order and shipping data with Nordhavn electronically. Manual ordering by e-mail or telephone is accepted only during a documented outage of the electronic link.

### 7.2.1 Message types

| Message | Direction | Content | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivery forecast | Nordhavn to supplier | Weekly demand for 26 weeks, monthly for a further 6 months | Weekly, every Monday by 06:00 CET |
| Delivery schedule (call-off) | Nordhavn to supplier | Firm quantities and delivery dates for 10 working days | Daily, by 14:00 CET |
| Just-in-sequence call | Nordhavn to supplier | Vehicle sequence and part variant | Every 20 minutes during production |
| Advance shipping notice | Supplier to Nordhavn | Shipment contents, serial container numbers, departure time | At departure, no later than 60 minutes after |
| Receipt confirmation | Nordhavn to supplier | Booked quantities and any difference | Within 2 hours of unloading |
| Invoice | Supplier to Nordhavn | Invoice per delivery note | Within 1 working day of dispatch |

Suppliers must acknowledge every forecast and delivery schedule within four working hours. A supplier whose acknowledgement is missing more than three times in a calendar month will be contacted by its Nordhavn purchaser. The EDI link must be available 98.5 percent of the time measured per month, and an outage longer than two hours must be reported by telephone to the Nordhavn logistics desk on +46 8 555 0140 (fictional).

## 7.3 Forecasts and capacity

The forecast is planning information and does not itself oblige Nordhavn to buy. The delivery schedule is the binding document for the quantities and dates it contains.

Suppliers must hold the capacity to deliver the following relative to the most recent forecast for each period.

| Horizon | Capacity flexibility upwards | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Delivery schedule only | Firm quantity |
| Weeks 3 to 4 | +15 percent | Forecast |
| Weeks 5 to 8 | +10 percent | Forecast |
| Weeks 9 to 26 | +5 percent | Forecast |

Suppliers must confirm each month, by the third working day, that capacity covers the forecast peak. If the supplier expects to be unable to meet a forecast quantity, it must say so within two working days of receiving the forecast. Nordhavn will then agree the necessary steps with the supplier before the shortfall reaches the firm period.

Parts with a coverage of less than two days of production at the Nordhavn plant are classed as critical-stock parts. For these, the supplier must hold a safety stock equal to 3 working days of the average weekly demand, held in its own warehouse or at a nearby hub.

## 7.4 Delivery windows and transport

Every delivery is booked into a window at a named unloading point. Windows are 30 minutes long for just-in-sequence and line-feed parts, and 2 hours long for warehouse parts.

### 7.4.1 Window rules

| Delivery type | Window length | Permitted earliness | Permitted lateness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Just-in-sequence | 30 minutes | 0 minutes | 0 minutes |
| Line-feed, daily | 30 minutes | 30 minutes | 0 minutes |
| Warehouse, standard | 2 hours | 2 hours | 0 minutes |
| Warehouse, sea or rail freight | Calendar day | 1 day | 0 days |

A vehicle arriving outside its window may be refused or held until the next available slot. Vehicles must report at the gate at least 15 minutes before the start of the window to complete the check-in. Drivers must carry the delivery note printed or in electronic form, and the advance shipping notice must have been received before the vehicle arrives at the gate.

Loading must be planned so that the unloading sequence follows the stated order. A shipment may not combine more than 12 different part numbers per pallet position unless a mixed load is requested in the packaging instruction. Quantities must match the schedule exactly. Over-deliveries above 5 percent are returned at the supplier's expense, and shortages are handled under section 7.6.

## 7.5 Measuring delivery performance

Nordhavn measures each supplier monthly and publishes the result in the supplier portal by the fifth working day of the following month.

| Measure | Definition | Target | Escalation level |
|---|---|---|---|
| On-time-in-full | Deliveries within window and with exact quantity, divided by all deliveries | 98.0 percent or higher | Below 95.0 percent |
| Quantity accuracy | Booked quantity compared with notice quantity | 99.8 percent or higher | Below 99.0 percent |
| Notice accuracy | Advance notices matching physical content | 99.5 percent or higher | Below 98.0 percent |
| Stoppage minutes | Line minutes lost due to the supplier | 0 | Above 10 minutes in a month |
| Premium freight events | Number of events caused by the supplier | 0 | More than 2 in a month |

A supplier whose on-time-in-full result is below the escalation level for two consecutive months must present a recovery plan to Nordhavn logistics at the plant within ten working days. The plan must name an owner, list the root causes found, and set weekly measurable targets.

## 7.6 Shortages and premium freight

If the supplier recognises that it cannot deliver the scheduled quantity on time, it must inform the Nordhavn logistics desk without delay and no later than 12 hours before the start of the window. The call must state the part numbers, the quantity at risk, the earliest recovery date and the proposed transport.

Premium freight means any transport faster or more costly than the standard route booked for the delivery, for example express road freight, a dedicated vehicle, air freight or courier. Where the cause lies with the supplier, the supplier bears the full cost. This includes the cost of delay in the supplier's own production, late release of material by sub-suppliers, missing capacity and incorrect advance notices.

Premium freight must be agreed with Nordhavn logistics beforehand wherever time allows. The supplier will be invoiced for the actual cost of the transport plus an administration fee of SEK 1,200 per event. Where a line stoppage occurs, Nordhavn will charge SEK 640 per minute of stoppage for the first 60 minutes, and the cost of the stoppage thereafter as documented by the plant, up to the amounts permitted by the purchasing agreement.

Where the cause lies with Nordhavn, for example a schedule change inside the firm period, the cost is borne by Nordhavn after the supplier has documented that the standard route could not meet the new date.

## 7.7 Continuity of supply

Each supplier must maintain a written continuity plan for supply interruptions covering its main site, key sub-suppliers and transport routes. It must be able to restart delivery within 72 hours of a major interruption at the standard rate for the weeks 1 to 4 schedule. The plan is to be reviewed at least once every 12 months and shown to the Nordhavn logistics specialist on request.

## 9. Nonconforming product and problem solving

## 8.1 Purpose and scope

This chapter defines how Nordhavn reports nonconforming product to suppliers, what the supplier must do and by when, the format for analysis and corrective action, and how costs are recovered. It applies to production parts, spare parts and services delivered to any Nordhavn site, and to nonconformities found at Nordhavn, at a Nordhavn dealer or workshop, or at a vehicle owner.

## 8.2 Complaint handling

| Severity | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Safety or legal compliance risk, or vehicle immobilised | Brake line routed incorrectly; loose steering fastener |
| S2 | Line stoppage or vehicle cannot be completed or delivered | Wrong connector variant stopping a cab line |
| S3 | Functional or fit problem that is corrected by rework at Nordhavn | Bracket hole misplaced by 1.5 mm |
| S4 | Appearance or documentation issue without function effect | Scratch on a hidden surface; wrong label text |

The supplier must name a quality contact reachable during Nordhavn production hours (06:00 to 22:00 CET, Monday to Friday) and a deputy, and must keep these names current in the portal.

### 8.2.2 Return of parts

Nordhavn will retain defective samples for 30 days and will ship them to the supplier on request, in a container marked with the complaint number. Where the supplier asks for the parts, it must collect or arrange transport within 10 working days. Parts not collected may be scrapped by Nordhavn, and the supplier is then not entitled to dispute the nature of the finding on the basis of physical samples.

## 8.3 Response times

| Step | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge and name owner | 2 hours | 4 hours | 1 working day | 2 working days |
| Containment in place, with stock count at supplier, in transit and at Nordhavn | 24 hours | 24 hours | 2 working days | 5 working days |
| Interim report with cause hypothesis (D1 to D3 in 8D format) | 1 working day | 2 working days | 3 working days | 5 working days |
| Root cause and corrective actions (D4 to D6) | 5 working days | 10 working days | 15 working days | 20 working days |
| Full report with preventive actions and lessons learned (D7 to D8) | 15 working days | 25 working days | 30 working days | 40 working days |

## 8.4 Immediate containment

1. Check all stock at the supplier, in transit, in external warehouses and at sub-suppliers, and report the quantities found, split by good, nonconforming and not yet inspected.
2. Establish a safe supply of good parts, using sorted or reworked material marked with the green label described in section 6.6, and say when it will arrive at the Nordhavn plant.
3. Introduce an additional inspection of the failure feature at the end of the production process, with a written work instruction and a record of results. This inspection remains in force until the root cause is eliminated and the effect has been confirmed by at least five consecutive lots, or 30 production days, whichever is longer.
4. Support Nordhavn in identifying the affected vehicles by providing production dates, lot and serial number ranges within 24 hours for S1 and S2 cases.

Sorting performed at the Nordhavn plant is performed by a service provider selected by Nordhavn. The rate is SEK 410 per hour per person, with a minimum charge of two hours per event.

## 8.5 Problem solving in 8D format

### 8.5.1 Required content of each step

| Step | Content |
|---|---|
| D1 Team | Named team members with functions; a champion with authority over the process |
| D2 Problem description | Is and is-not analysis with quantity, lot, location and detection point; photographs; measured values against drawing limits |
| D3 Containment | Actions from section 8.4 with dates, owners and results |
| D4 Root cause | Occurrence cause and escape cause, each verified by test, trial or data; use of the five-why method or fault tree with evidence for every step |
| D5 Corrective action | Chosen actions for occurrence and escape, with expected effect |
| D6 Implementation and verification | Proof of effect, for example capability index of 1.67 or greater on the critical feature, or 100 percent test results over at least 5 lots |
| D7 Prevention | Spread of the action to similar parts, lines and sites; update of process control documents, risk analysis and work instructions |
| D8 Closing and recognition | Summary of lessons learned and sign-off by the supplier's quality manager |

A report that states "operator error" or "human error" as the root cause without an analysis of why the process allowed the error is not accepted. A reported root cause must explain both why the defect occurred and why it was not detected before shipment.

Nordhavn will give written feedback on each 8D report within five working days. Where the report is found insufficient, the supplier must send a revised version within five working days of the feedback.

## 8.6 Repeat problems and escalation

A problem is a repeat if the same failure mode occurs on the same part family within 12 months after the corrective action date stated in the report. The following escalation applies to suppliers with a repeat or a high count of complaints.

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Parts per million above 50 in a rolling three-month period | Quality improvement plan presented to the Nordhavn plant quality manager within 10 working days |
| Repeat of an S1 or S2 problem | Management review at the supplier's site within 15 working days, led by the supplier's site director |
| Three or more S2 or S1 complaints within six months | Review of the supplier's sourcing position by the Nordhavn purchasing council |

Parts per million is calculated as the number of nonconforming parts found at Nordhavn, in service or at dealers, divided by the number of parts delivered in the period, multiplied by one million.

## 8.7 Chargebacks

Nordhavn recovers the cost caused by supplier nonconformities. Costs are charged on the basis of documented time and material and, where listed below, at fixed rates.

| Cost item | Rate or basis |
|---|---|
| Administration fee per complaint | SEK 1,800 for S3 and S4; SEK 4,500 for S1 and S2 |
| Sorting and rework at Nordhavn | SEK 410 per person-hour, two-hour minimum |
| Line stoppage | SEK 640 per minute for the first 60 minutes, then documented plant cost |
| Part and material cost of scrapped parts | Purchase price plus 12 percent handling |
| Premium freight | Actual cost plus SEK 1,200 per event, as in section 7.6 |
| Vehicle repair at workshop or dealer | Documented labour and parts, at the labour rate invoiced to Nordhavn |
| Vehicle recall or field action | Documented cost, with an allocation based on the supplier's share of the cause |

The supplier will receive a cost statement with supporting data. It must raise any objection in writing within 20 working days of the statement, giving the reason and evidence. After this period the statement is taken as accepted and the amount will be deducted from the next payment.

Chargebacks are not a replacement for the supplier's duty to correct the problem and do not limit other rights Nordhavn holds under the purchasing agreement.

## 10. Warranty and field returns

6.1 Purpose and scope

This chapter sets out how Nordhavn Trucks AB (Nordhavn) handles parts that come back from the field and how the cost of those parts is shared with suppliers. It applies to every supplier of series parts, spare parts and accessories for Nordhavn trucks and vans built at the Gävle truck plant and the Örebro van plant. It covers warranty claims, goodwill repairs, campaign repairs and parts returned from dealers for any other technical reason.

The aim is simple: find the true cause of each failure quickly, return the cost to the party that caused it, and feed the result back into design and process control. Suppliers are expected to treat returned parts as a primary source of learning and not as a commercial dispute.

6.2 Definitions

- Field return: a part removed from a vehicle in service and sent to the Nordhavn Returns Centre in Södertälje.
- Warranty period: 24 months from the vehicle handover date for vans, and 36 months for trucks, with no mileage limit for the first 12 months. Powertrain parts carry 60 months or 500,000 km, whichever comes first.
- Supplier fault: a failure shown by analysis to come from the supplier's design, material, manufacturing process or packaging.
- No trouble found (NTF): a returned part that passes all defined tests and shows no fault in the analysis described in 6.4.
- Parts per million in service (PPM-S): the number of confirmed supplier-fault returns per million parts fitted, measured over the first 12 months in service.

6.3 Return parts flow and time limits

Dealers send removed parts to the Returns Centre within 10 working days of repair. The Returns Centre registers each part, attaches a tag with the vehicle identification number, repair date, fault code and mileage, and photographs it before any handling. Parts are then sorted by supplier and released for analysis.

The following time limits apply from the date the Returns Centre makes the part available to the supplier.

| Step | Responsible | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| Part registered and photographed | Nordhavn Returns Centre | 3 working days after receipt |
| Initial screening by Nordhavn Quality Engineering | Nordhavn | 5 working days |
| Supplier collects part or requests shipment | Supplier | 5 working days |
| Supplier analysis, first findings | Supplier | 10 working days |
| Full analysis with root cause (8D report) | Supplier | 30 calendar days |
| Joint review of disputed results | Nordhavn and supplier | 15 working days |

For parts with a safety or legal relevance flag, the first findings are due in 3 working days and the full analysis in 15 calendar days. Nordhavn marks these parts with a red tag on arrival.

6.4 No-trouble-found analysis

A part may only be classed as NTF after a defined sequence of checks. Suppliers must not declare NTF based on a visual check or a single functional test.

6.4.1 Minimum analysis sequence

1. Incoming inspection of the tag data and comparison with the fault code and repair note.
2. Visual and microscopic inspection, with photographs of connectors, seals, housings and surfaces.
3. Functional test at the specification end-of-line limits, followed by a test at the extremes of the specified temperature range (−40 °C and +85 °C for under-bonnet electronics unless the drawing states otherwise).
4. Vibration and thermal cycling of at least 48 hours for electrical and electronic parts, using the profile in the part specification. Mechanical parts follow the endurance test defined on the drawing.
5. Read-out of stored fault memory and operating hours for parts that have memory.
6. Where the above shows nothing, a destructive or cross-section analysis of at least one part per 10 parts of the same failure pattern, with a minimum of 2 parts.

6.4.2 NTF rate targets and follow-up

Nordhavn expects the NTF rate for each supplier to stay below 20 percent of analysed returns per quarter. Electronic control units are measured separately and the target is 30 percent, because intermittent faults are harder to reproduce. If a supplier exceeds its target for two consecutive quarters, Nordhavn and the supplier agree a test improvement plan with named owners and dates. Nordhavn may attend the supplier's analysis and may take back its own parts for independent analysis at the Södertälje laboratory.

Every NTF result must still list the tests carried out, the equipment used and the measured values. Nordhavn also compares NTF parts against vehicle data. If three or more NTF returns from the same part number carry the same fault code within 90 days, the case is treated as a suspected systematic fault and both parties work on it jointly, regardless of the NTF result.

6.5 Cost recovery

6.5.1 Principle

Nordhavn recovers warranty cost from the supplier when analysis confirms supplier fault or when the supplier does not supply an analysis within the time limits in 6.3. A part with a confirmed NTF result is not charged to the supplier. The cost of NTF parts is borne by Nordhavn, except where a systematic fault is later confirmed under 6.4.2, in which case earlier NTF parts of that pattern are reassessed.

6.5.2 Chargeable elements

| Cost element | Basis of charge | Example rate (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement part | Supplier's current price, delivered | Per price list |
| Dealer labour | Standard repair time × dealer labour rate | SEK 1,150 per hour, capped at 1.5 × standard time unless agreed |
| Diagnostic time | Actual up to 2 hours per case | SEK 1,150 per hour |
| Freight and handling of returned part | Actual cost | Typically SEK 180 to SEK 650 per part |
| Towing and recovery | Actual cost with proof | Up to SEK 9,500 per event |
| Administration fee | Fixed per claim | SEK 420 per claim |
| Analysis at Nordhavn laboratory | Hourly rate | SEK 1,350 per hour |

6.5.3 Cost sharing and caps

Where both parties contributed to the failure, cost is shared in the proportion set in the joint review. The default split when the cause is unclear but the supplier's part is confirmed defective is 100 percent supplier. Consequential damage to other vehicle parts is chargeable up to SEK 250,000 per vehicle. Costs above that amount are handled under the supply agreement and the supplier's insurance.

6.5.4 Campaigns

For a campaign, the supplier bears the cost of parts, labour, logistics and customer communication in proportion to its confirmed share. Nordhavn issues a cost statement with a breakdown by vehicle and dealer. The supplier has 20 working days to confirm or dispute each line item with evidence.

6.5.5 Invoicing and payment

Nordhavn issues a monthly debit note per supplier, listing each claim with vehicle number, repair date, analysis result and amount. Payment terms are 45 days net. Disputed lines are separated from undisputed lines, and the undisputed amount remains payable on the due date.

6.6 Return parts handling and retention

The Returns Centre keeps each part in a labelled bin for 90 days after the supplier's first findings are received. Suppliers must not modify, clean, disassemble or destroy a returned part before photographs are taken and, where Nordhavn has asked to witness the analysis, before Nordhavn has attended. If analysis requires destruction, the supplier must retain the remaining pieces.

After the full analysis, parts are kept as follows:

- Parts under dispute: 12 months from the joint review.
- Parts in a suspected systematic fault: 24 months, or longer if the investigation continues.
- All other parts: 90 days, then returned to the supplier or scrapped by Nordhavn at the supplier's cost of SEK 35 per kilogram.

Suppliers must pack parts for return in original or equivalent protective packaging, and parts from electrical systems must be shipped in antistatic bags with the tag attached.

6.7 Reporting

Nordhavn publishes a monthly warranty summary to each supplier through the supplier portal. It includes PPM-S, number of returns, NTF rate, average cost per claim and the ageing of returned parts awaiting analysis. The portfolio target is a supplier PPM-S of 50 or lower for mechanical parts and 120 or lower for electronic parts. Values above these targets feed the supplier scorecard described in Chapter 7.

## 11. Supplier performance scorecard and escalation

7.1 Purpose

Nordhavn measures every series supplier on the same set of indicators so that performance is visible, comparable and discussed regularly. The scorecard supports three decisions: which suppliers receive new business, which suppliers need help, and which suppliers must follow a stricter control plan. It is also the basis for supplier reviews and for the annual supplier recognition programme.

7.2 Scope and data sources

The scorecard applies to all suppliers with an annual purchase volume above SEK 2 million or any supplier of a part classed as safety-relevant. Data is drawn from the Nordhavn goods receiving system, the quality database, the logistics planning system and the warranty system. Scores are calculated monthly and consolidated per quarter. Suppliers can view their data on the supplier portal on the fifth working day of each month and have 10 working days to question any data point with evidence.

7.3 Key performance indicators

Five indicators make up the score. Each has a weight and a target.

| KPI | Definition | Target | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delivered quality (PPM) | Rejected parts per million delivered, measured at Nordhavn incoming inspection and on the assembly line | 25 PPM or lower | 30 % |
| Field quality (PPM-S) | Confirmed supplier-fault returns per million parts fitted in the first 12 months | 50 PPM-S or lower (mechanical), 120 or lower (electronic) | 20 % |
| Delivery reliability | Share of order lines delivered within a window of 1 day early to 0 days late | 98.5 % or higher | 25 % |
| Quality incidents | Number of line stoppages, sorting actions and containment actions caused by the supplier, weighted by severity | 0 per quarter | 15 % |
| Responsiveness | Share of requested analyses, action plans and data requests delivered on time | 95 % or higher | 10 % |

Severity weights for quality incidents are 1 for a sorting action of under 4 hours, 3 for a sorting action of 4 hours or more, 5 for a line stoppage under 1 hour, and 10 for a line stoppage of 1 hour or more or any vehicle held at the plant. Premium freight caused by the supplier is recorded for information and charged separately at actual cost plus 10 percent handling.

7.4 Scoring and rating

Each indicator is converted to a score from 0 to 100. A supplier that meets the target receives 100 points. Points fall linearly to 0 at the limit values: 250 PPM for delivered quality, 500 PPM-S for field quality, 90 percent for delivery reliability, 8 severity points per quarter for quality incidents, and 60 percent for responsiveness. The total score is the weighted sum.

| Rating | Total score | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| A | 90 to 100 | Preferred supplier |
| B | 75 to under 90 | Acceptable, routine monitoring |
| C | 60 to under 75 | Improvement needed, formal action plan |
| D | Under 60 | Unacceptable, escalation applies |

A supplier also moves to the next escalation level regardless of total score if it has any single indicator at its limit value in two consecutive quarters, or causes a vehicle to be held at the plant because of a part defect.

7.5 Levels of escalation

Escalation is a stepped process. Each level has a defined trigger, a defined set of actions and a named Nordhavn owner. The purpose is to give the supplier early warning and support before commercial measures apply.

| Level | Trigger | Main actions | Nordhavn owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 Normal | Rating A or B | Quarterly scorecard review by email | Supplier Quality Engineer |
| 1 Watch | Rating C for one quarter, or one serious incident | Written action plan within 15 working days, monthly review call | Supplier Quality Engineer |
| 2 Controlled | Rating C for two consecutive quarters, or rating D for one quarter | Site visit within 20 working days, extra inspection at the supplier before shipment, weekly review | Supplier Quality Manager |
| 3 Intensive | Rating D for two consecutive quarters, or repeated line stoppages (two or more in 90 days) | Nordhavn resident engineer at the supplier, containment at the supplier's cost, executive meeting | Director Purchasing and Director Quality |
| 4 Executive | No measurable improvement after 6 months at level 3 | Decision on sourcing strategy, possible resourcing of parts to an alternative supplier | Vice President Purchasing |

7.5.1 Costs at each level

Additional inspection, sorting and resident engineer time at levels 2 and 3 are charged to the supplier. Nordhavn uses a rate of SEK 620 per hour for sorting and inspection staff and SEK 1,450 per hour for engineering support. A resident engineer is charged for a minimum of 8 weeks.

7.5.2 Meetings and records

At levels 1 to 3, Nordhavn records the agreed actions, owners and due dates in the supplier portal after each meeting. The supplier's plant manager must attend the level 2 and level 3 meetings. At level 4 the supplier's chief executive attends.

7.6 New business hold

New business hold means that Nordhavn does not nominate the supplier for new parts, new vehicle programmes or additional volume until the hold is lifted.

7.6.1 Triggers

A new business hold starts when a supplier:

- reaches escalation level 3, or
- stays at level 2 for more than 6 months, or
- is the cause of a safety-relevant field issue that leads to a recall action, or
- has a total score below 50 in any single quarter.

7.6.2 Scope of the hold

The hold applies to all Nordhavn plants and all product lines of the supplier's group, unless Nordhavn names a narrower scope in writing. Parts already nominated continue to be supplied under their existing terms. Quotation requests may be answered by the supplier, but Nordhavn will not award the business during the hold.

7.6.3 Conditions to lift the hold

The hold is lifted when the supplier meets all of the following:

1. Total score of 80 or higher for three consecutive months.
2. No quality incident with a severity weight of 5 or higher for 6 months.
3. Completion of every action in the agreed recovery plan, verified at an on-site audit by Nordhavn.
4. A satisfactory result in a process audit of the affected production line, with a score of at least 85 percent.

The Director Purchasing decides on lifting the hold after a recommendation from the Supplier Quality Manager. The minimum duration of any hold is 3 months.

7.7 Review cadence and recognition

Nordhavn holds a business review with each rated A supplier once a year and with each rated B supplier every second year. Suppliers at level 1 or higher are reviewed according to 7.5. Suppliers that keep an A rating for four consecutive quarters are listed as preferred suppliers and receive priority in sourcing decisions, early insight into upcoming programme plans, and a reduced incoming inspection plan with a sampling level reduced by about half.

## 12. Traceability, records retention and audits

8.1 Purpose

Nordhavn must be able to identify, within hours, which vehicles contain a given production lot of a part, and must be able to show the evidence that the part was made and checked as specified. This chapter defines the traceability level, the record retention periods and the audit programme that every supplier must keep in place.

8.2 Lot traceability

8.2.1 Lot definition

A production lot is the quantity of parts made on one machine or line, from one batch of input material, in one continuous setup, within one shift. A lot may not exceed 8 hours of production or 5,000 parts, whichever is smaller. For safety-relevant parts the maximum lot size is 1,000 parts or 4 hours of production.

8.2.2 Marking

Every part and every packaging unit carries a label or direct marking that shows, at minimum:

- Nordhavn part number and drawing index
- supplier code (a four-digit code assigned by Nordhavn)
- production date in the form YYMMDD and shift
- lot number
- for safety-relevant parts, a unique serial number in a two-dimensional code

The label format is a printed data matrix code with human-readable text of at least 3 mm character height. Where a label cannot survive the process, the part is marked by laser, stamp or moulded date wheel.

8.2.3 Traceability depth

Suppliers must link each lot to the input material batches, the process settings, the inspection results and the operator or machine identity. Traceability must also extend to sub-suppliers. Each supplier must hold the lot records of its own sub-suppliers for the same time as it holds its own, or have a contract that gives it access within the retrieval times in 8.5.

8.2.4 Traceability levels by part class

| Part class | Traceability level | Linked data |
|---|---|---|
| Safety-relevant (class S) | Serial number | Serial to lot, material batch, process data, final test result |
| Functional (class F) | Lot | Lot to material batch, process data, inspection result |
| Standard (class N) | Date code | Date and shift to material batch |

8.2.5 Mixing and quarantine

Parts from different lots must not be mixed in one packaging unit. If rework or sorting is carried out, the reworked parts receive a new lot number that is linked to the original lot and the rework record. Quarantined material must be kept in a marked area and must not be released without a signed decision from the supplier's quality manager.

8.3 Records retention

8.3.1 Retention periods

Retention periods run from the end of the calendar year in which the part was delivered, unless stated otherwise. The periods below are minimum values. Where the law or a customer of Nordhavn requires a longer period, the longer period applies.

| Record type | Retention period |
|---|---|
| Final inspection and end-of-line test results, class S parts | 20 years |
| Final inspection and end-of-line test results, class F parts | 15 years |
| Final inspection records, class N parts | 10 years |
| Process parameter records (for example torque, temperature, pressure) | Same as the test results for the part class |
| Material certificates and sub-supplier lot records | 15 years |
| Calibration records of measuring equipment | 10 years after the equipment is withdrawn from use |
| Qualification and first sample records | Life of the part plus 15 years |
| Packaging and shipping records, including delivery notes | 7 years |
| Training and qualification records of operators on class S processes | 10 years after the operator leaves the process |
| Audit reports and corrective action records | 10 years |
| Retained samples of class S parts | 3 years, 1 retained sample per lot |

For spare parts, the retention period starts at the end of the last calendar year in which the part was delivered, so that the retention period extends over the whole spare parts supply time of 15 years after the end of vehicle production.

8.3.2 Storage and format

Records may be kept on paper or in electronic form. Electronic records must be backed up at least daily, held in at least two physical locations at least 20 km apart, and readable throughout the retention period, including after software or hardware changes. Paper records must be stored in a dry, fire-resistant room with controlled access. Records must show the name or user identity of the person who created or altered them, with date and time.

8.3.3 End of retention and disposal

Records that reach the end of their retention period may be destroyed in a controlled way. Suppliers must keep a disposal log showing what was destroyed, when and by whom, for 5 years. If Nordhavn has told the supplier in writing that a legal dispute, recall or investigation is in progress, the supplier must suspend destruction of the affected records until Nordhavn releases them.

8.4 Containment scope and recall support

When a defect is found, the supplier must be able to define the affected lots precisely. The target is to limit the containment to the lots in which a failure can occur, with the first and last good lots identified by data. If the supplier cannot do this because the records are incomplete, Nordhavn will treat all lots in the period concerned as affected and recover the cost of the wider action under Chapter 6.

8.5 Retrieval times

The supplier must be able to deliver the records in these times from the date of Nordhavn's request, including weekends and public holidays for safety-relevant parts.

| Request type | Lot list and affected delivery dates | Full records for the lot |
|---|---|---|
| Safety-relevant (class S) | 4 hours | 24 hours |
| Functional (class F) | 1 working day | 3 working days |
| Standard (class N) | 3 working days | 10 working days |

Nordhavn tests traceability with unannounced exercises at least once a year for each class S supplier. In an exercise, Nordhavn names one finished part and the supplier must show all data back to the raw material and forward to the last delivery of the same lot. The pass criterion is a complete and consistent answer within the time in the table above. A recovery of 100 percent of the lot quantity must be shown, with a tolerance of 0.5 percent for parts scrapped and recorded.

8.6 Audit schedule

8.6.1 Audit types

Nordhavn carries out three kinds of audit: system audits of the management system, process audits of specific production lines, and product audits of finished parts. Suppliers must also carry out internal audits of the same kinds at least once a year.

8.6.2 Frequency

Audit frequency depends on the supplier's scorecard rating from Chapter 7 and on the part class supplied.

| Scorecard rating | System audit | Process audit | Product audit |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Every 3 years | Every 2 years per line supplying class S parts | Once a year |
| B | Every 2 years | Every year per line supplying class S parts | Twice a year |
| C | Every year | Every 6 months per line | Quarterly |
| D | Every 6 months | Every 3 months per line | Monthly |

New suppliers receive a system audit and a process audit before the first series delivery, and a further process audit within 6 months after start of series production. Nordhavn draws up the audit plan in November for the following year and shares it with each supplier by 15 December. Planned audits are announced at least 15 working days in advance. Traceability exercises and audits following a serious quality problem may be unannounced.

8.6.3 Audit duration and team

A system audit takes 2 to 3 days with two auditors. A process audit takes 1 day with one or two auditors. A product audit takes up to half a day. Nordhavn auditors need access to production areas, test laboratories, records and staff, and may take photographs of the process for audit purposes only. Nordhavn may bring a representative of its own customer or a regulator when asked to do so.

8.6.4 Scoring and findings

Process audits are scored against a checklist with a maximum of 100 points. A score of 85 or higher is required, with no individual element scored below 70. Findings are graded as major (a requirement is not met or a risk to the vehicle or the customer exists) or minor (a weakness that does not by itself lead to nonconforming parts). The supplier must send a containment plan for each major finding within 5 working days and a corrective action plan with owners and dates within 20 working days. Minor findings must have an action plan within 30 working days. Nordhavn checks effectiveness on site or by remote review within 90 days.

8.6.5 Supplier costs

The first scheduled system audit and process audit of each year are free of charge to the supplier. Further audits caused by the supplier's performance are charged at SEK 9,800 per auditor day, plus travel cost at actual rates.
